## Why this Act, and why scoped

**13 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the limits on
bargaining and the dismissal route: ss 18(2), 23(5)-(6), 30A and 35. The Court's
constitution, recognition, conciliation, tripartite mediation, awards generally, the
platform work Parts and offences are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A union cannot bargain over who gets retrenched

s 18(2)(d): a union may not propose anything about termination for redundancy "or the
criteria for such termination". Retrenchment **benefits** are open to bargaining (they
are not in the list), but **who** is retrenched is not. Promotion, transfers that cause
no detriment, hiring into vacancies and assigning duties within the contract are also
off the table. Asserted.

### 2. Getting the Minister to act ends any lawsuit

s 35(2): the Industrial Arbitration Court cannot hear a dismissal dispute unless it is
union victimisation under s 82. The route is s 35(3): representations to the Minister,
**through the union**, within **one month**. The Minister may order reinstatement with
back wages, or compensation (s 35(6)). That decision is "final and conclusive" (s 35(8)),
and a direction "shall operate as a bar to any action for damages by the employee in
any court" (s 35(9)). Asserted.

### 3. A collective agreement survives a takeover for at least 18 months

s 23(5)(a): a collective agreement runs for 2 to 3 years. s 23(6): on a transfer of
undertaking it binds the transferee for 18 months or until it expires, whichever is
**later**. Asserted.

### 4. Executives: one at a time, and only for disputes

s 30A: a union may represent an executive "individually, and not as a class", only for:
- reinstatement
- retrenchment-benefit disputes
- breach-of-contract disputes
- victimisation proceedings
- re-employment disputes

The employer may object only if the executive is in senior management. A collective
pay claim for executives is outside s 30A. Asserted.
