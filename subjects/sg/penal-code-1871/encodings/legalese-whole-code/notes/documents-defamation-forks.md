## Chapter 18 - forgery

**DD-1. s 464 illus (a), (c), (d): adding a cypher, or filling in a blank, is limb (a) or limb (b)?**
(a)(i) "makes ... part of a document ... with the intention of causing it to be believed that [it] was made ... by or by the authority of a person by whom or by whose authority he knows that it was not"; or (b) "alters a document ... in any material part ... after it has been made".
Taken: (a)(i), making part of the document, because the Illustrations speak of intending it to be believed that Z "so wrote" the letter (a), and of filling up (c), (d) - the language of limb (a)'s intention, not of alteration.
Answer changes? No: the ladder is an OR of the three limbs, and both readings make the document false.

**DD-2. s 463 "or to enter into any express or implied contract": who enters?**
(i) "to cause any person to ... enter into" (parallel with "to cause any person to part with property"); (ii) the forger himself entering.
Taken: (i). Licensed by s 464 illus (k): "he intended to deceive Z ... and thereby to induce Z to enter into an express or implied contract for service".
Answer changes? Only where the forger alone would contract; no Illustration puts that case.

**DD-3. s 471 "punished in the same manner as if he had forged such document": which forgery section?**
(i) chosen by what the document purports to be - s 467, else s 466, else s 465; (ii) also s 468 or s 469 where the forger's intent (cheating, harming reputation) is known.
Taken: (i). s 468 and s 469 turn on the FORGER's intention, which the user of the document need not share or know; the description is a fact about the document itself. The `Charge`'s `punishment` field carries the words "in the same manner as if he had forged such document or electronic record:" followed by the chosen section's words, and its `section` is "471".
**Open question for a domain expert:** does practice frame this as "punishable under section 471 read with section 465/466/467"? If so, `charge under s 471` should use `frame the charge read with` and the catalogue entry's section would change. Not verified - no source for charging practice is in this job's inputs.

**DD-4. s 467 "forges a document" - does it reach an electronic record?**
ss 466, 468, 469, 471 and 474's first limb all say "document or electronic record"; s 467 (and s 474's second limb) says only "document".
(i) The omission is meaningful (its neighbours name electronic records expressly; whether the drafting history confirms that is not in this job's inputs): s 467 needs a document; (ii) s 29(d)-(f) already makes many electronic things "documents", so nothing turns on it.
Taken: (i), as a leaf: `offence under s 467` requires `a document` TRUE. Where an electronic record is also a s 29 document, the investigator answers `a document` TRUE and s 467 is available. Tested: `a forged e-certificate` is s 465 and s 466 but not s 467.

**DD-5. "false instrument" (ss 473A, 473B, 473C) is not defined.**
s 31A defines "instrument" for Chapters 12 and 18; nothing defines "false".
(i) An instrument made false as s 464 describes a false document; (ii) the meaning of "false" in the English Forgery and Counterfeiting Act 1981 s 9, from which ss 473A-473C appear to be drawn (not in this job's inputs - unverified).
Taken: (i), in the @desc only. No ladder decides falsity of an instrument: the leaves are the accused's knowledge (s 473A) and intention (s 473B) about a false instrument.

**DD-6. s 474: a document of BOTH a s 466 and a s 467 description.**
Taken: s 467's punishment (15 years), the more specific and heavier limb. The Code does not say.
Answer changes? The punishment string only; `offence under s 474` is the same.

**DD-7. s 477 "any document which is or purports to be a will, or an authority to adopt a child, or any valuable security".**
Does "is or purports to be" govern "any valuable security"? Taken: yes - one leaf `is or purports to be a valuable security`. The alternative (a genuine valuable security only) would narrow the leaf's @desc, not the ladder.

**DD-8. s 477A second limb: "any such book ..." - does "such" carry the employer condition?**
(i) Yes: "such" points back to books etc. "which belong to or [are] in the possession of his employer, or [have] been received by him for or on behalf of his employer"; (ii) no: "such" points only to the kinds of record.
Taken: (i), so the employer condition applies to both limbs. Reading (ii) would make a servant's false entry in any record whatsoever an offence, which the section heading ("falsification of accounts") and the capacity element do not suggest.

**DD-9. s 477A "with intent to defraud" is not defined.**
(i) An atomic fault element, as the section and its Explanation 1 ("a general intent to defraud") treat it; (ii) read through s 25 "fraudulently" (intent to deceive, and an advantage or detriment by it).
Taken: (i), one leaf. Case law on the meaning is not in this job's inputs; a domain expert may want (ii) as a drill-down.

**DD-19. s 473 "any forgery which would be punishable under any section of this Chapter other than section 467".**
Chapter 18 now includes the currency sections, so a seal or plate for forging currency (s 489A) is within s 473 on the words. Taken: literal - the leaf's @desc says "a section of Chapter 18 other than s 467". s 489D is the specific offence for currency instruments; both may apply.

**DD-20. s 489A(2) "currency includes any currency note or coin ... which is legal tender".**
"includes" is not exhaustive, but `currency within section 489A(2)` answers FALSE for anything its limbs do not reach. Taken: accepted for the drill-down only; no offence ladder calls it (they read the flat leaf `currency or a bank note`, which the investigator answers). The same shape as `pc-general.l4`'s decision not to encode "property includes". A reviewer may prefer to drop the drill-down.

## Chapter 21 - defamation

**DD-10. s 499's Exceptions and the presumption of good faith.**
The Exceptions are in the defining section ("is said, except in the cases hereinafter excepted, to defame"), so notes/PLAN.md §5 puts them on the ladder.
The Explanation to the Exceptions: "good faith shall be presumed unless the contrary appears" (Second, Third, Fifth to Tenth).
(i) The leaf is `the want of good faith appears`, and good faith holds while it is FALSE; (ii) a positive `in good faith` leaf.
Taken: (i), because it is the presumption the Code states, and because the charge generator fills an unknown leaf with FALSE: under (i) an unknown good-faith leaf leaves good faith presumed, and still no Exception applies unless the investigator answers its OTHER leaf TRUE - so a charge is never refused on an unanswered question. Under (ii) the same unknown would silently defeat every good-faith Exception, contradicting the Code.
Who bears the burden of proving an Exception (the Evidence Act 1893, which is not in this job's inputs) is not encoded: the charge is refused when the investigator answers that an Exception applies, and framed otherwise.

**DD-10a. s 499 Explanation 1 ("It may amount to defamation to impute anything to a deceased person, if ...").**
"may amount" - (i) a sufficient alternative route once its two conditions hold; (ii) a necessary-but-not-sufficient permission, leaving the main limb's fault element still to be proved.
Taken: (i), an alternative limb: deceased, would harm if living, and intended to hurt the family. Explanations 2, 3 and 5 ("may amount") are read the same way, as widening leaves (their @desc).

**DD-10b. s 499 Explanation 4 limits "harm" - a leaf or a definition?**
Taken: a leaf beside the fault element, `the harm is harm to reputation within Explanation 4`, since whether an imputation lowers someone "in the estimation of others" is a question of fact. Tested: without it, no defamation.

## Chapter 22 - criminal intimidation, insult and annoyance

**DD-11. s 505 Exception: "has reasonable grounds for believing that such statement ... is true and makes ... it without any such intent as aforesaid".**
Taken: literal - the Exception needs BOTH reasonable grounds AND the absence of every (a)-(c) intent, so it can only rescue a "likely to" limb. Tested both ways.

**DD-12. s 503 "as the means of avoiding the execution of such threat" qualifies which intents?**
Taken, as the reference row did: the two inducement intents (to do / to omit), not the alarm intent - a person alarmed is not avoiding anything.

**DD-13. The charge text's quotation marks.**
The reference row's s 506 charge quotes the threat with curly quotes; notes/PLAN.md §1 and BRIEF.md require ASCII. Taken: straight quotes (`\"`). **Consequence:** the Chan Yok Tuang charge text differs from the reference row's byte for byte in those two characters, and nowhere else (asserted in the tests).

**DD-14. s 507: laid under s 507 alone, or "506 read with 507"? And is the imprisonment mandatory?**
"shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to 2 years, in addition to the punishment provided for the offence by section 506".
Taken: a charge under s 507 (its own `offence under s 507` over the same `Criminal Intimidation Facts`), and the imprisonment recorded as `shall be punished with` (mandatory), maximum 24 months, fine `not prescribed` (s 506's fine comes with s 506's punishment, which the words say is "in addition").
**Open question:** whether practice frames it as s 506 read with s 507. Not verified.

**DD-15. s 489E "punished in the same manner as if he abetted the counterfeiting of such currency within Singapore".**
The punishment is abetment's (ss 109-117, general-part's) applied to s 489A. Taken: the charge carries the section's words; no `Punishment` record is declared, because its figures are general-part's to compute.

**DD-16. s 506: one `offence under s 506` or two?**
notes/PLAN.md §2 asks for one per punishing section "or subsection that punishes differently". s 506 has no subsections; its second limb changes the punishment, not the elements. Taken: the reference row's reading - one offence, two `Punishment` records, the choice made by `the threat falls within the aggravated limb of s 506`. The catalogue's existing s 506 entry is unchanged.

**DD-17. s 506 "an offence punishable with death or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to 7 years or more".**
Read with s 41 (a maximum of exactly 7 years counts). The helper `punishable with death or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to 7 years or more` answers it over a `Punishment` by calling pc-general's s 41 helper, which counts imprisonment for life as "upwards" (`pc-general.l4:937`) and refuses where imprisonment has no stated maximum.
So an offence punishable with imprisonment for life (and not death) counts as "7 years or more". The words ("death or ... imprisonment for a term which may extend to 7 years or more") could be read to exclude life imprisonment, which is not "a term which may extend to"; taken: included, as pc-general's s 41 reading does. Not exercised by any test.
