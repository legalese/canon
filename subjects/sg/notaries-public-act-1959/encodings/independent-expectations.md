# Independent expectations — Notaries Public Act 1959 (2020 RevEd)

Written from `BRIEF.md`, `../source/NPA1959.txt` and `../source/PROVENANCE.md` only, before any
`.l4`, `NOTES.md` or `encoding.json` was opened.

## Readings taken where the text is silent or ambiguous

- **R1 Date gate (brief).** Every rule about a day refuses for a day before 1 June 2007:
  31 May 2007 → refuse; 1 June 2007 → answered.
- **R2 "not less than 7 years" (s 3(2)).** Exactly 7 years of practice qualifies; 6 years and
  364 days (or 6.99 years) does not. Both limbs needed: *is* a practising advocate and solicitor in
  Singapore *and* has so practised ≥ 7 years. Someone with 20 years' past practice who is not now
  practising does not qualify.
- **R3 "not exceeding 12 months" (s 3(1), s 3(6)).** 12 months is allowed, 13 months (or 12 months
  and a day) is not.
- **R4 Temporary appointments (s 3(5)) and the 7-year rule — ambiguous.** s 3(5) names its own
  qualification ("any person, being a practising advocate and solicitor in Singapore"); s 3(2)
  says "No person shall be appointed as a notary public unless ... not less than 7 years". I expect
  the specific s 3(5) qualification to govern: a practising advocate and solicitor of, say, 2 years
  may be appointed temporarily. Genuinely ambiguous; the other reading applies s 3(2) to both.
- **R5 s 3(3) and s 3(4) apply to "any appointment under this section"**, temporary included:
  consultation with the Law Society Council is required for a temporary appointment too.
- **R6 "absent ... for a period exceeding one month" (s 3(5)).** Exactly one month does not
  qualify; one month and a day (or 2 months) does. The Senate "may" — discretionary.
- **R7 s 3(6).** A temporary appointment lapses on the absent notary's death or return to Singapore,
  and never has effect beyond 12 months.
- **R8 s 2 "notary public".** Excludes a person whose appointment was revoked under s 5. The text
  does not mention s 6 revocations or expiry of the term; I expect "the context otherwise
  requires" — a person revoked under s 6, or whose term has expired, is not a notary public.
  Ambiguous on the words.
- **R9 s 4(2)/(3).** For an affidavit/statutory declaration to be used in a court or place *within*
  Singapore, a notary may administer the oath / take or attest only where it is executed (i) to
  confirm or prove due execution of a document, or (ii) by a master or crew member of a vessel
  about a matter concerning the vessel. For use *outside* Singapore (iii), always. Any other
  English-notary power (s 4(1)) and any prescribed power (s 4(3)(c)) is not in the source → refuse.
- **R10 s 5 mandatory.** If it appears to the Senate that (a) the notary has become bankrupt or
  made an arrangement with creditors, (b) has been struck off, or (c) has been found guilty of
  misconduct that in the Senate's opinion renders him unfit — the Senate *shall* revoke and
  publish in the Gazette. Misconduct found, but not in the Senate's opinion rendering him unfit →
  no s 5 duty.
- **R11 s 6 discretionary** — on the notary's request the Senate *may* revoke (by Gazette
  notification). No request → no s 6 power.
- **R12 s 7.** Exercising within Singapore any notarial function otherwise than in accordance with
  the Act is an offence; on conviction before a District Judge, fine not exceeding $10,000. The
  $10,000 is a ceiling: a fine of 10,000 is within it, 10,001 is not. Exercise outside Singapore
  is not caught by s 7.
- **R13 s 8(2).** Rules come into force on the date of publication in the Gazette, or such later
  date as specified in the rules.
