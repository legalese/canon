# Credit Bureau Act 2016 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
conventions and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation (version in force from
9 March 2025), with amendments to Act 5 of 2025 shown.

**Checks:** one case file, 92 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**10 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a credit
bureau, a lender that uses one, and a person whose credit is reported actually meet:
who needs a licence and who may get one (ss 4, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12), what a bureau may
disclose (ss 13, 16), the data subject's copy of the credit report (s 17), correcting
data (ss 18, 19), approved members (ss 27, 29, 33, First Schedule), and the general
penalty and composition (ss 65, 66). Fees, notifications, audit, the other member
duties (ss 34 to 38), control of shareholders, inspections, the Authority's control
of a failing bureau, and appeal procedure are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. There can be only one corporate credit bureau

s 7(6): the Authority "must not grant" a licence for corporate credit reporting, or
for both kinds, "when another person holds a licence for that business". Consumer
licences have no such bar. Reading "that business" as corporate credit reporting
business is an inference; it is the reading encoded. Asserted.

### 2. Your consent overrides every protection in s 13

s 16 opens "Despite section 13": a bureau may disclose customer information or a
credit report to the data subject, or to a third party with the data subject's
written consent. Read literally, this reaches even what s 13(5) forbids outright
(deposit information, and information about public authorities, central banks and
sovereign wealth funds), though s 13(4) forbids collecting those in the first
place. Asserted, as a literal reading.

### 3. Your report is free only within 30 days of a credit decision

s 17: a bureau must give you your credit report within 5 business days and "may
charge" a fee. It must be free only where an individual applied for credit from an
approved member and asks "within 30 days after the approved member has approved or
rejected the application". You choose one of four forms; ordinary post is not one
of them (registered post is). Asserted.

### 4. Banks, merchant banks, finance companies and card issuers are in automatically; no one else is

First Schedule and s 29(1): those four are deemed approved members. A moneylender,
insurer or anyone else may receive customer information only if the Authority
approves it (s 27, s 29(6)). An approved member may request or use the information
only where "strictly necessary to assess the creditworthiness" of its customer, or
for a purpose the Authority permits by notice (s 33(1), (2)). Asserted.

### 5. The licence is by type, and the penalty is steep

s 6(1) requires a licence "in respect of that type of credit reporting business",
so a consumer licensee doing corporate reporting commits the offence. The maximum
is $125,000 or 3 years for an individual, $250,000 for anyone else, with daily
fines for continuing. Public authorities are outside the Act unless the Minister
orders otherwise (s 4). Asserted.

### 6. A bureau may lose its licence without a hearing on only three grounds

s 11(4), (5): a hearing is required before revocation or suspension except where
the bureau is being wound up, a receiver or judicial manager has been appointed,
or it has been convicted of an offence of fraud or dishonesty. Appeal to the
Minister is within 30 days (s 12). Asserted.

### 7. Late renewal costs more, not the licence

s 8: renewal must be applied for at least 6 months before expiry; a later
application is still accepted, with a late application fee. The fee amounts are
prescribed, not in the Act. Asserted.

### 8. Some offences have no stated penalty

ss 29(11), 30(9) and 31(10) make a bureau's failure to publish or update its list
of approved members an offence without a penalty; s 65 then gives $100,000 for a
non-individual. Composition is up to half the maximum fine, but only for offences
prescribed as compoundable (s 66). Asserted for ss 65, 66.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The regulations (fees, prescribed entities under s 13(6), prescribed circumstances
  under s 19(2), compoundable offences) were not retrieved.
- "Within a year" in s 18(3)(b)(ii) is taken as 365 days, and day 30 as within the
  30-day periods of ss 12 and 17(3); the Act does not count them.
- The Authority's written notices, which set most deadlines in ss 18, 21, 35, were
  not retrieved.
- No case law or MAS guidance was searched.
