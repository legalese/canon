## Why this Act, and why scoped

**17 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the rules a
broadcaster, an investor, a shopkeeper, an online platform or an internet access
provider actually meets: the licensing requirement and its penalty (ss 8, 46, Second
Schedule), satellite dishes (ss 20, 47, First Schedule), supporting proscribed foreign
services (s 30), decoders (ss 48, 48A), the board and control rules for broadcasting
companies (ss 32, 33, 35, 36, 41), foreign funding and ownership (ss 43, 44), and the
egregious-content offences (ss 45D to 45J). Not encoded: Ministerial directions,
Codes of Practice, licence modification and cancellation, public service and
must-carry obligations, frequency planning, declared foreign services, the Ministerial
approval criteria and directions (ss 37, 39, 40, 42), the regulated online
communication service regime (ss 45K to 45O), investigation powers and the
transitional provisions.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A dish needs a licence; a television does not

The First Schedule's only "broadcasting apparatus" is a "Television receive-only
satellite receiving system". Under s 20(1)(c) operating one on your own premises
without a licence is an offence carrying up to $40,000 or 3 years, with the dish
forfeited (s 47). Members of the SAF, Police, Vigilante Corps and SCDF working one on
duty are exempt (s 20(3)); a private security guard is not. Ordinary televisions and
terrestrial aerials are outside the Schedule. Asserted.

### 2. An online platform faces up to $1 million; an internet provider's fine is capped at $500,000

s 45E(2): a platform that fails to comply with a s 45H direction about egregious
content faces up to $1 million, plus $100,000 a day continuing after conviction. s
45F(2): the internet access provider that fails a s 45I blocking direction faces
$20,000 a day "but not exceeding a total fine of $500,000". Neither carries
imprisonment. And the blocking direction under s 45I stops access "to the online
communication service" — the whole service, not the content. Asserted.

### 3. Knowledge is presumed; the defence is the provider's to prove

If the content was accessible when the direction was given, knowledge "must be
presumed until the contrary is proved" (s 45E(4), 45F(4)). The s 45G defence (nothing
more was reasonably practicable and there was no better means) must be proved by the
accused on a balance of probabilities. A direction must not reach "communication
between 2 or more end-users that is of a private or domestic nature" (s 45H(3)).
Criticism of government policy and defamation of a private person are not in s
45D(1)(a) to (g); s 45D(1)(h) lets regulations add categories, and none were read.
Treating a call to defy a quarantine order as obstructing a "public health measure" is
an inference from the s 45D(3) definition. Asserted.

### 4. Becoming a hidden controller can mean prison; becoming a 12% shareholder cannot

Without the Minister's prior approval no one may become a substantial shareholder, a
party to an agreement to act together over more than 5% of the votes (s 35), a "12%
controller" counting associates, or an "indirect controller" (s 36). s 41(1) punishes
the shareholding breaches by fine only (up to $50,000 and $5,000 a day); s 41(2) adds
up to 3 years' imprisonment for becoming an indirect controller. Lack of awareness is a
defence only if the Minister is notified within 14 days and the directed action taken
(s 41(3)); otherwise "it is not a defence" that the contravention was unintended (s
41(5)). "Substantial shareholder" is defined by s 81 of the Companies Act 1967, which
was not read; it is an input here. Asserted.

### 5. A permanent resident is a "foreign source"

s 43(9)(c): "any person who is not a citizen of Singapore, whether or not he or she is
resident in Singapore". So is a Singapore company with a single non-citizen director
or member (para (d), garbled in the deposit and read as such). Receiving such a fund to
finance a broadcasting company's service needs the Authority's prior consent; an
unsolicited fund must be reported within 7 days (s 43(3)); listed-share dealings are
outside the section (s 43(7)). An employee paid from outside Singapore to run an item
or "a particular line or bias" is presumed to be paid by a foreign source and must
report it to the chief executive within 7 days (s 43(5), (6)). Asserted.

### 6. Foreign ownership of 49% bars the licence, and loss of it is uncompensated

s 44(1): unless the Minister approves, a company must not hold a relevant licence if
foreign sources together hold at least 49% of the shares or voting power, or direct a
majority of its management; the Authority "must" then cancel the licence "without any
compensation" (s 44(2)). A relevant licence is a free-to-air or subscription licence
reaching 50,000 dwelling-houses or more, not a class licence, or one the Minister
specifies (s 32(2)); its company's chief executive and at least half its directors must
be citizens unless the Authority approves otherwise (s 33(2)). Asserted.

### 7. Watching a proscribed foreign service is not a s 30 offence; advertising on it is

s 30(2) lists supplying equipment or programmes, inviting others to supply programmes,
advertising, publishing programme times, supplying decoders (displaying them in a shop
is deemed an offer, s 30(5)) and promoting the service. Merely receiving it is not on
the list. Providing a licensable service without a licence (s 46) carries up to
$200,000 or 3 years, plus $10,000 a day continuing; a delivery system that only
provides facilities is not itself licensable (Second Schedule). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Companies Act 1967 ss 7 and 81 (interests in shares, substantial shareholder) were not
  read; the 5% and 12% tests depend on them.
- No Part 10A regulations were read: s 45D(1)(h) categories and the compoundable
  offences under s 57 are unknown.
- The garbled s 43(9)(d) and the 2024 date on the Act 4 of 2008 annotation should be
  checked against the SSO HTML.
- The exact six-year boundary in s 48A(3) and "part of a day" in the daily fines are not
  modelled; days are whole numbers.
- No case law or Authority guidance was searched.
