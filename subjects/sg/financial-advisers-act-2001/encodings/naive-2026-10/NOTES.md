# Financial Advisers Act 2001 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation. The Revised Edition
renumbered the Act. The reasonable-basis rule, long cited as s 27, is s 36 here, and
the deposit carries the old numbers in brackets.

**Checks:** one case file, 32 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**15 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the conduct rules
a client can rely on: ss 35, 36, 39, 41 to 43 and 45. Licensing and exemptions,
representatives, MAS powers, and the notices and guidelines under s 74 (where most
conduct detail actually sits) are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. An unsuitable sale stands; the client's remedy is damages

s 39(1): a contravention of the Act "does not affect the validity or enforceability of
any agreement, transaction or arrangement". A client sold a product without a
reasonable basis keeps the product. The Act gives damages under s 36(3), and only if
the client relied reasonably **and** suffered loss. An unsuitable recommendation that
happened to make money gives no claim. Asserted.

### 2. "Reasonable basis" means knowing the client and the product

s 36(2): there is no reasonable basis unless the adviser considered the client's
"investment objectives, financial situation and particular needs", investigated the
product reasonably, **and** based the recommendation on that work. The rule applies to
implied recommendations too (s 36(4)). Asserted.

### 3. A careless misstatement is enough

s 35(1)(e): "knows or ought reasonably to have known". s 35(5): omitting a material
matter counts as a misstatement. Damages need reasonable reliance and loss (s 35(3)).
It is an offence even if no contract results (s 35(6)). Asserted.

### 4. Insurance rules

- **s 41(3), (4):** a lien on a premium account is void unless it is for the adviser's
  own unpaid fees, and any charge over the account is void.
- **s 42:** an adviser may place a Singapore risk only with a licensed insurer.
  Reinsurance, overseas risks and MAS-permitted placements are excepted.
- **s 43:** advising a client, with intent to deceive, to leave a material matter (such
  as a pre-existing condition) off a proposal form is an offence.

Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 45:** a circular recommending specified products must disclose the adviser's
  interests. Underwriters are deemed interested, there is a lack-of-knowledge defence,
  and a copy must be kept for 5 years.
