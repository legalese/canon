## Why this Act, and why scoped

**6 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what an
operator, crew member, witness or bystander actually meets: which occurrences the
Act reaches (s 5), the duty to report (s 12), when the TSIB must or may investigate
(ss 15, 16), draft and published reports (ss 19, 21), the limits of a compelled
medical examination (s 29(4)), the offences and their penalties, self-incrimination
and the identification-card defence (ss 37, 38(1)), and the composition cap (s 42).
The TSIB's constitution, investigators' entry, search and seizure powers, cost
recovery, reopening, corporate liability, service and the Part 8 amendments are not
encoded. Which occurrences are "immediately reportable", the reporting time, the
particulars and which offences are compoundable are all prescribed by regulations
that were not retrieved.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The Act can reach an occurrence that the TSIB may not investigate

s 5(3)(b)(iii) applies the Act to a marine occurrence abroad whenever "a witness to,
or a person having information ... arrives or is found at any place in Singapore".
But s 16(1) lists the occurrences that may be investigated, and a witness in
Singapore is not on that list: a foreign ship's casualty abroad, with no Singapore
ship, no evidence here, no foreign request and no treaty right, is within the Act
and still "must not be investigated". The reverse also happens: a derailment in
Singapore before the Minister's "relevant date" (s 5(4), (5)) is outside the Act but
meets s 16(1)(a). Asserted.

### 2. A Minister's order cannot override s 16

s 15(2)(b) says the Director "must" investigate when the Minister orders it, but
the whole of s 15 is "Subject to section 16". A foreign airliner crash abroad with
no Singapore link stays uninvestigable even with an order. Where an investigation is
ordered and allowed, the Director cannot discontinue it (s 15(4)). Asserted.

### 3. A draft report is barred even from a coroner's inquiry; a published one is not

s 21(1) makes a published report inadmissible in civil, criminal and "other
proceedings", and s 21(2) excepts a coroner's inquiry. s 21(3) bars a draft report
in the same words, with no such exception, and s 2 defines "civil proceedings" to
include a coroner's inquiry. Proceedings in Parliament are outside "other
proceedings" altogether, so s 21 does not reach them. Asserted.

### 4. Self-incrimination is no excuse, and only individuals get use immunity

s 37(1): no one is excused from answering on the ground that the answer might
incriminate them. s 37(2) gives use immunity only to "a person, who is an
individual", only if the claim is made before answering, and never in proceedings
for the falsity of the answer. A company's answers can be used against it. Refusal
carries up to $100,000 or 5 years (s 36(3)). But under s 38(1) it is no offence to
refuse an investigator who has not produced an identification card as s 24(3)
requires. Asserted.

### 5. Military occurrences are out unless a civilian element, an air show, or the Armed Forces bring them in

s 16(2) bars investigating an occurrence that involves only exempt (military, foreign
state, police and similar) vehicles and no civil transport facility. It is lifted
for a flying display with the site outside military premises, and by s 16(3) when
the Armed Forces ask the TSIB to investigate a Singapore military ship or aircraft.
How the "unless" clause in s 16(2)(c) attaches is a reading (an inference): it is
encoded as taking such an air show out of the bar altogether. Asserted.

### 6. The penalties are heavy, and the composition cap is always $5,000

Failing to report (s 12), failing to give a required report (s 13), adversely
affecting an investigation (s 33) and refusing an investigator (s 36): up to
$100,000 or 5 years. Copying or disclosing a draft report (s 19), hindering (s 34)
and disclosing restricted information (s 43): $20,000 or 12 months. Not responding
to a safety recommendation (s 22) and entering a cordoned-off area (s 35): $10,000 or
6 months. s 42 caps composition at the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000.
Since the smallest maximum fine in the Act is $10,000, the cap for any Act offence is
$5,000; only a regulation offence with a lower maximum could be compounded for less.
Asserted.

### 7. Smaller points

- A responsible person need not report if they know another responsible person
  has already reported "with all the particulars required" (s 12(3)); an ordinary
  reportable matter must be reported only on the Director's written notice
  (s 12(2)). Asserted.
- A compelled medical examination can include a breath test, toxicology, blood or
  urine, but not surgery or a drug injection (s 29(4)). Asserted.
- The draft-report offence (s 19) has no "reasonable excuse" defence, only the
  exceptions for preparing submissions and remedying safety issues. Asserted.
- s 16(1)(b)(iv) (a bus in Singapore with a Minister's order) adds nothing to
  s 16(1)(a), since such an occurrence already happens in Singapore. Not asserted
  separately.
- s 38(2) (not possessing the document) applies, in its words, to offences "under
  Part 4", but the refusal offences are in Part 5. Read literally it does not touch
  s 36; whether that is a drafting slip is an inference. It is not encoded.
- One annotation in s 43(5) reads "wef 10/01/2015" where the others read
  10/01/2025; it looks like a typo in the deposit (an inference).
