## Why this Act, and why scoped

**7 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
landowner, developer, motorist or park visitor meets: when cutting a tree or plant
needs the Commissioner's approval (ss 8, 14, 18, 27), releasing animals into nature
reserves (s 9(3)), the maximum penalties for the main offences, the vehicle owner's
liability (s 45), the due-diligence defence (s 46(2)), composition (s 51), appeals
(s 56) and the Government's immunity (s 3). Appointments, designation of areas and
the Schedule, the content of notices, planting-area specifications, enforcement
powers, cost recovery and the s 53-54 presumptions are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A tree planted under a s 27 notice can never be lawfully cut with approval

s 27(2): "A person must not, at any time, cut or damage any tree or plant which has
been planted pursuant to a notice". Unlike ss 8, 14 and 18, there is no approval
route; the only way out is the immediate-threat exception in s 27(4) (or a
reasonable excuse, s 27(3)). Approval is therefore no defence here, while it is
under s 14. Asserted.

### 2. Inside a national park there is no immediate-threat exception, and no girth threshold

s 8(1)(a) forbids cutting "any tree or plant or any part thereof" in a national park
or nature reserve without approval, whatever its size. ss 14(6), 18(4)(b) and 27(4)
excuse cutting a tree that is an immediate threat to life or property; s 8 does not
(only officers acting in duty are exempt, s 8(4)). s 38 lets the Commissioner act on
an imminently dangerous tree. Asserted.

### 3. The one-metre rule bites only on tree conservation areas and vacant land, and only above one metre

s 14(1) applies to a tree "with a girth exceeding one metre" on a tree conservation
area or on vacant land "whether within or outside a tree conservation area". A tree
of exactly one metre is outside it (encoded as strictly greater; girth is measured
"in the prescribed manner", s 14(4), not retrieved). In a heritage road green
buffer there is no girth threshold at all, but a tree the Commissioner determines to
be dying or dead needs no approval (s 18(4)(a)). A large tree in an occupied
garden outside any designated area is not restricted by these sections. Asserted.

### 4. Releasing animals: nature reserves only

s 9(3) restricts releasing or abandoning animals into a nature reserve, or into a
watercourse the person knows or ought to know flows into one, and letting animals
stray into one. It does not name national parks. Asserted.

### 5. Penalties

ss 8 and 9: $50,000 or 6 months or both, plus $500 a day for a continuing offence.
ss 14, 18 and 19: $50,000, and for ss 14 and 18 the court must have regard to any
financial benefit from the offence. ss 24, 26, 27: $30,000. s 43 (obstruction):
$10,000 or 6 months. s 44 (parking on a green verge or Board-managed turfed open
space): $10,000. s 46 (not complying with a notice or condition): $20,000 plus a
daily fine "not exceeding $200" — lower than the flat $500 elsewhere. Composition
(s 51) is capped at the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000, which is $5,000
for every offence encoded here. Asserted.

### 6. The owner answers for the vehicle unless the driver is named within 14 days

s 45: the owner is guilty as if the actual offender, unless the vehicle was stolen or
illegally taken, the owner truthfully names the driver in writing within 14 days of
the notice, or satisfies the court the driver could not have been ascertained. Once a
penalty is imposed on one person, none can be imposed on another (s 45(2)). Asserted.

### 7. Appeal within 14 days, but comply meanwhile; the Government cannot be prosecuted

s 56: appeal to the Minister within 14 days; the notice "must be complied with unless
otherwise ordered by the Minister"; the Minister's decision is final. s 3: the
Government is not liable to prosecution, but its contractors are not immune.
s 46(2) gives a defence to the person who proves "all due diligence" to comply.
Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Regulations were not retrieved: which offences are compoundable (s 51), how girth
  is measured (s 14(4)), and the application forms and fees (ss 12, 20).
- The Schedule (which areas are national parks, nature reserves, marine parks) and
  the Gazette orders designating tree conservation areas and heritage road green
  buffers were not read; the encoding takes the place as a given.
- "Damage" (s 2) is wide — poisoning, ringbarking, root compaction — and ss 14(2),
  18(2) catch damaging as well as cutting; only cutting is encoded.
- The s 54 presumptions (occupier, contractor, employer and qualified person presumed
  to have cut) and s 53(2) were not encoded.
- No case law was searched.
