## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its everyday-life relevance to ordinary people in Singapore: anyone
harassed, doxxed, stalked, impersonated or deepfaked online, or whose intimate
images are shared, meets it, as does anyone who runs a chat group or forum. An
automated count found no other deposited Act citing it by its slug title, which
is unsurprising for a 2025 Act and says nothing about its importance.

The Act runs to 111 sections and a Schedule. This row takes who may report to the
Commissioner (ss 4, 22, 25), when only a right of reply may be ordered (s 26(5), (6)),
selected definitions (ss 9, 11 to 16, 19(4), 20), the offences of non-compliance
(ss 69, 71 to 73), the Part 10 torts (ss 83 to 88), the administrator's duty to answer
an online harm notice (ss 89, 91, 92), enhanced damages (s 98), and ss 101, 102, 106.
Not encoded: the Commissioner and Appeal Panel, reconsideration and appeal (Parts 2
and 7), what each Part 5 direction and order requires (ss 29 to 47), information and
identity powers (Part 6), s 90, the remedies in ss 96, 97, 99, 100, the online service
provider provisions of Part 12 (s 94 reads in parallel to s 91, with a prescribed period
for prescribed providers), and the amendments to other Acts. Anything left to
regulations (prescribed connections, periods, damages limits, fees) is unknown here.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. False statements and reputational harm get only a right of reply — unless money is involved

s 26(6): for a "publication of statement harmful to reputation", only a right-of-reply
direction may be given. s 26(5): the same for a "publication of false material", unless
the falsehood says the victim endorsed, invested in or is associated with a trade,
investment, business, commercial activity or fundraising. So the Commissioner can take
down a fake celebrity endorsement of a scam, but not an ordinary lie about a person.
Asserted.

### 2. The person who did it can be sued for only six of the thirteen harms

Part 10 gives a tort against the wrongdoer for intimate image abuse, image-based child
abuse, online impersonation, inauthentic material abuse, online instigation of
disproportionate harm and incitement of violence (ss 83 to 88), and nothing else. There
is no Part 10 tort for online harassment, doxxing, stalking, private-information
disclosure, false material or reputational harm. An online harm notice to an
administrator or service provider, by contrast, covers harassment, doxxing and stalking
but not false material, reputation, private information, instigation or enmity
(ss 89(1), 93(1)). Asserted.

### 3. Belief in consent is a defence to intimate image abuse but not to child abuse images

s 83(3)(b) excuses a person with "reasonable grounds to believe that the victim
consented"; ss 85 and 86 have the same defence. s 84(3) offers only reasonable conduct
and lack of knowledge. A claim under s 84 requires the victim to have been below 16
"at the time the victim was depicted", so it can be brought in adulthood. Asserted.

### 4. Deepfakes count, cartoons do not, and a label can save you

s 13(2) includes images "altered or generated by any means" but excludes one "that no
reasonable person would believe depicts that person". s 16's inauthentic material must be
"realistic enough" to be believed, and illustration (b) says a prominent AI label can
defeat that. Asserted.

### 5. An administrator who leaves material up after notice is presumed at fault

s 91(6): if the victim proves a proper notice, harm at the administrator's location
involving online material, and that the material was not removed after a reasonable
time, failure to take reasonable steps "is presumed until the contrary is proved". The
only stated defence is non-receipt of the notice through no fault (s 91(7)). A frivolous
notice, or one materially false and known to be so, gives the administrator a claim back
(s 92). Asserted.

### 6. An appeal does not suspend a direction, and is no defence

ss 58(3) and 63(4): reconsideration and appeal do not suspend the direction unless the
Commissioner or Chairperson decides otherwise; s 73(1)(b) makes a pending appeal no
defence to non-compliance. The maximum for an individual is $20,000 and 12 months plus
$2,000 a day; for others $500,000 plus $50,000 a day (s 71(2)). A vexatious reporter can
be barred for up to 3 years with no appeal (s 25(4), (5)). Asserted (except ss 58(3) and
63(4), which are read, not encoded).

### 7. Calls to vote, protest or demand a Minister's resignation are outside "instigation"

ss 19(4) and 87(7) exclude statements tending to instigate voting, political activity,
calls for removal of an office holder or MP, and statements to them or public bodies on
public-interest matters. Asserted.
