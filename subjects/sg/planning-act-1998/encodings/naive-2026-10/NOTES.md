## Why this Act, and why scoped

**46 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it, most often s 5 (the
competent authority, 15 Acts) and s 21 (9). This row takes development control as
an owner, landlord or occupier meets it: what counts as development, the penalty
for doing it without permission, the owner's liability for a tenant, appeals,
and enforcement notices.

**Not encoded:** the Master Plan and conservation (Part 2), the application
procedure, qualified persons, information and entry powers, and debt recovery
beyond s 42(3).

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Letting a home for stays under three months is development

s 3(3)(d) and Fourth Schedule para 2: using a dwelling house "to provide
short-term accommodation, where the dwelling house or any part of it is occupied
by the same person for a period of **less than 3 consecutive months**" for rent is
development, and so needs planning permission. A stay of exactly three months is
outside it; a six-month lease is outside it; a free stay with a friend is outside
it. And s 3(3)(d) says this is so "whether such use commenced before, on or after
15 May 2017": there is no grandfathering. Asserted.

### 2. A landlord is presumed guilty of the tenant's short-term letting

s 12A(1): if a tenant carries out development without permission, "the owner of
the land is **taken to have permitted**" it, committing the s 12 offence (fine up
to $200,000). The owner's defence is proving all reasonable precautions, or that
due diligence could not have prevented it (s 12A(2)), or that it happened before
they owned the land (s 12A(3)). The owner can be convicted whether or not the
tenant is (s 12A(4)). Asserted.

### 3. Dormitory operators face prison on a first offence; short-term hosts do not

s 12(4)(a): a first offence is a fine only (up to $200,000). Imprisonment of up to
12 months is available only for a **repeat** offender (s 12(4)(b)), **except**
where the contravention "involves the use of any land or building to provide
dormitory accommodation" (s 12(6)), or demolition in a conservation area (s 12(5),
fine up to $500,000). "Dormitory accommodation" means 7 or more paying occupants
who are not one family with its domestic worker and carer (s 2). Both are Fourth
Schedule uses of a home; only one carries prison the first time. Asserted.

### 4. A permission lapses unless the works are completed in two years

s 20(1): a planning or conservation permission "lapses if the development or works
authorised by it are not **completed** or effected within 2 years". Not commenced:
completed. A half-built project at 25 months has no permission unless a condition
or an extension says otherwise. Asserted at 23 and 25 months.

### 5. Complying with an enforcement notice does not end it

s 28(8): compliance "does not discharge the notice". s 30(2): resuming the use, or
reinstating demolished works, after compliance is an offence "despite the earlier
compliance" ($200,000 or 12 months). s 30(2) is committed by "any person", so a
later owner who resumes the use is caught. The notice lasts until superseded by a
permission or withdrawn (s 28(7)). Asserted.

### 6. One place to challenge an enforcement notice, and as little as 14 days

s 28(9): validity cannot be questioned "in any court or proceedings whatsoever"
except by an appeal to the Minister under s 29, which must be made "before the
notice takes effect" -- as little as 14 days after service. Not as a defence in
the prosecution; not by judicial review. The Minister's decisions on planning
appeals (s 22(9)), called-in applications (s 21(4)) and enforcement appeals
(s 29(4)) are all final. Asserted.

### 7. Paying the civil penalty stops the prosecution of anyone

s 34: the competent authority may require a penalty, capped at the lower of 50
times the application fee or $150,000, as the price of a permission regularising
an offence. Once paid, "No further proceedings are to be instituted or taken
against **any person**" under s 12. The text does not confine the bar to the payer.
Asserted.

### 8. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 4(2), (4):** a lease of part of land is not a subdivision up to 7 years (14 in
  Third Schedule developments); options count towards the term; break clauses do
  not shorten it. This mirrors s 25 of the Registration of Deeds Act (see that row).
- **s 22(4) vs s 29(5):** a planning appeal does not suspend enforcement; an
  enforcement-notice appeal suspends the notice.
- **s 42(3):** a tenant who pays the owner's planning debt to avoid a sale may deduct
  it from rent and stay until repaid, unless a written agreement says otherwise.
