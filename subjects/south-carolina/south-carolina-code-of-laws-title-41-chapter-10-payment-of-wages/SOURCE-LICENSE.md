# Source license

The text of **South Carolina Code of Laws Title 41, Chapter 10 - Payment of Wages** is published on the [South Carolina General Assembly - South Carolina Code of Laws (South Carolina Legislative Council / Legislative Services Agency)](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/statmast.php).

- **Terms**: Open for the statutory text, by the register's own express permission. The Legislative Council's disclaimer at the head of the Code of Laws index grants copying of the Code text, numbering, history and the Effect of Amendment, Editor's and Code Commissioner's notes from the website without permission and without fee. There is no copyright notice anywhere on the Code pages. That express permission sits on top of the constitutional position: under the government edicts doctrine confirmed in Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020), the text, numbering and section catchlines of the South Carolina Code, being the work of legislators acting in their legislative capacity, are outside copyright altogether, so reuse and adaptation are lawful regardless. Two qualifications a human should note. First, the site-wide disclaimer at https://www.scstatehouse.gov/disclaimer.php says of the General Assembly website generally that 'This information is intended for personal, not commercial, uses.' - a broad website-wide statement that sits awkwardly with the Code-specific permission; the Code-specific and more particular permission should govern for the Code text, and in any event the doctrine means the text cannot be restricted by either statement. Second, the website version is expressly not the official version: the printed South Carolina Code of Laws Annotated is, and the annotations in that commercial edition (published by LexisNexis) are not part of what the permission covers and are not part of the enacted text. Treated as open: reuse and adaptation permitted.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
