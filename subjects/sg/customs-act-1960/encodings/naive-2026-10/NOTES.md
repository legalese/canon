## Why this Act, and why scoped

**40 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it, most often ss 3 and 4.
This row takes what a traveller or small importer meets at the border and after
a seizure. The levying machinery, warehousing, manufacture, drawback, licensing,
and search and arrest are not encoded. **No duty rates, duty-free allowances or
the fuel order were retrieved**; the fuel minimum is a parameter.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Giving away your duty-free allowance is an offence with a mandatory minimum fine

s 128J: "Any person who sells, exchanges or **gives away** ... to any person in
Singapore goods which are the person's duty-free allowances" commits an offence.
s 128L(7) makes it a "specified offence", so s 128L(2) applies: a fine of **not
less than** 10 times the duty evaded or $5,000, whichever is the lesser, up to the
greater of 20 times or $5,000, and up to 12 months. A bottle brought in duty-free
and given to a friend is within the text. Asserted.

### 2. The minimum fines are floors the court cannot go under

s 128L(2): on $40 of duty the floor is $400; on $1,000 it is $5,000. For tobacco the
floor is never below **$1,000**, so $10 of evaded duty on a pack of cigarettes
carries a $1,000 minimum. Over 2 kg of tobacco (s 128L(4)), the floor becomes 15
times the duty and the maximum term rises to 3 years. Asserted across six fixtures.

### 3. Knowledge is presumed, and so is possession

s 128L(6): in prosecutions under ss 128D to 128K the goods are "deemed to be ...
uncustomed or prohibited goods to the person's knowledge, unless the contrary is
proved". s 93(4): a traveller in the Green Channel with dutiable goods over the
allowance is presumed to possess uncustomed goods. s 115A(3): goods "found in any
vehicle" are presumed in the possession of "the **owner** of the vehicle **and** of
the person in charge". Lend your car to someone who smuggles, and you are presumed
to possess what they carried. s 115 puts the burden of proof on payment, lawful
import, exemption, origin and drawback on the defendant. Asserted.

### 4. Denying having goods that are then found is an offence in itself

s 128E: a person who "after he or she denies having, is found to have" dutiable or
prohibited goods commits a specified offence. The denial is the offence; no evasion
of duty is required. Asserted.

### 5. Seized goods are forfeited after a month unless you claim

s 124: if there is no prosecution, seized goods are "deemed to be forfeited at the
expiry of one month from the date of seizure unless a claim ... is made before that
date". On a claim the claimant carries the s 115 burden (s 124(6)). s 127: no costs
or damages follow a seizure, beyond return of the goods or their value, unless it
was made "without reasonable or probable cause". Asserted.

### 6. Refunds have five years; short-levy demands have five, or forever for fraud

s 19(3): a refund must be claimed within 5 years. s 20(1A), (1B): a demand for duty
short-levied must be made within 5 years, unless the shortfall came from the
payer's fraud or evasion, in which case there is no limit. Asserted.

### 7. The near-empty tank

s 136: a Singapore-registered vehicle leaving with less than the prescribed fuel
commits an offence (fine up to $500). Driving past the Customs gantry before the
departure bay is presumed to be an attempt to leave; the fuel gauge is evidence.
Permission given **after** being stopped lets the driver go but does not undo the
offence (s 136(3)). Asserted with a 0.75 minimum as a stand-in.
