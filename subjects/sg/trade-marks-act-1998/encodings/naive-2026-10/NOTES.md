## Why this Act, and why scoped

**23 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
registration gives a trader and what can go wrong: non-use, infringement, parallel
imports, threats and counterfeiting. Registration procedure and the grounds for
refusal are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Parallel imports are lawful

s 29(1): a mark is not infringed by goods "put on the market, whether in Singapore **or
outside Singapore**", by or with the proprietor's consent. Singapore applies
international exhaustion. The exception (s 29(2)) needs **both** a changed or impaired
condition **and** unfair dilution; repackaged genuine goods that do not dilute remain
lawful. Asserted.

### 2. Threatening a manufacturer or importer is safe; threatening a retailer is not

s 35(1): a person threatened with infringement proceedings may sue for a declaration,
an injunction and damages -- **except** where the threat concerns applying the mark to
goods, importing goods, or supplying services under the mark. A proprietor may write
to a manufacturer or importer with impunity. A retailer who only sells has the
remedy, unless the proprietor proves infringement. Mere notice of registration is not
a threat (s 35(5)); lawyers acting for clients are not liable (s 35(6)). Asserted.

### 3. An ® on an unregistered mark is an offence if you know

s 51(2): the word "registered" or "any other word or symbol importing a reference ...
to registration" is deemed a claim of **Singapore** registration, unless shown to refer
to a foreign registration that exists. Done knowingly, it is an offence (s 51(1),
$10,000). Asserted.

### 4. Counterfeit stock: $10,000 an item, capped at $100,000

s 49: importing, selling or possessing for trade goods with a falsely applied mark is
punishable by "$10,000 for each goods or thing ... (but not exceeding in the aggregate
$100,000) or ... 5 years". A careful seller escapes only by proving reasonable
precautions, no reason to suspect, **and** full disclosure of suppliers on demand -- or
innocence. Asserted.

### 5. Five years of non-use, and the three-month rule

s 22(1)(a), (b): a mark unused for 5 years without proper reasons can be revoked. s
22(3), (4): revived use saves it, but use begun in the 3 months before the revocation
application is disregarded unless preparations began before the proprietor knew of a
likely application. Asserted.

### 6. Infringement and the exceptions

s 27: an identical sign on identical goods infringes without proof of confusion; other
combinations need a likelihood of confusion; dissimilar goods only for marks well
known in Singapore, with damage. s 28: honest use of one's own name, descriptive use,
spare-parts indications, prior continuous use, fair comparative advertising,
**non-commercial use** and news reporting are not infringements. Asserted.
