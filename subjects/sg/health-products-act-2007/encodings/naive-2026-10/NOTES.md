## Why this Act, and why scoped

**13 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the offences a
seller or advertiser meets: supply, registration, adulterated and counterfeit
products, false advertising and the publisher's defence, active ingredients, and the
cosmetics carve-out. Manufacture and import licensing, wholesaling, registration
procedure, post-market duties, enforcement powers and the regulations are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Free samples and shelf displays are "supply"

s 2: "supply" means transferring possession "whether or not for reward", and includes
displaying the product as an invitation to treat. A free sample or a product on the
shelf counts. Asserted.

### 2. Cosmetics need no registration, but must not be adulterated

First Schedule: for cosmetic products, ss 12-15, 19(1)(b) and Parts 6 and 7 "do not
apply". So the s 15 offence of supplying an unregistered product does not reach
cosmetics, but s 16 (adulterated, counterfeit, tampered, unwholesome) does. Asserted.

### 3. The due-diligence defence has four limbs

s 16(3): the seller must prove all of:
- did not know
- had no reason to believe
- could not with reasonable diligence have found out
- took all reasonable precautions

Penalties are 3 years for adulterated, counterfeit or tampered products, and 2 for
unwholesome ones. Asserted.

### 4. A paid promoter has no publisher's defence

s 22: the defence for advertising offences is only for a person whose **business** is
publishing advertisements, who received the ad in the ordinary course, has **no
financial interest** in the product's supply, and did not know or suspect. A brand
owner, or an agency paid on sales, cannot use it. Asserted.

### 5. Possessing an active ingredient is presumed knowing

s 47(5): proof of possession raises a presumption that it was "knowingly" possessed,
unless the contrary is proved. Asserted.
