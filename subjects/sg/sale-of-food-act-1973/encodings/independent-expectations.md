# Independent expectations: Sale of Food Act 1973 (Singapore)

Written from `BRIEF.md` and `../source/SFA1973.txt` alone, before any `.l4` file was opened.
These are fixed. They are not to be changed after the encoding is read.

Notation: YES/NO = the legal answer; amounts in SGD; dates are calendar dates.

## A. What the Act reaches

### Food (s 2A)
- E1. s 2A(1)(a): cooked rice offered for human consumption — food: YES.
- E2. s 2A(1)(d): chewing gum — food: YES.
- E3. s 2A(1)(e): a substance that is not otherwise food but that the Minister has declared by Gazette order to be food — food: YES.
- E4. s 2A(3)(b): a medicinal product under the Medicines Act, even if swallowed by humans — food: NO.
- E5. s 2A(3)(f): a tobacco product — food: NO (the exclusion applies even though it is consumed).
- E6. s 2A(3)(g): a package that is not edible — food: NO; s 2A(3)(g) edible packaging (e.g. a rice-paper wrapper) — food: YES.
- E7. s 2A(3)(h): animal fodder — food: NO.
- E8. s 2A(3)(i): a substance of a kind used for human consumption that the Minister has declared not to be food — food: NO.
- E9. s 2A(4): rotten meat unfit for consumption — still food: YES.
- E10. s 2A(5): a live lobster for human consumption — food: YES (live animals may be food).

### Unsafe (s 2C)
- E11. s 2C(1): food contaminated with salmonella, likely to cause illness when consumed per its reasonable intended use — unsafe: YES.
- E12. s 2C(2)(d): peanuts, harmful only to people with a peanut allergy — unsafe: NO.
- E13. s 2C(2)(c): food harmful only in inappropriate quantities — unsafe: NO.
- E14. s 2C(2)(b): food objected to on religious grounds, not otherwise likely to cause harm — unsafe: NO.

### Unsuitable (s 2D)
- E15. s 2D(1)(a): food deteriorated to an extent affecting its reasonable intended use — unsuitable: YES.
- E16. s 2D(1)(c): product of a diseased animal, not declared safe under another Act — unsuitable: YES; same but declared safe — unsuitable on that limb: NO.
- E17. s 2D(1)(d): food containing an unexpected foreign object (glass) — unsuitable: YES.
- E18. s 2D(2)(b): food containing an agricultural chemical within regulation limits and nothing else wrong — unsuitable: NO.
- E19. Food with nothing wrong under any limb — unsuitable: NO.

### Sell (s 2E)
- E20. s 2E(1)(a): bartering food — sell: YES.
- E21. s 2E(1)(l): giving away food for the purpose of advertisement — sell: YES.
- E22. s 2E(3)(b): donating food for a charitable purpose (no other limb) — sell: NO.
- E23. s 2E(2)(a): exchanging food between friends, non-commercial — sell: NO.
- E24. s 2E(1)(n)(i): supplying meals to hospital patients free of charge — sell: YES.
- E25. s 2E(1)(j): disposing of food by raffle — sell: YES.
- E26. s 2E(2)(b): food and accommodation to a person at a private residence in exchange for labour — sell: NO.

### Food business (s 2B)
- E27. s 2B(1)(b): selling food on the Internet — food business: YES.
- E28. s 2B(1): a charity selling home-baked food on one occasion only — food business: YES.
- E29. s 2B(2)(a): an Internet auction site (not trading in food) acting as intermediary for reward — food business: NO.
- E30. s 2B(2)(b): business consisting exclusively of hiring out marquees, tables and chairs — food business: NO.
- E31. s 2B(1)(c): a fish farm (primary food production) — food business: YES.

### Non-retail food business (s 2F)
- E32. s 2F(1), (2)(c): a factory making frozen meals for wholesalers, no retail component, not primary production — NRFB: YES.
- E33. s 2F(1)(a): a business carried out solely for a retail (EPHA First Schedule) purpose, e.g. a hawker stall — NRFB: NO.
- E34. s 2F(1)(b): a primary food production business only (a vegetable farm) — NRFB: NO.
- E35. s 2F(1) "but includes": a food business with a central-kitchen (non-retail) operation and a retail café component — NRFB: YES.
- E36. Something that is not a food business at all (marquee hire) — NRFB: NO.

### Adulteration (s 25)
- E37. s 25(c): food mixed with a substance of lower commercial value — adulterated: YES.
- E38. s 25(d): food not complying with the prescribed standard — adulterated: YES.
- E39. s 25(e): food containing a substance rendering it injurious to health — adulterated: YES.
- E40. s 25(b): food from which an ingredient has been extracted so its nutritive properties are less than pure food — adulterated: YES.
- E41. Pure, normal, compliant food — adulterated: NO.

## B. Offences and penalties

### Part 3 (ss 11-20)
- E42. s 11: selling adulterated food without informing the purchaser of the adulteration — offence: YES.
- E43. s 11: selling adulterated food having fully informed the purchaser of the nature of the adulteration at the time of sale — offence: NO.
- E44. s 12: selling food with a substance prohibited by regulations — offence: YES.
- E45. s 13: food containing a greater proportion of a substance than permitted — offence: YES.
- E46. s 14: food containing exactly 50 parts per million methyl alcohol — offence: NO.
- E47. s 14: food containing 51 parts per million methyl alcohol — offence: YES.
- E48. s 15(1): selling food the seller knows is unsafe — offence: YES.
- E49. s 15(1): seller did not know but ought reasonably to have known it was unsafe — offence: YES.
- E50. s 15(1): food unsafe, seller neither knew nor ought reasonably to have known — offence under s 15: NO.
- E51. s 15(2)-(3): food unsuitable but safe, seller knows it is unsuitable — offence: YES.
- E52. s 16(1): selling food labelled non-compliantly — offence: YES.
- E53. s 17: selling food labelled in a misleading manner — offence: YES.
- E54. s 18: food not of the nature demanded, to the prejudice of the purchaser — offence: YES; same but not to the prejudice of the purchaser — offence under s 18: NO.
- E55. s 19: selling food stored under insanitary conditions — offence: YES.
- E56. A sale with none of the Part 3 defects — no Part 3 offence.

### s 16A and its defences
- E57. s 16A(2): a seller publishing an advertisement false as to composition (s 16A(1)(b)) — offence: YES.
- E58. s 16A(6)(a): accused is a carrier/broadcaster with no control over content, no (7) circumstance — defence available: YES.
- E59. s 16A(6)(b): accused is an advertising publisher who published in the ordinary course of business, no (7) circumstance — defence: YES.
- E60. s 16A(7)(a): publisher who ought reasonably to have known publication was an offence — defence: NO.
- E61. s 16A(7)(b): publisher previously informed in writing by the Director-General — defence: NO.
- E62. s 16A(7)(c): carrier who is also proprietor of the food business whose ads these are — defence: NO.
- E63. Accused neither a carrier nor an advertising publisher — s 16A(6) defence: NO.

### s 21 licensing
- E64. s 21: carrying on an NRFB without a licence — offence (s 24(1)): YES.
- E65. s 21: carrying on an NRFB in accordance with a licence — offence: NO.
- E66. s 21: carrying on a retail-only food business without a licence — s 21 offence: NO.
- E67. s 24/49: penalty for a s 21 first offence — fine up to $5,000, no imprisonment.

### s 10K
- E68. s 10K(1)(b): person to whom a direction is addressed refuses to comply without reasonable excuse — offence: YES; maximum $10,000 fine and/or 12 months.
- E69. s 10K(1): same but with reasonable excuse — offence: NO.
- E70. s 10K(3)-(4): removing a direction affixed under s 10C(1)(c) while it remains in force — offence: YES; maximum $5,000 and/or 6 months. After the direction has ceased to be in force — offence: NO.

### s 40
- E71. s 40(2)-(3): selling a food contact article in contravention of a Minister's prohibition notification — offence: YES; penalty via s 49 ($5,000 first).
- E72. s 40(4)(a): carrier with no control over content — defence: YES.
- E73. s 40(5)(b): carrier previously informed in writing by DG — defence: NO.
- E74. s 40(5): the accused is proprietor of a food business but neither (5)(a) nor (5)(b) applies — the s 40(4) defence remains available: YES (s 40(5) has no counterpart to s 16A(7)(c)).

### s 41
- E75. s 41(1): obstructing an authorised officer — offence: YES.
- E76. s 41(2)(a): breaking an officer's seal without authority — offence: YES; with authority — offence: NO.

### ss 5, 6, 7, 10(3), 33(5), 46(6)
- E77. s 5(4): refusing to produce records requested under s 5 — offence: YES.
- E78. s 6(4): refusing an officer's sample demand under s 6 — offence: YES.
- E79. s 6(3): retailer refuses to sell less than the whole of an unopened retail package — offence: NO.
- E80. s 7(2): seller refuses to let an officer take milk in course of delivery under contract — offence: YES.
- E81. s 7(1)(b): for food other than milk, without the purchaser's request or consent, seller refuses — offence: NO (no power arises).
- E82. s 10(3): using a copy of an analysis result as an advertisement — offence: YES.
- E83. s 33(5): giving a false written warranty without reason to believe it true — offence: YES; with reason to believe it true — offence: NO.
- E84. s 46(6): wilfully furnishing false information in a licence application — offence: YES and the licence is void.

### Penalties
- E85. s 49: first conviction for an offence without express penalty — max fine $5,000, no imprisonment.
- E86. s 49: second or subsequent conviction — max fine $10,000 and/or 3 months' imprisonment.
- E87. s 48(3): failing to give name and address on demand — max fine $300 (also on repeat; s 49 does not apply because a penalty is expressly provided).
- E88. s 10K(2) on repeat — still $10,000/12 months (s 49 does not apply).
- E89. s 56(1)(r): regulation offence, continuing for 3 days after conviction — maximum $5,000 + 3 x $500 = $6,500.
- E90. s 56(1)(r): continuing 2 days and part of a third day — counts 3 days — $6,500.

### Composition (s 50)
- E91. s 50(1): prescribed compoundable offence, sum $800 — may compound: YES.
- E92. s 50(1): sum $1,500 — may compound for that sum: NO (cap $1,000).
- E93. s 50(1): offence not prescribed as compoundable — may compound: NO.

## C. Defences and who else is liable

- E94. s 31: defence that the purchaser bought for analysis and was not prejudiced — NOT a defence.
- E95. s 32: accused shows only that the act was not wilful — NOT a defence; plus all reasonable steps to ascertain the sale would not be an offence — defence: YES.
- E96. s 33(1),(3): Part 3 offence, written warranty, no reason to disbelieve, conformity would avoid offence, notice and copy delivered to prosecutor and registered-post notice to warrantor within 7 days of service of summons — defence: YES.
- E97. s 33(3): summons served 1 March 2026; notice delivered 8 March 2026 — in time: YES; delivered 9 March 2026 — in time: NO (defence fails).
- E98. s 33(3): no registered-post notice to the warrantor — defence: NO.
- E99. s 33(2): warrantor resident outside Singapore; accused did not take reasonable steps or did not believe — defence: NO; took reasonable steps and did believe — defence: YES (other conditions met).
- E100. s 33(1): warranty defence raised against a s 21 (Part 4) offence — available: NO.
- E101. s 33(1)(b): accused had reason to believe the food did not conform — defence: NO.
- E102. s 26: food sold in an unopened package whose label states X manufactured it — X deemed manufacturer and liable to same fine as seller: YES; package opened — s 26 presumption: NO.
- E103. s 27(b): employee sells on employer's behalf — employer liable as if it sold personally: YES; employee also deemed to sell (s 27(a)): YES.
