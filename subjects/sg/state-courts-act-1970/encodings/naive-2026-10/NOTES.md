## Why this Act, and why scoped

**23 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the civil
jurisdiction of the District and Magistrates' Courts and its costs consequences --
which court a claimant can use, and what it costs to use the wrong one. Appeals
from the State Courts are in the `supreme-court-of-judicature-act-1969` row.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Passing off cannot be brought in a State Court at all

s 19(3)(ca) and s 52(1A)(aa): neither the District Court nor the Magistrate's Court may
hear "an action in passing off", whatever its value. A $20,000 passing-off claim must
go to the High Court. Asserted.

### 2. A Magistrate's Court cannot grant an injunction alone

s 52(1A)(a): a Magistrate's Court has no jurisdiction where "there is no claim for any
sum of money". A small claim for an injunction or declaration only must go to the
District Court. Asserted.

### 3. The parties can agree the District Court limit away

s 23: by a memorandum signed by the parties or their solicitors, a District Court may
try an action of **any** value. Asserted at $400,000.

### 4. Abandoning the excess is permanent

s 22: a claimant may abandon the amount over $250,000 to sue in the District Court,
but "the judgment ... is a full discharge of all demands in respect of the cause of
action". s 35: and a claim cannot be split into two actions. Asserted.

### 5. Contributory negligence does not count against the costs penalty

s 39: a claimant who sues in the High Court and recovers within a State Court limit is
confined to that court's costs. s 39(2): the recovery is measured **before** any
deduction for contributory negligence. A claimant with $300,000 of loss who recovers
$200,000 after a one-third reduction keeps High Court costs. Asserted.

### 6. Where criminal jurisdiction reaches

ss 50(2), 51(2): offences in Singapore, on Singapore ships and aircraft, by citizens on
the high seas or on any aircraft, and wherever a written law makes an offence triable
here. Asserted.
