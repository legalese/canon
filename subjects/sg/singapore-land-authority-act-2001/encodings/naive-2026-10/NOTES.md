## Why this Act, and why scoped

**11 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. It is an institutional Act
that sets up the Authority running land registration, survey and State land. This
row takes the rules with decision content: the definition of State land (s 2), the
power to demand land information and its offences (s 8), personal immunity (s 12),
borrowing (s 20), the financial year (s 21), the symbol offence (s 30), enforcement
powers and the Third Schedule list (s 31), officers' and partners' liability (s 32),
composition (s 33), secrecy (s 35), and board size, quorum, voting, resignation,
vacancy and disqualification (s 5, First Schedule paras 7, 9, 11, 13). The functions
and powers lists (ss 6, 7), Ministerial directions, staff, funds, estimates and the
Third Schedule laws' own content are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The Authority's officers police a fixed list of provisions, not land law at large

s 31 enforcement powers (demanding identity, documents and attendance) reach only
offences under this Act and "any written law set out in the Third Schedule". The
Schedule names provisions section by section: s 27 of the Land Titles Act 1993 is in,
s 28 is not; the State Lands Protection Act 2022 is in only at ss 5, 6, 10(2), 12(5),
18(5), 23(5) and (7), 24(3) and 26(1) and (2); the Land Betterment Charge Act 2021 is
in whole. The State Lands Act 1920 and the Registration of Deeds Act 1988, both of
which s 6 names among the laws the Authority administers, are not in the Schedule at
all. The powers also need the officer to declare the office and produce the
identification card. Asserted.

### 2. A nature reserve is not State land

s 2 defines State land as all land in Singapore except granted freehold or
perpetuity land, land under a Government agreement, lease or licence, and "land
dedicated as national parks or nature reserves". Land that was granted or leased and
then surrendered, resumed, compulsorily acquired, forfeited or reverted comes back in.
Reading the "includes" limb as overriding the exclusions is an inference. Asserted.

### 3. Ignoring an information request costs $1,000 plus $100 a day

s 8 lets the Authority demand land information from "any person" by a written,
served request specifying the particulars. Wilful refusal, or neglect without a
lawful excuse "the proof of which lies on the person", or wilfully giving false
information, is an offence: a fine up to $1,000 and, for a continuing offence, $100
for "every day or part of a day" after conviction. No imprisonment. s 8(4) excuses
information not in the person's possession or that the law forbids disclosing; this
row reads that as no answer to a charge of giving false information (an inference).
Asserted.

### 4. The penalties rank oddly

Obstructing an officer (s 31(2)) carries $10,000 or 6 months; using a confusingly
similar symbol (s 30(2)) $5,000 or 6 months; a staff member's unauthorised
disclosure (s 35(2)) only $2,000, but up to 12 months, the longest term in the Act.
Composition (s 33) is capped at $1,000 and available only for offences "prescribed
as a compoundable offence"; no prescribing regulations were read. Asserted.

### 5. Borrowing from banks needs the Minister for Finance; borrowing from Government does not

s 20: loans "from the Government", or "with the approval of the Minister for Finance"
from banks or other financial institutions in Singapore or elsewhere. Treating any
other lender as outside s 20 is an inference. Asserted.

### 6. A small board quorum, and a partner's escape route

The board has a Chairperson, a Deputy and 8 to 20 others (s 5). The quorum is "half
the number of its members" (para 13(2)); simple majority of those voting, with a
casting vote on a tie (para 13(5)); 4 members can force a meeting within 7 days
(para 13(7)). Missing 3 consecutive meetings without cause, as decided by the
Authority itself, vacates the office (para 9(b)); a 6-month sentence or a dishonesty
conviction disqualifies unless pardoned (para 11). Under s 32(5) a partner escapes a
partnership's offence only by proving ignorance or an attempt to prevent it.
Asserted.

### 7. Immunity needs reasonable care as well as good faith

s 12(1) protects members, officers and those acting under the Authority's direction
only for acts done "in good faith and with reasonable care". The s 12(2) protection
for errors in information supplied to the public needs only good faith and the
ordinary course of duty. s 12(1) carries an Act 42 of 2022 annotation; what it
changed was not read. Asserted.
