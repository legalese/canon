# Wholesome Meat and Fish Act 1999 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation. The deposit says it
"incorporates all amendments up to and including 1 December 2021" and is the
current version as at 01 Oct 2026. The latest annotation seen is Act 25 of 2021
wef 1 April 2022 (ss 6(6), 20(4), 23(4)).

**Checks:** one case file, 55 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**6 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
trader, slaughter-house or seller of meat or fish meets: when a consignment may be
imported, exported or transhipped (ss 5, 6), when a product may be sold (s 23),
the defences (ss 6(5)-(6), 17(3), 23(3)-(4)), licensed slaughter (s 11), appeals
(s 9), forfeiture (s 32), officers' liability (s 31(1)), composition (s 39), the
cap on offences in the rules (s 42(3)) and the maximum penalties. Administration,
licence-grant criteria, show-cause procedure, examination and closure orders,
enforcement powers, service and evidence, and exemptions are not encoded. So is
everything the Act leaves to rules: prescribed standards, labelling, fees, and
which offences can be compounded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A licence is not enough: each consignment needs its own permit

s 5 requires a licence to import, export or tranship at all. s 6 then requires a
licensee to hold "a permit ... in respect of each consignment", and for imports the
"whole consignment" must match the permit's description, meet the prescribed
standards and be packaged and labelled as prescribed. Transhipment needs only the
licence and the permit. The licence offence (s 5(2)) carries up to $50,000 or 2
years, rising to $100,000 or 3 years on a repeat. The permit offence (s 6(4))
carries $10,000 or 12 months, rising to $20,000 or 2 years. Asserted.

### 2. Every export, fish included, must have gone through a licensed processor

s 6(2)(d) requires that "the whole consignment has been processed in a processing
establishment licensed under this Act", with no carve-out for fresh or whole fish.
Exported meat must also come from licensed slaughter or lawful import (para (c)).
The exporter must give "satisfactory evidence" that the consignment meets the
destination country's requirements (para (f)). Read literally, a fresh fish export
that never passed through a licensed processing establishment fails. The encoding
follows the words. Whether practice or the rules read (d) more narrowly was not
checked. Asserted.

### 3. The sale offence is the widest and is punished like trading without a licence

s 23(1) bars selling, supplying, possessing for sale or advertising any product that
was unlawfully imported, not inspected and certified under s 10, from unlawful
slaughter, from an unlicensed processor, cold store or wholesale market, unmarked,
mislabelled or falsely labelled, or "diseased, adulterated or unfit". A product found
on premises used for sale or storage "is deemed to be in that person's possession
for sale until the contrary is proved" (s 23(5)). The maximum is the same as s 5:
$50,000 or 2 years, and $100,000 or 3 years on a repeat. Para (e), read alone,
catches every product "not marked, branded or tagged in accordance with section
20"; s 20 marks only carcases from licensed slaughter-houses. The encoding narrows
(e) to products that s 20 requires to be marked. That is an inference. Asserted.

### 4. The due diligence defence has a 7-clear-day notice trap

ss 6(5) and 23(3) let the accused prove the offence was due to another person or a
cause beyond their control, and that they took all reasonable precautions. If the
defence blames another person, it cannot be used "without permission of the court"
unless written notice identifying that person reached the prosecutor "within a
period ending 7 clear days before the hearing" (ss 6(6), 23(4), as amended by Act 25
of 2021). The encoding counts clear days as whole days strictly between service and
hearing. That count is an interpretation. Asserted.

### 5. The slaughter-house defence covers slaughtering, not disposal

s 17(1) gives the owner and occupier two duties. They must not slaughter a diseased
or unfit animal, and they must dispose of an unfit carcase in the prescribed manner.
The s 17(3) defence ("could not with reasonable diligence have ascertained") is
written only for the first. Separately, s 11(3) deems the occupier of premises where
unlawful slaughter happens to be the slaughterer "until the occupier proves" lack of
knowledge or consent. "Animal" means a bird, mammal, reptile or amphibian (s 2(1)),
so fish are not slaughtered under s 11. Asserted.

### 6. Seven days is the window everywhere

An appeal to the Minister must be lodged "within 7 days" of receiving the notice,
and the Minister's decision "is final" (s 9(1)). A condition or suspension takes
effect on the notified date despite the appeal "unless the Minister otherwise
orders" (s 9(2)). Seized items with no prosecution are deemed forfeited "on the
expiry of 7 days" from the notified date unless claimed. They are deemed forfeited at
once if the owner consents to disposal (s 32(5), (8), (9)). "Within 7 days" is
encoded as at most 7 days, which is an inference about the boundary. Asserted.

### 7. Small things

- Composition is capped at $1,000, but only for offences prescribed as compoundable
  (s 39). Asserted.
- A rule offence may carry at most $10,000, plus $100 a day for a continuing offence
  after conviction (s 42(3)). Asserted.
- Directors, managers and similar officers are guilty of the body's offence unless
  they prove both no consent or connivance and due diligence (s 31(1)). Asserted.
- Refusing a plain-clothes officer who will not declare their office and produce
  identification on demand is not an offence (s 4(2)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The Wholesome Meat and Fish rules (standards, labelling, compoundable offences,
  export certification) were not retrieved. Much of the Act's content lives there.
- s 23(1)(e) and s 6(2)(d) need checking against practice or case law. Both are read
  here literally or narrowed by inference.
- Licence-grant criteria (ss 7(2)-(3), 13(4)-(5)), the show-cause procedure (ss 8, 14)
  and the enforcement powers (Part 5) are not encoded.
