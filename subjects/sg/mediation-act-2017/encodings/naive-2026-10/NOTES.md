## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its **everyday-life relevance** (Tier 1 of the remaining Acts), not by
citation count. Anyone who settles a neighbour, family, consumer or business dispute
through a private mediator meets two parts of this Act: the rule that what was said
in mediation stays confidential, and the route by which a mediated settlement becomes
a court order. This row takes when the Act applies (ss 4, 6, 16), the stay of a
lawsuit brought despite a mediation agreement (s 8), confidentiality and evidence
(ss 9, 10, 11(3)) and recording a settlement (s 12). Not encoded: the definitions
beyond what the rules use, s 4(5)–(6), the Minister's orders under s 6(4)–(5), the
international settlement agreement carve-back (s 6(3)), designations (s 7), the
factors in s 11(2), which court records a settlement (s 12(6)), and the rule-making
powers (ss 13–15).

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Every party agreeing is not enough to put mediation talk in evidence

s 9(2)(a) lets a mediation communication be disclosed to outsiders with the consent
of all the parties (and of a non-party who made it). But s 10 says a mediation
communication "is not to be admitted in evidence in any court, arbitral or
disciplinary proceedings except with the permission of a court or an arbitral
tribunal under section 11". There is no consent exception in s 10, so on its words
the parties cannot agree their way round it. Asserted.

### 2. A settlement can be made a court order only if no lawsuit was started, and fast

s 12(1) is limited to a dispute "for which no proceedings have been commenced in a
court", needs the consent of all the other parties, and s 12(2) sets 8 weeks from the
settlement unless the court allows longer. The court may then record it only if the
mediation was run by a designated mediation service provider **or** a certified
mediator, the agreement is written and signed for all parties, and it has the
prescribed information (s 12(3)). A settlement with an uncertified mediator outside a
designated provider cannot be recorded. Once recorded it is enforceable like a
judgment (s 12(5)). Asserted.

### 3. The confidentiality duty has no stated penalty

s 9(1): "a person must not disclose any mediation communication ... to any third
party to the mediation". The Act creates no offence and states no penalty for
breaching it. The duty runs only towards "third parties" (s 2(1): not a party, not
the mediator, not a mediation service provider); telling the other side or the
mediator is not restricted by s 9. Ten exceptions follow in s 9(2), among them
seeking legal advice, protecting a child, helping a police investigation, and
communications relating to an offence. Leaks do not count as "already public"
(s 9(2)(b)). Asserted (the exceptions); the absence of a penalty is noted, not
asserted.

### 4. Only written, private mediations count

The Act applies only to a mediation "conducted under a mediation agreement" (s 6(1)),
and "A mediation agreement must be in writing" (s 4(3)) — though it is in writing if
its content is "recorded in any form", even when concluded orally (s 4(4)). It does
not apply to court-directed mediation, or to mediation under any scheme set up by
written law (s 6(2)). There must also be a Singapore link: conducted wholly or partly
in Singapore, or an agreement choosing the Act or Singapore law. Asserted (the
reading that an agreement recorded in no form fails s 6(1) is an inference from
s 4(3)).

### 5. A stay of a lawsuit is discretionary

s 8: a party to a mediation agreement sued on a matter it covers "may apply" for a
stay, and the court "may" grant it on terms. Nothing makes the stay automatic.
Asserted (only who may apply).

### 6. Mediations from before 1 November 2017 need everyone's agreement

s 16: the Act does not apply to a mediation which commenced before 1 November 2017
unless it was still open with no settlement on that date and all parties agree. A
mediation "commences" when all parties agree to refer the dispute (s 16(3)).
Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The Mediation Rules prescribing what a recordable settlement must contain
  (s 12(3)(c)), the Family Justice Rules classes (s 12(6)(a)), and the list of
  designated providers and approved certification schemes (s 7) were not retrieved.
- The commencement note reads "1 November 2017: Except section 16"; the deposit
  does not say when that section came into force (it may refer to the original
  numbering, which is an inference).
- No case law on s 8 stays, s 10 permission or s 12 refusals was searched.
- The s 11(2) factors and the s 9(3) permission purposes are collapsed to "permission
  was granted".
