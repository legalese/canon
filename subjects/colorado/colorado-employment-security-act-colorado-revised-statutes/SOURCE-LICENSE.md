# Source license

The text of **Colorado Employment Security Act (Colorado Revised Statutes, Title 8, Articles 70 to 82)** is published on the [Colorado Legal Resources - Colorado Revised Statutes Annotated (LexisNexis, under contract to the Committee on Legal Services of the Colorado General Assembly)](https://leg.colorado.gov/colorado-revised-statutes).

- **Terms**: Not open, and Colorado is unusual in claiming copyright by statute: the Colorado Revised Statutes table of contents shows s 2-5-115, 'Copyright by state', and s 2-5-118, 'Official statutes - publications by other persons or agencies'. The public-access site tells anyone wanting to republish to come to the state first, and the platform itself carries 'Copyright © 2026 LexisNexis.' with LexisNexis's general Terms & Conditions. So neither redistribution nor adaptation is permitted on the register's own terms without contacting the Office of Legislative Legal Services. Against that, US federal law is that statutory text as enacted is a government edict and not copyrightable (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)); Colorado's assertion has been litigated, and any surviving claim is best understood as reaching the editorial layer (annotations, source notes, editor's notes, cross references) rather than the enacted text. The site is prepared and maintained by LexisNexis under a contract with the Committee on Legal Services, and the underlying database is prepared by the General Assembly.
- **Terms checked**: 2026-09-23, at https://www.lexisnexis.com/terms/general.aspx
- **From the terms**: "Colorado law recognizes that persons, agencies, or political subdivisions, other than the General Assembly, may seek to publish, reprint, or distribute the statutes of the state of Colorado, in print format, digital format, or both. See § 2-5-118, C.R.S. Any person, agency, or political subdivision seeking to publish, reprint, or distribute the Colorado Revised Statutes by using the statutory database containing the official text of the statutes as prepared by the General Assembly should contact the Office of Legislative Legal Services for further information."
- **Reuse and adaptation permitted, per the index check**: no, or not established
- **Attribution required**: No state attribution formula published. Cite as Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.); the online platform is provided by the Committee on Legal Services through LexisNexis.
- **Source**: https://www.lexisnexis.com/hottopics/colorado/

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
