## Why this Act, and why scoped

**5 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what an
importer, distributor, exhibitor or member of the public meets: the business
licence (s 6), what a party political film is (s 2(2), (3)), films that must be
refused classification (s 16), the unclassified-film and age-condition offences
(s 21), obscene films (ss 29-31), children (s 32), party political films (s 33),
composition (s 38B) and the Government exemption (s 40(1)). Classification
procedure and ratings (set by Ministerial order under s 13), film content
assessors, advertisement approval, regulatory action against licensees, appeals,
enforcement powers and the Minister's prohibition orders are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. An obscene or party political film is not an "unclassified film"

s 16 requires the Authority to refuse classification to obscene, party political
and prohibited films, and s 16(2) says a refused film "is an unclassified film".
Yet s 21(10) says that in s 21 a reference to an unclassified film "does not
include" an obscene, exempt, party political or prohibited film. So distributing
an obscene film is not the s 21(1) offence ($40,000 or 6 months); it is a s 29
offence, which requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe it obscene.
Asserted.

### 2. Mere possession of an obscene film is an offence, with no knowledge element

s 30(1): "Any person who has in his or her possession any obscene film" — $20,000
or 6 months or both. s 30(2) adds a higher tier where the person knew or had
reasonable cause to believe it obscene ($40,000 or 12 months; repeat $80,000 or 2
years). Advertising has the same two tiers (s 31(1): $5,000 or 6 months; s 31(2):
$50,000 or 12 months; repeat $100,000 or 2 years). Making, importing,
distributing and exhibiting (s 29) all need the knowledge element. Asserted.

### 3. Some penalties omit "or to both"

s 29(4)(a) (exhibiting an obscene film, first conviction) reads "$40,000 or to
imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years" without "or to both", while every
other tier in ss 29-31 has it, including s 29(4)(b) for a repeat conviction. s 33
(party political films, $100,000 or 2 years) also omits it. Read literally, the
court must choose one or the other. Whether that is deliberate is not something
the text says. The s 29-31 tiers are asserted; s 33's penalty is recorded in a
comment only.

### 4. The custodian presumption reaches only children and only the no-knowledge offences

s 32(1) covers anyone who causes or procures a child or young person (under 18),
or having custody allows one, to commit an offence under ss 29-31. But the
presumption in s 32(2) — that the custodian allowed it "until the contrary is
proved" — applies only to a child (below 14) and only to ss 30(1) and 31(1).
Asserted.

### 5. A party political film turns on the Authority's opinion, with seven carve-outs

s 2(2): a film is directed towards a political end if it contains matter that "in
the opinion of the Authority" is likely to affect voting, or comments on political
matter that are "either partisan or biased" (political matter includes "an issue
of public controversy in Singapore"). s 2(3) excludes news by licensed
broadcasters, election-procedure information, live records of lawful events,
records for participants, factual documentaries (not "reality" or dramatised),
and party-manifesto and candidate-declaration films without animation or drama.
The conditions inside each carve-out are taken as met when the carve-out is
chosen. Asserted.

### 6. The business licence and its continuing fine

s 6: importing, distributing or publicly exhibiting a film "in the course of any
business" without a licence or class licence is an offence ($40,000 or 12 months,
plus $500 a day after conviction). "Business" includes one not carried on for
profit (s 2(1)). Government films are outside the Act altogether (s 40(1)).
Asserted.

### 7. Composition is capped at the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000

s 38B(1), for offences prescribed as compoundable. Which offences are prescribed is
left to regulations, not retrieved. Asserted.

### 8. The age-condition defence needs documentary evidence

s 21(7), (9): selling or exhibiting in breach of an age condition is an offence
($5,000), and the defence is that documentary evidence was produced that "might
reasonably be accepted" as the person's and as proving the age, proved on a
balance of probabilities. Asserted.
