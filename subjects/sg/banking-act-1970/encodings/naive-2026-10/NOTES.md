## Why this Act, and why scoped

**35 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. Most of its 404,000
characters regulate banks' capital, ownership and conduct towards MAS. This row
takes what a depositor, a customer, or someone thinking of taking the public's
money meets.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The deposit-insurance scheme is paid before any depositor

s 62(1): in a bank's winding up, the specified liabilities rank "(a) **firstly**, any
premium contributions due and payable by the bank under the Deposit Insurance ...
Act 2011; (b) secondly, ... insured deposits", then uninsured Singapore-dollar
deposits, then foreign-currency deposits, then a resolution fund's claim. The
scheme's own unpaid premiums come before the insured depositors it exists to
protect. Asserted.

### 2. Singapore-dollar deposits rank ahead of foreign-currency deposits

s 62(1)(c), (d): uninsured deposits "in Singapore dollars" (or dischargeable in
them) rank third; all other deposits fourth. A depositor holding US dollars is paid
only after every Singapore-dollar depositor, whatever the sums. Within a class, a
shortfall abates proportionately (s 62(2)). Asserted.

### 3. Banking secrecy yields to written consent only

s 47 and Third Schedule Part 1 item 1: a bank may disclose customer information with
the customer's permission "**in writing**" (or the personal representative's, after
death). Oral consent is not enough; a spouse has no right of access; a journalist has
none. The police get it only under a specified written law (item 5). Breach: $125,000
or **3 years** for an individual (s 47(6)). Asserted.

### 4. Taking deposits without a licence -- and what is not a deposit

s 4A: accepting deposits in the course of a deposit-taking business without being a
bank or otherwise licensed is an offence ($125,000 or 3 years). But s 4B narrows both
words: an advance payment for goods, a security deposit, and money from a **close
relative** or group company are not deposits (s 4B(5), (6)); and a business that
neither holds itself out as taking deposits day to day nor does so is not
deposit-taking (s 4B(8)). And s 4A(7): a deposit taken unlawfully can still be
recovered. Asserted.

### 5. "Bank" in any language

s 5: only banks (and MAS-consented users) may use "bank or any of its derivatives in
any language" in a business name. A food bank is outside it only because it does no
financial business **and** its name shows it is not a bank (s 5(2)(e)). A non-bank
lender calling itself "Bank X" contravenes it whatever language it uses. Asserted.
