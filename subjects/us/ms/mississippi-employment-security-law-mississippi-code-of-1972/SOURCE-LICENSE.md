- **Terms**: Not open. Mississippi is the strongest copyright assertion of the group: the Secretary of State states that the laws of Mississippi are copyrighted by the State of Mississippi, citing Miss. Code Ann. s. 1-1-9, and that section (seen in the code's own search results) provides that copyright in the Mississippi Code of 1972 and its notes, annotations and indexes is taken in the publisher's name and then assigned to, and owned by, the State. The official code is published under contract by LexisNexis (Michie) for the Joint Legislative Committee on Compilation, Revision and Publication of Legislation, and the public-access platform footers read 'Copyright (c) 2026 LexisNexis.' with its own 'Terms & Conditions'. Against that: under the government edicts doctrine (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)) the enacted statutory text itself is not copyrightable no matter what the State asserts, so the words of the sections may be reused and adapted; what is realistically protected is the publisher's apparatus (annotations, editorial notes, indexes, the platform's own formatting) and what is contractually restricted is bulk use of the LexisNexis platform under its Terms & Conditions. open_licence is set false because neither the State nor the publisher grants reuse-and-adaptation rights, and there is no state-run source to take the text from. A human must decide whether to rely on the government edicts doctrine here and should read the LexisNexis Terms & Conditions before any bulk extraction.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
