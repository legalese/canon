# Public Utilities Act 2001 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, with amendments to Act 8
of 2026 (in force 29 May 2026) shown.

**Checks:** one case file, 92 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**10 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. Most of the Act constitutes
and empowers the Public Utilities Board. This row takes what an owner, contractor,
plumber or investor actually meets: the Part 5 water offences and their penalties
(ss 45A–52, 57, 58), the due-diligence defence and principal liability (ss 47, 47A,
50, 56A), who may do plumbing and water-service design work (ss 40G, 40H and the s 2
definitions), 5% controller notices (ss 44E, 44J), composition (s 59), recovery of
unpaid bills (s 25) and refunds (s 68B). The Board's constitution and finances,
tariffs, entry powers, Part 3A compensation, plumber licensing procedure, approved
suppliers' conditions and the 12%/30% controller approvals are not encoded.

The arrangement of sections at the head of the deposit is out of step with the body
from about s 24 onwards (for example it puts "Duty to enquire before excavation" at
56A). This row follows the body, where agent liability is s 56A and the excavation
duty is s 57; the cross-references in ss 47(2) and 47A(3) to "section 56A" agree with
the body.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Damaging a big water main is twenty times worse, and "otherwise" is enough

s 47A(1): damaging a Board water main or connecting pipe "whether wilfully or
otherwise" carries $40,000 or 3 months; if it "is 300 mm or more in diameter", $200,000
or 3 years. The tier turns on diameter alone: 299 mm and 300 mm sit on opposite sides.
Asserted.

### 2. The due-diligence defence is not available to an employer liable through s 56A

ss 47(2) and 47A(3) give a defence of "all reasonable precautions and ... all due
diligence", "but this defence is not available to any person who may be liable by
virtue of section 56A". So a contractor whose operator strikes a main can be liable
for the operator's act and cannot plead its own diligence. It can still escape under
s 56A itself by proving both no consent or connivance and no neglect. Asserted.

### 3. Some water offences have the defence and some do not

s 50(6) gives the defence for s 50(1), (2)(b) and (2)(d), but not for dishonest
abstraction (s 50(2)(a)) or meter interference (s 50(2)(c)). Meter tampering has the
defence; meter interference does not. Asserted.

### 4. Supplying drinking water without approval carries the longest term

s 41(2): $50,000 or 5 years, longer than any other offence in scope. Offences with no
penalty of their own (ss 39, 40G(8), 40H, 45, 46, 49, 51, 52) fall to s 58: $10,000 or
12 months and $250 a day. Asserted.

### 5. Who may do the work depends on whether the design has a pump or a tank

"Limited WSI design works" (s 2) means designing a downstream installation with no
pumping equipment or storage tank. A licensed plumber may do that; a professional
engineer may too (s 40G(9)). Once a pump or tank is in the design, only a
professional engineer may be engaged (s 40H), and a licensed plumber may not do it. A
professional engineer who is not a licensed plumber may not do sanitary works.
Asserted.

### 6. Small residential tanks escape the detailed security duties, not the general one

s 45A(3) disapplies the locking, checking and record-keeping duties of s 45A(2) to
residential premises not supplied through a master meter. The general duty in (1) to
secure the tank still applies. NEWater tanks are outside s 45A. Asserted.

### 7. A 5% controller has 7 days, with two narrow escapes

s 44E: notice within 7 days of becoming a 5% controller (5% or more, but under 12%).
Defences: unaware, and notified within 14 days of becoming aware; or the crossing came
from an associate with no arrangement, and notified within 7 days. Penalties under
s 44J are $500,000 for an individual and $1 million otherwise. Asserted.

### 8. Overpaid water bills: five years, and only on the Board's terms

s 68B: no refund "Except as provided in this section"; a claim must be made within 5
years, in the Board's form, and the Board must be satisfied. Five years is encoded as
fewer than 60 whole months, which is an inference about the boundary. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Which offences are "prescribed as compoundable" (s 59(1)(b)) is left to regulations,
  which were not retrieved; the encoding takes it as an input.
- The persons "responsible" for maintenance and security (ss 45(8), 45A(7)) are left
  to regulations and are not encoded.
- The exact boundary of "within 7 days" and "within 5 years" was not checked against
  the Interpretation Act; the encoding treats day 7 as in time and month 60 as out.
- The 12%/30% controller approvals (s 44F), remedial directions and special
  administration were not encoded.
- No cases or PUB guidance were searched.
