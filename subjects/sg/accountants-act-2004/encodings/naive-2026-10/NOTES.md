# Accountants Act 2004 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation (amendments to Act 24
of 2025).

**Checks:** one case file, 38 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**35 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it, thinly, mostly to say who
may audit. This row takes what is reserved to public accountants and what a public
accountant or firm is exposed to. Registration procedure, practice monitoring and
quality reviews, and the inquiry procedure are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Only audit is reserved

s 2: "public accountancy services" means "the audit and reporting on financial
statements and the doing of any other acts that are required by any written law to
be done by a public accountant". Bookkeeping, tax returns, management accounts and
valuation advice are not reserved. Anyone may provide them for a fee. Asserted.

### 2. An offence of contravening a disentitlement

s 57(1): a person "is not entitled to recover any expenses or remuneration" for public
accountancy services unless registered or approved. s 57(3): "Any person who
contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence" ($5,000 or 12 months).
Subsection (1) states what a person cannot recover, not what a person must not do.
Whether suing for the fee, receiving it, or providing the service is the
contravention, the text does not say. Asserted as that absence; the disentitlement is
encoded.

### 3. Auditors face a tenth of the financial penalty doctors and lawyers face

s 52(2): the Oversight Committee may suspend a public accountant for up to **2 years**
and impose a penalty up to **$10,000**. The Medical Registration Act allows 3 years and
$100,000 (s 59D); the Legal Profession Act 5 years and $100,000 (s 83). Asserted
against the other two rows' figures.

### 4. When the firm misbehaves and no one is to blame, everyone is

s 25(2): the directors or partners who are public accountants are "jointly liable to
disciplinary proceedings" for the firm's conduct where it "cannot be attributed to the
act or omission of any particular public accountant". Asserted.

### 5. Old convictions still bar employment

s 59(1)(c): without the Oversight Committee's consent, a public accountant or firm must
not knowingly employ anyone "convicted of an offence ... and sentenced to imprisonment
for a term of not less than 12 months or to a fine of not less than $5,000". No time
limit, and the offence need not involve dishonesty. Asserted.

### 6. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 10(1):** an applicant aged 21 or more who meets the prescribed requirements "is
  entitled" to registration, but s 11(5) lets the Committee refuse on character.
- **s 56(2):** only an approved accounting corporation may use "Public Accounting
  Corporation" or "PAC".
- **s 24:** an accounting corporation owes clients the same fiduciary, confidential and
  ethical duties as a public accountant.
