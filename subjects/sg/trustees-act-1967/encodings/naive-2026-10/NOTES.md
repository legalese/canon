## Why this Act, and why scoped

**16 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a private
trustee and a beneficiary most often need: the duty of care, investment, advancement,
the number and replacement of trustees, delegation, remuneration, relief and
impounding. Powers of sale, maintenance, protective trusts, vesting orders, payment
into court and charitable trusts are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A solicitor who is sole trustee has no statutory right to be paid

s 41R(1): a trust corporation gets reasonable remuneration. s 41R(2): a professional
trustee gets it only if they are "not a trust corporation or a sole trustee" and every
other trustee agrees in writing. A professional who is the **sole** trustee therefore
has no statutory remuneration. Neither does a lay trustee. And s 41R(4): if the trust
instrument makes **any** provision about remuneration, the statutory right falls away
entirely. Asserted.

### 2. Advancement is still capped at one-half

s 34(3): capital advanced for a beneficiary must not exceed "one-half of the
presumptive or vested share". (England removed this cap in 2014; Singapore's text
keeps it.) A prior life tenant must be an adult and consent in writing (s 34(4)).
Asserted.

### 3. More than four named trustees: only the first four count

s 36(1): for private trusts, "the 4 first named (who are able and willing to act) must
alone be the trustees". Charitable and public trusts are exempt. Asserted.

### 4. What cannot be delegated

s 41B(2): investment management and rent collection can be delegated. Deciding who gets
a distribution, whether a payment comes from income or capital, and who becomes a
trustee cannot. A trustee who took care in appointing and reviewing an agent is not
liable for the agent's default (s 41N). Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 3A(2):** the statutory duty of care can be excluded by the trust instrument.
- **s 6(3):** advice before investing may be skipped if the trustee *reasonably*
  concludes it is unnecessary.
- **s 9:** a loan up to two-thirds of the advised value, on proper advice, is not a breach
  merely because of its size. s 10: over-lending makes the trustee liable only for the
  excess.
- **s 37:** a trustee abroad for more than 12 months can be replaced.
- **s 60:** relief needs honesty **and** reasonableness **and** fairness.
- **s 61:** a beneficiary who instigated, requested or consented *in writing* to a breach
  can have their interest impounded. Oral consent is not enough. The section still
  refers to "a married woman restrained from anticipation".
