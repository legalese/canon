# Wildlife Act 1965 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation (WA1965.txt), incorporating
amendments to 1 December 2021, with Act 23 of 2023 (wef 18 December 2023) and Act 15
of 2026 (wef 1 July 2026) shown.

**Checks:** one case file, 54 assertions satisfied, 0 errors.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**5 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what an
ordinary person or business meets: what counts as wildlife (s 2), the
approval-based prohibitions (ss 5A to 5C, 7 to 9), defence of property (s 6), their
maximum penalties, the maxima of the other offences (ss 10, 11A, 12, 12B, 12E, 12G,
21(3)), composition (s 12K), arrest without warrant (s 12), forfeiture of a
conveyance (s 12C) and the time limit on a repatriation direction (s 12E(3)).
Appointments, search and seizure powers, s 12D forfeiture, expenses, vicarious and
officer liability, service of documents, exemptions and the rules are not encoded.
Which species are protected, and which offences are compoundable, are left to
subsidiary legislation that was not retrieved.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Selling, exporting and importing need no intent; feeding, releasing and killing do

ss 5A, 5B and 5C prohibit only what a person does "intentionally". ss 8 and 9 have
no such word: offering for sale, selling or exporting wildlife or any part of it,
or importing living wildlife, without the Director-General's written approval is
an offence on its face. Asserted (an accidental release is not an offence; an
unknowing sale is).

### 2. Importing protected wildlife carries no more than importing any other

s 8(3) raises the maximum for selling or exporting protected wildlife to $50,000 or
2 years. s 9(3), for importing living wildlife, has a single tier: $10,000 or 12
months, protected or not. Asserted.

### 3. Defence of property covers killing, not keeping

s 6(1): nothing in the Act makes it unlawful for the occupier or person in charge
of land to "kill, trap or take" wildlife found damaging crops or property there.
It says nothing of keeping the animal, and nothing of a neighbour. The onus of
proving the damage is on the person alleging it (s 6(2)). Asserted. INFERENCE,
also asserted: s 6 does not excuse setting a snare or other dangerous device under
s 7, because s 7 prohibits the setting, not the killing.

### 4. Feeding wildlife is fine-only the first time

After Act 15 of 2026, s 5A(3)(a) makes a first feeding offence punishable by a fine
up to $10,000 only; imprisonment (up to 12 months) arrives with a second or
subsequent conviction. Releasing wildlife (s 5B(3)) is fine-only at $5,000 with no
repeat tier. Asserted.

### 5. The killing and keeping penalties have three tiers, and the repeat tier stops at the business tier

s 5C(3): protected wildlife, $50,000 or 2 years; otherwise in the course of an
animal-related business, $20,000 or 12 months; otherwise $10,000 or 6 months, rising
to $20,000 or 12 months on a second or subsequent conviction. A repeat offender
dealing in protected wildlife faces no more than the protected tier. Asserted.

### 6. Composition is capped at $2,000

s 12K: the lower of half the prescribed maximum fine and $2,000. For every offence
in this Act with a maximum of $4,000 or more, the cap is $2,000. Asserted.

### 7. Smaller points

s 12(2): no arrest if the person satisfies the officer of name, residence in
Singapore and no intent to abscond. s 12C(2): a conveyance of more than 200 tons net,
or a regular passenger aircraft or train, cannot be forfeited under s 12C(1)(a). s
12E(3): a repatriation direction binds only if made within 12 months of import or 6
months of the conclusion or composition of proceedings, whichever is later. Asserted.
INFERENCE, encoded but not asserted: while proceedings are pending, the later date
has not arrived, so a direction is in time. Where no proceedings were brought or
compounded, only the 12-month limb is available (asserted).

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The arrangement of sections at the top of the deposit numbers ss 15 to 21
  differently from the body (for example, the arrangement lists "Exemption" at
  s 19 and "Rules" at s 19A; the body has them at ss 20 and 21); the body's
  numbering was followed.
- The protected wildlife species and the compoundable offences, both prescribed
  under s 21, were not retrieved; nor were any written approvals published under
  s 5(1)(c).
- "Animal-related business" (s 2) is taken as a given fact, not encoded; the same
  definition is encoded in the Animals and Birds Act 1965 row.
- No case law was searched.
