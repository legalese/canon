The text of **South Dakota Codified Laws Chapter 43-32 - Lease of Real Property** is published on the [South Dakota Legislature - South Dakota Codified Laws (Legislative Research Council)](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes).

- **Terms**: Mixed, and the most restrictive of the three verified jurisdictions in this group. The register's own Disclaimer page (https://sdlegislature.gov/Disclaimer) says nothing about copyright or reuse at all - it is a pure accuracy disclaimer. The operative claim is statutory: SDCL 2-16-8 ('Copyrights of material--Contract for use of state's copyright') provides that material in the Codified Laws is copyrighted by the State of South Dakota 'to the extent permitted by federal copyright law', and SDCL 2-16-8.1 makes it a misdemeanour to print or distribute copyrighted material from the Codified Laws except as federal copyright law allows. Both sections were verified in force (Repealed=false) on the register's own API. The qualifier does the work. Under the government edicts doctrine confirmed in Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020), statutory text, numbering and section catchlines produced by legislators in their legislative capacity are outside copyright entirely, so federal copyright law permits no state copyright over them; the state's claim can only reach separately authored editorial material, and SDCL 2-16-13.1 in any event excludes editorial material and appendices from the official code. On that reading reuse and adaptation of the statutory text are lawful, and open_licence is set true for the enacted text only. A HUMAN MUST SIGN THIS OFF: unlike South Carolina there is no affirmative permission anywhere, the state asserts copyright by statute, and it attaches a criminal penalty to unauthorised distribution - a materially stronger posture than a bare website footer notice. Note also that the Codified Laws are published in print by a commercial publisher under contract with the Code Commission (SDCL 2-16-7, 2-16-7.1); annotations in that edition are not part of the enacted text and are not covered.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
