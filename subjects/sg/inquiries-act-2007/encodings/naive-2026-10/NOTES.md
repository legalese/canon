## Why this Act, and why scoped

**5 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. The Act is short and mostly
institutional, so this row takes the rules with decision content: what a commission
(President, s 3) and a committee (Minister, s 9) may inquire into, how each must be
composed (ss 4, 10), terminating assessors (ss 7(3), 13(3)), and from the Schedule the
bar on ruling on liability, the commission's extra enforcement powers, suspension, the
bar on using a witness's evidence against them, the offences and contempt, immediate
sentencing for contempt, the right to an advocate, and what "court" means. Secretaries,
police, terms of reference and time, rule-making, the transitional saving,
evidence-gathering powers, interpreters, the Public Prosecutor's and
Attorney-General's roles, protection and privilege, procedure, remuneration and reports
are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Contempt carries 2 years; lying or hiding documents carries 7

Every offence in the Schedule has the same maximum fine, $10,000. But giving false
evidence, distorting, suppressing or destroying evidence (para 5), and hindering,
deterring or retaliating against witnesses (para 6) carry up to **7 years**, while
contempt (para 7) — insulting a member, disrupting the sitting, refusing an oath,
disobeying a summons, refusing to answer — carries up to **2 years**. Asserted.

### 2. Only commissioners can sentence on the spot

Para 8(1): contempt "committed in the presence of the commissioners sitting in
commission" may be sentenced immediately, after hearing the offender. Para 8 speaks
only of commissioners, and para 1(4) gives only a commission the High Court's powers to
give effect to imprisonment and enforce fines. A committee of inquiry has neither. How
a committee's contempt is then dealt with is not said in the Schedule; that it must go
to ordinary prosecution is an inference, not encoded. Asserted (para 8 and para 1(4)).

### 3. The two bodies reach different subjects

A committee (s 9(1)) covers accidents causing death, serious injury or serious property
damage and threats to public safety or health, but the conduct of officers and the
management of departments only where they fall "under the responsibility of that
Minister". It has no catch-all head. A commission (s 3(1)) covers public officers and
departments outright, and anything else only if "in the opinion of the President" an
inquiry is in the public interest — so an accident reaches a commission only through
that opinion. Asserted.

### 4. Evidence given to an inquiry cannot be used against the person who gave it

Para 4: such evidence is not admissible in proceedings against the giver, except
proceedings "for an offence under this Act or an offence of giving or fabricating false
evidence". The paragraph is silent on use against anyone else. Asserted.

### 5. A commission needs someone qualified to be a High Court Judge; a committee, a District Judge

ss 4(1) and 10(1). A body of more than one must have a chairperson appointed by the
appointing authority; a sole member has the chair's powers. Para 16 then reads
"court" as the General Division of the High Court for a commission and a District Court
for a committee. Asserted.

### 6. No finding of liability, and a right to counsel for the implicated

Para 1(1): an inquiry body "must not rule on and has no power to determine the civil or
criminal liability of any person", though it is not inhibited by the likelihood of
liability being inferred. Para 10: a person whose conduct is under inquiry, or who is
"in any way implicated or concerned", is entitled to an advocate; anyone else needs the
body's permission. Asserted.

### 7. Lawful excuse only for three kinds of contempt

Only para 7(1)(d), (e) and (f) — refusing an oath, disobeying a summons, refusing to
answer — say "without lawful excuse". Insults, threats and interruptions carry no such
words. Asserted.
