## Why this Act, and why scoped

Requirement **REQ-0087**: Tier 2 of the remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by
everyday-life relevance. It asks what the Act decides for a person or business it
applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper question yet. This row takes what a
trader, freight forwarder, researcher or broker meets: what movements are controlled
(s 2), the transfer ban and the weapons "catch-all" (s 5), brokering (s 6), appeals
(ss 7(5), 8(4)), the penalties (ss 5(7), 6(9), 9, 10(2), 11(4), 12(3), 18(2), 20, 30),
composition (s 31), officers' liability (s 32(1)) and the 48-hour detention limit
(s 21(5)).

Not encoded: **what is on the list** — strategic goods and technology are whatever the
Minister prescribes by order under s 4A, and that order is not in the deposit, so
"listed" is a bare input here. Also left out: Ministerial declarations (s 35) and
exemptions (s 36), the permit and registration regulations, search and seizure powers
(ss 14 to 19), the conditions for disclosure to foreign authorities (s 12(2)),
proceedings (ss 22 to 29) and s 38.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The Act reaches ordinary goods, not only weapons

s 5(2) bans exporting, transhipping or bringing in transit **any** goods, or exporting
or transmitting **any** technology, if the person was notified by an officer, knows, or
"has reasonable grounds to suspect" it is intended or likely to be used for nuclear,
chemical or biological weapons or missiles to deliver them. Nothing need be on the
list. Reasonable suspicion alone is enough; the only way out is the s 5(9) defence of
"all reasonable inquiries", and it covers the suspicion limb only — not a person who
knew or was notified, and not listed goods. Asserted.

### 2. The fine scales with the cargo, without a cap

s 5(7) and s 6(9): on a first conviction, a fine up to $100,000 **or 3 times the value
of the goods or technology, whichever is greater**, or 2 years; on a second, $200,000
or 4 times the value, or 3 years. Value is taken at the time of the offence (s 2(4)).
So a first offence over $50,000 of goods carries up to $150,000, and a repeat over
$80,000 carries up to $320,000. Asserted.

### 3. Brokering a deal between two foreign countries is caught

s 6(1) bans arranging, negotiating, or doing "any act to facilitate" a contract the
person knows or has reason to believe will move goods or technology "from one foreign
country to another foreign country". The goods need never touch Singapore. Someone
whose sole role is transport, finance, insurance or advertising public information is
outside it (s 6(7)). A deal bringing the goods into Singapore is not brokering under
s 6 at all. Asserted.

### 4. Registration and permits do not substitute for each other

For Minister-specified **goods**, only registration excuses the broker (s 6(5)); a
permit under s 6(6) covers only catch-all goods and technology. For specified
**technology**, only a permit excuses; registration does not. A registered broker who
knows of a weapons use still offends. Asserted.

### 5. Transit approval lifts only the catch-all

s 5(6): a senior authorised officer's approval disapplies "Subsection (2)(a)" to goods
in transit. It does not touch s 5(1), so listed strategic goods in transit with that
approval but without a permit are still an offence on the words. Asserted (as a
reading of the text; whether approval is in practice tied to a permit is not in the
deposit).

### 6. Uploading is "transmitting"; the defence is for transmission only

"Transmit" includes making technology available on a computer in Singapore so it is
accessible to a person abroad (s 2(1)). s 5(8) gives a defence to one who did not know
or have reason to believe it would become so accessible — for transmission only, not
for physically exporting a document recording the technology. Public-domain technology,
patent applications and research with no practical application are outside s 5
altogether (s 5(4), (5)). Asserted.

### 7. Smaller points

Imports are not controlled; a foreign ship passing through without stopping, or
anchoring only in distress, is not "bringing in transit" (s 2). Refusing, not renewing
or cancelling a permit, and refusing or suspending registration, may be appealed to the
Minister, "whose decision is final"; the conditions imposed are not listed as
appealable (ss 7(5), 8(4)). Disclosing Part 3 information carries the smallest fine
($6,000) but 12 months (s 12(3)). Composition is capped at $10,000 and only for
prescribed offences (s 31). An officer is guilty with the company on consent,
connivance or neglect (s 32(1)). Detention after arrest without warrant may not exceed
48 hours, excluding the journey to the Magistrate (s 21(5)). Asserted.
