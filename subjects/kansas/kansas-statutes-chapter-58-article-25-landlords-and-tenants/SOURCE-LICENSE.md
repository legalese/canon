# Source license

The text of **Kansas Statutes Chapter 58, Article 25 (Landlords and Tenants)** is published on the [Kansas Statutes on the Kansas Legislature's site (statutes compiled by the Office of Revisor of Statutes)](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/statute/).

- **Terms**: Kansas does assert copyright, unusually plainly for a US register: every statute page ends with a Revisor notice and the site footer claims all rights reserved, and the Kansas.gov portal policies claim the compilation of site content as the property of the Information Network of Kansas. Those assertions cannot reach the statute text itself: under the government edicts doctrine (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)) work an official produces in the course of official duties - including a state's statutes - is not copyrightable, so the text of the Kansas Statutes is free to reuse and adapt. What the assertions can plausibly cover is the compilation, arrangement and any editorial matter, i.e. the selection and ordering of the site's pages rather than the enacted words. Practical consequence: take the statutory text, do not mirror the site's own compilation wholesale, and expect no permission problem for an adapted encoding. The official Kansas Statutes Annotated is compiled by the Office of Revisor of Statutes (a legislative agency, not a commercial publisher), though the annotated print edition is distributed commercially.
- **Terms checked**: 2026-09-23, at https://portal.kansas.gov/portal-policies/
- **From the terms**: "(c) 2026 Revisor of Statutes, State of Kansas - and, in the site footer, (c) 2026 Kansas State Legislature. All rights reserved. The Kansas.gov portal policies add: The compilation (meaning the collection, arrangement and assembly) of all content on this site is the exclusive property of INK."
- **Reuse and adaptation permitted, per the index check**: yes
- **Attribution required**: cite as Kansas Statutes Annotated (2026), Office of Revisor of Statutes, State of Kansas - prudent given the Revisor's copyright notice, though not required for the uncopyrightable text
- **Source**: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/statute/058_000_0000_chapter/058_025_0000_article/

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
