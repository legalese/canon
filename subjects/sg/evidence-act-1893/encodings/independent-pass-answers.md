# Independent pass: answers decided from the statute text alone (before opening any .l4)

Source read: source/EA1893.txt only. Format: ID. scenario -> answer (section).

## Relevancy ss 5-16
R1. Fact in issue -> evidence may be given (s 5). R2. Fact neither in issue nor declared relevant -> not (s 5 "and of no others").
R3. Fact part of same transaction as fact in issue, at different time/place -> relevant (s 6).
R4. Occasion, cause, effect, state of things or opportunity for a fact in issue -> relevant (s 7).
R5. Fact showing motive or preparation -> relevant (s 8(1)).
R6. Conduct of a party influenced by / influencing a fact in issue, before or after -> relevant (s 8(2)); conduct not so linked -> not.
R7. A bare statement is not "conduct" unless it accompanies and explains acts other than statements (s 8 Expl 1; illus (j),(k)): bare statement -> not relevant under s 8.
R8. Statement made in presence and hearing of person whose conduct is relevant and which affects the conduct -> relevant (s 8 Expl 2; illus (f),(g)).
R9. Fact necessary to explain/introduce a relevant fact -> relevant only insofar as necessary (s 9; illus (c)).
R10. Conspiracy: reasonable ground to believe 2+ conspired; act/statement of one in reference to common intention after intention first entertained -> relevant against each (s 10). No reasonable ground -> not. Act before the intention was first entertained -> not.
R11. Fact inconsistent with a fact in issue -> relevant (s 11(a)); fact making a fact in issue highly probable/improbable -> relevant (s 11(b)); neither -> not.
R12. Damages suit, fact enabling amount to be determined -> relevant (s 12); not a damages suit -> not.
R13. Right or custom in question: transaction creating/claiming/recognising/denying -> relevant; particular instance of exercise or dispute -> relevant (s 13); question not about right/custom -> not.
R14. State of mind in issue/relevant; fact shows it in reference to particular matter -> relevant (s 14); general disposition only -> not (Expl 1; illus (n),(o),(p)).
R15. Previous offence relevant under s 14 -> previous conviction also relevant (s 14 Expl 2).
R16. Question whether act accidental/intentional; act part of series of similar occurrences by same person -> relevant (s 15); isolated act -> not.
R17. Question whether act was done; course of business under which it naturally would have been done -> relevant (s 16).

## Admissions ss 17-23, 31
A1. Statement suggesting inference as to a fact in issue/relevant fact, by a person within ss 18-20 -> admission (s 17(1)).
A2. Admission by a person accused of an offence suggesting that he committed that offence -> confession (s 17(2)); by non-accused -> not confession; by accused about something else -> not confession.
A3. Statement by party -> admission (s 18(1)); by authorised agent -> admission; by unauthorised agent -> not.
A4. Representative-capacity party: statement made while holding the character -> admission; before holding it -> not (s 18(2)).
A5. Person with proprietary/pecuniary interest, statement in that character during continuance of interest -> admission; after the interest ended -> not (s 18(3)(a)). Person from whom party derived interest, during continuance of his interest -> admission; after -> not (s 18(3)(b)).
A6. Person whose position/liability must be proved against a party; statement relevant against him, made while occupying the position -> admission (s 19); made after leaving the position -> not.
A7. Person to whom party expressly referred for information -> admission (s 20); no express reference -> not.
A8. Admission against its maker (or representative) -> relevant (s 21). By maker: not, unless (a) relevant under s 32 if maker dead, (b) state of mind/body statement at/about the time accompanied by conduct making falsehood improbable, (c) relevant otherwise than as an admission. Illus (a): A cannot prove A's own statement; B can prove A's.
A9. Oral admission as to contents of a document: not relevant unless the proposer is entitled to give secondary evidence or genuineness of a document produced is in question (s 22).
A10. Civil: admission made on express condition that evidence not be given -> not relevant; made on circumstances from which court infers agreement not to give evidence -> not relevant; ordinary -> relevant (s 23(1)). Criminal -> s 23(1) does not apply.
A11. s 23(2): advocate not exempted from giving evidence he may be compelled to give under s 128; s 128A for legal counsel.

## Hearsay ss 32-33 etc
H1. s 32(1)(a): statement by person as to cause of his death / circumstances of transaction resulting in death, where cause of death in question -> relevant whether or not under expectation of death.
H2. s 32(1)(b): statement made in ordinary course of trade, business, profession or occupation -> relevant; not in ordinary course -> not.
H3. s 32(1)(c): against pecuniary/proprietary interest of maker, or exposing maker to prosecution/suit for damages -> relevant; self-serving -> not.
H4. s 32(1)(d): opinion on public right/custom/matter of general interest, maker likely to be aware, made before controversy -> relevant; made after controversy -> not; maker not likely to be aware -> not.
H5. s 32(1)(e): relationship by blood/marriage/adoption, maker with special means of knowledge, before the question in dispute was raised -> relevant; after -> not; no special means -> not.
H6. s 32(1)(f): relationship between deceased persons, in will/deed relating to family affairs, pedigree, tombstone, portrait, before dispute -> relevant; after -> not.
H7. s 32(1)(g): statement in deed/document relating to transaction in s 13(a) -> relevant.
H8. s 32(1)(h): statement by a number of persons expressing feelings/impressions -> relevant; by one person -> not.
H9. s 32(1)(i): maker compellable for party wanting statement, attends but refuses to be sworn, or sworn but refuses to give evidence -> relevant; not compellable -> not.
H10. s 32(1)(j): maker (i) dead or unfit by bodily/mental condition; (ii) cannot be found despite reasonable efforts; (iii) outside Singapore, not practicable to secure attendance; (iv) competent but not compellable and refuses -> relevant; none -> not.
H11. s 32(1)(k): parties agree -> relevant.
H12. s 32(6): (k) agreement does not enable prosecution to give statement in criminal proceedings unless when agreement made the accused or a co-accused was represented by an advocate. Prosecution, no advocate -> not; prosecution, advocate -> yes; defence, no advocate -> yes; civil -> yes.
H13. s 32(7): (k) agreement made in committal hearing before examining Magistrate (old regime) is of no effect in High Court proceedings.
H14. s 32(3): not in interests of justice -> not relevant.
H15. s 32(4): except (k), party must comply with notice requirements; non-compliance -> not given; (k) exempt.
H16. s 32(2): oral statement put in writing at maker's request at the time/reasonably soon afterwards -> document treated as maker's statement for paragraphs (a),(c),(d),(e),(f),(g),(h),(i),(j) but not (b) or (k); not at maker's request or not soon afterwards -> no.
H17. s 32A: protest/greeting/utterance may be treated as stating the fact it implies.
H18. s 32B(2): opinion statement admissible under s 32(1) only if admissible as direct oral evidence. s 32B(3): witness's opinion conveying facts personally perceived -> admissible.
H19. s 32C: credibility evidence of the hearsay maker admissible; (1)(b) with permission of court.
H20. s 33: prior testimony relevant if witness dead / cannot be found / incapable / kept out of way by adverse party / presence unobtainable without unreasonable delay or expense, AND same parties or representatives, AND adverse party had right and opportunity to cross-examine, AND substantially same questions. Failing any -> not.
H21. s 34: books of account regularly kept -> relevant; but not alone sufficient to charge a person with liability.
H22. s 37: entry in public record made by public officer in discharge of official duty (or other person in a duty specially enjoined by law) -> relevant; not in discharge of duty -> not.
H23. s 38: published map/chart generally offered for public sale, or map/plan made under Government authority as to matters usually represented -> relevant; otherwise not.
H24. s 39: recital in Act/Ordinance/Gazette etc as to fact of public nature, when court has to form opinion on a fact of public nature -> relevant; not a public-nature fact -> not.
H25. s 40: statement of law in government-authorised law book / report of rulings, when court has to form an opinion as to a foreign law -> relevant.

## Judgments ss 42-46
J1. s 42: judgment barring suit/trial -> relevant when question is whether court should take cognizance.
J2. s 43: final judgment of competent court in probate, matrimonial, admiralty, bankruptcy jurisdiction conferring/taking away legal character or declaring entitlement absolutely (not against a specified person) -> relevant; conclusive proof. Other jurisdiction, or not final, or only against a specified person -> not under s 43.
J3. s 44: other judgment relating to a matter of public nature relevant to the inquiry -> relevant, not conclusive.
J4. s 45: other judgments irrelevant unless existence is fact in issue or relevant under another provision.
J5. s 45A: Singapore conviction/acquittal admissible where relevant to an issue; not if appeal/review undetermined, quashed/set aside, or pardoned; foreign conviction -> not under s 45A.
J6. s 46: party may show incompetence/fraud/collusion for judgments relevant under 42, 43, 44; not for s 45 judgments.

## Opinion ss 47-53
O1. s 47: expert opinion on specialised knowledge point where court likely to derive assistance -> relevant; not expert / no assistance -> not; matter of common knowledge does not make irrelevant; not in interests of justice -> not.
O2. s 48: facts supporting/inconsistent with relevant expert opinions -> relevant; if expert opinion not relevant -> not.
O3. s 49: opinion of person acquainted with handwriting (seen him write / answered correspondence / documents habitually submitted) -> relevant; not acquainted -> not.
O4. s 50: opinion on general custom/right by persons likely to know -> relevant; unlikely to know -> not.
O5. s 51: opinion on usages/tenets, foundations, local meanings by persons with special means of knowledge -> relevant; no special means -> not.
O6. s 52: opinion expressed BY CONDUCT as to relationship by person with special means -> relevant; opinion expressed in words only -> not; s 52(2) not sufficient to prove a marriage in s 6A Women's Charter prosecutions.
O7. s 53: grounds of a relevant opinion of a living person -> relevant; of a dead person -> not.

## Character ss 54-57
C1. s 54: civil, character to show conduct probable -> irrelevant except insofar as it appears from otherwise relevant facts.
C2. s 55: criminal, accused's good character -> relevant. Civil -> not.
C3. s 56: accused asks questions to establish good character / gives such evidence / calls witness -> prosecution or co-accused may establish bad disposition/reputation (including previous convictions). Without trigger -> not. s 56(4): s 122(4) not apply to that cross-examination.
C4. s 57: civil, character affecting damages -> relevant; criminal -> not under s 57. Explanation: only general reputation/disposition, not particular acts.

## Part 2 Proof
P1. ss 58-59: judicially noticeable facts need not be proved: laws in force in Singapore; public holidays only if notified in Gazette (else not); appointment of public officers only if notified in the Gazette (else not); meaning of English words; private fact -> not noticeable.
P2. s 60: facts admitted by parties/agents at hearing, by writing before hearing, or by pleadings need not be proved; court may in discretion require proof anyway.
P3. s 61: all facts except contents of documents may be proved orally; contents of document -> not by oral evidence.
P4. s 62(1): oral evidence must be direct: seen -> witness who saw; heard -> heard; opinion -> holder. Hearsay oral -> not. s 62(2): treatise: author dead/cannot be found/incapable/unreasonable delay -> by treatise; commonly offered for sale.
P5. s 62A: live link with permission in non-criminal, non-contempt proceedings if witness under 18, or agreed, or outside Singapore, or expedient. Criminal -> no. Contempt -> no. No permission -> no. 18-year-old in Singapore, no agreement, not expedient -> no.
P6. ss 63-64: primary evidence is the document itself; each part of a document executed in parts is primary; counterpart is primary against parties executing it; uniform-process copies (printing) each primary of the rest but not of the original; electronic copy shown accurately to reflect the document is primary (Expl 3).
P7. s 65: secondary evidence: certified copies; copies by technical process ensuring accuracy (and copies compared with them); copies made from or compared with original; counterparts against non-executing parties; oral account by one who has himself seen it. Illus (d): copy transcribed from a copy afterwards compared with original IS secondary; not compared -> NOT. Illus (e): oral account of a copy compared with the original, or of a photograph/machine copy -> NOT secondary. Oral account by someone who never saw the original -> not.
P8. s 66: primary evidence required except s 67 cases.
P9. s 67(1): secondary evidence in cases: (a) original with adverse person / person out of reach / person legally bound AND notice given and not produced; (b) existence/condition/contents admitted in writing by person against whom proved; (c) destroyed or lost, or cannot be produced in reasonable time for a reason not arising from own default or neglect; (d) not easily movable; (e) public document under s 76; (f) certified copy permitted by law; (g) numerous accounts not conveniently examined and fact is general result. None -> no. Own default -> (c) fails. (a) without notice -> no.
P10. s 67(2): in (a),(c),(d) any secondary evidence; (3) in (b) the written admission; (4) in (e),(f) certified copy only, no other kind; (5) in (g) general result by skilled person.
P11. s 67A: s 32(1) statement in a document may be proved by the document or an authenticated copy/material part even if original no longer exists.
P12. s 68(1): notice to produce required in 67(1)(a) cases. (2) not required when: document is itself a notice; adverse party must know; adverse party obtained original by fraud or force; adverse party or agent has original in court; admitted loss; person in possession out of reach/not subject to process; or court dispenses.
P13. s 68A: charts/summaries if likely to aid comprehension of other admissible evidence AND evidence so voluminous or complex that court considers it convenient; either missing -> no.
P14. s 69: signature/handwriting alleged must be proved; not applicable to electronic record/signature to which the Electronic Transactions Act 2010 applies.
P15. s 70: document required by law to be attested not used until one attesting witness at least called, if there is one alive, subject to process and capable of giving evidence; if none such -> not needed. s 71: none can be found or executed in UK -> prove attestor's handwriting and executant's signature. s 72: party's admission of execution sufficient against him. s 73: attesting witness denies/does not recollect -> other evidence. s 74: attested doc not required by law to be attested -> as if unattested.
P16. s 75: comparison with admitted/proved signature, writing, seal allowed, also finger impressions.
P17. ss 76-77: public documents: acts/records of acts of sovereign authority, official bodies and tribunals, public officers; public records kept in Singapore of private documents. Others private. A private contract is private.
P18. ss 78-79: certified copy: public officer with custody; document one person has right to inspect; certificate that true copy, dated and subscribed with officer's name and official title, sealed where officer authorised to use a seal. Missing date, or missing name/title, or missing seal when officer has a seal -> not a certified copy. Certified copy may be produced in proof of contents.
P19. s 80(2): Commonwealth Acts purporting to be printed by Government Printer received without proof of printing. s 80(1) listed methods.
P20. s 80A: print from film of document in possession of Government/specified statutory body (Second Schedule): needs (a) film taken to keep a permanent record while in custody/control AND (b) document subsequently destroyed, damaged to indecipherability, lost, or out of custody. Body not in Second Schedule -> no. Missing (a) or (b) -> no.
P21. s 81: court is to presume genuine a certificate/certified copy purporting to be duly certified by a Singapore public officer or authorised Malaysian officer, substantially in the form and executed as directed by law; and presume the officer's claimed official character. Officer elsewhere -> no. Not in the required form -> no.
P22. s 82: document purporting to be record of evidence / statement / confession signed by Judge/Magistrate/authorised officer -> presume genuine, circumstances true, duly taken. Not so signed -> no.
P23. s 83: presume genuine a Gazette, newspaper, journal, or document directed by law to be kept, if kept substantially in the required form and produced from proper custody.
P24. s 84: document admissible in England/NI without proof of seal/signature -> presume seal genuine and signer's character; admissible for same purpose.
P25. s 85: maps/plans by Government authority -> presume so made and accurate; (2) maps made for the purposes of a proceeding must be proved accurate.
P26. s 86: presume genuine publication purporting to be printed under authority of a government and containing its law, or an official law report.
P27. s 87: power of attorney executed before and authenticated by notary public/court/Judge/Magistrate/consular officer of Singapore -> presume so executed; other authenticator -> no.
P28. s 88: court MAY presume certified copy of foreign (non-Commonwealth) judicial record genuine/accurate if certified in the manner certified commonly in use.
P29. s 89: MAY presume book/map/chart written and published as purports. s 90: MAY presume telegraph message corresponds, but no presumption as to who delivered it.
P30. s 91: court is to presume document called for and not produced after s 68 notice was attested, stamped and executed as required by law; no notice -> no.
P31. s 92: document purporting or proved to be 30 years old from proper custody -> court MAY presume handwriting/execution/attestation. 29 years -> no. Improper custody -> no.
P32. s 93: terms of contract/grant/disposition reduced to writing, or required by law to be in writing -> only the document or admissible secondary evidence. Exc 1: public officer appointment writing need not be proved when he acted as such. Exc 2: wills admitted to probate may be proved by the probate. Expl 3 (illus (d),(e)): other facts stated in the document may be proved orally.
P33. s 94: once terms proved, no oral agreement/statement between parties/representatives to contradict, vary, add to, subtract. Provisos: (a) fact invalidating (fraud, etc.); (b) separate oral agreement on matter on which doc silent and not inconsistent, regard to formality; (c) condition precedent; (d) distinct subsequent oral agreement rescinding/modifying, except where law requires writing or registered; (e) usage/custom unless repugnant; (f) fact showing relation of language to existing facts. Stranger to the document -> s 101.
P34. s 95 ambiguous on its face -> no evidence to supply meaning. s 96 plain and accurate -> no evidence that not meant. s 97 plain but meaningless -> evidence of peculiar sense allowed. s 98 language applying to one of several -> evidence allowed. s 99 partly one set partly another -> allowed. s 100 illegible/technical etc -> allowed. s 101 non-parties may give evidence of varying agreement. s 102 wills unaffected.

## Part 3 Burden and presumptions
B1. s 103 asserter of facts needed for his judgment bears burden. s 104 burden on person who would fail if no evidence on either side (illus (a): claimant; (b): bond admitted, fraud alleged -> B). s 105 particular fact: on person who wishes court to believe it unless law provides otherwise.
B2. s 106: burden of proving fact needed to enable giving evidence of another fact is on the person wishing to give that evidence (death for dying declaration; loss for secondary evidence).
B3. s 107: accused: burden of proving circumstances within general exception / special exception / proviso is on the accused, and court is to presume the absence of such circumstances. Ordinary element -> on prosecution.
B4. s 108: fact especially within a person's knowledge -> burden on him (ticket).
B5. s 109: shown alive within 30 years -> burden of proving dead on affirmer of death. More than 30 years since last alive -> not engaged.
B6. s 110: not heard of for 7 years by those who would naturally have heard -> burden of proving alive shifts to affirmer. 7 years -> shifted; under 7 -> not; not heard of by those who would naturally hear -> not.
B7. s 111: partners / landlord and tenant / principal and agent shown to have acted as such -> burden of proving not/ceased on affirmer. Other relationship -> no. Not shown to have acted -> no.
B8. s 112: possession -> burden of proving not owner on person who says so. No possession -> no.
B9. s 113: transaction between parties one in active confidence -> burden of proving good faith on the party in position of active confidence.
B10. s 114: born during valid marriage, or within 280 days after dissolution with mother unmarried -> presumed legitimate child of that man unless contrary proved. 280 days yes; 281 no; mother remarried no; ART Act determination -> no.
B11. s 116: court MAY presume; never must.
B12. s 116A(1): device/process of kind that if properly used ordinarily produces/accurately communicates the record -> presume it did, unless evidence sufficient to raise doubt. (2) generated in usual course of business by non-party not under control of the proponent -> presume authentic; in criminal proceedings, investigating police officer and accomplice count as parties. (3) record generated by adverse party -> presume authentic. (6) approved imaging process -> presume accurate reproduction.
B13. s 122(1)-(3): civil parties and spouses competent; spouse of accused competent; accused competent for himself or co-accused but not compellable.
B14. s 122(4): accused gives evidence: must not in cross-exam be asked, and need not answer, a question tending to reveal (a) other offence committed/charged/convicted/acquitted or (b) bad disposition/reputation. Accused not testifying -> not engaged. Question about offence charged -> not within (4).
B15. s 122(5): (4) not apply if evidence of the fact is admissible under s 14 or 15 or CPC 265/266 or other written law to prove the offence charged. (6): jointly charged: fact admissible to show another accused not guilty. (7): accused asked prosecution/co-accused witness a question about witness's conduct/offences AND main purpose to raise credibility -> (4) lifted, but court not to permit a question under (4) unless relevant to accused's credibility. Other purpose -> not lifted. (8): accused gave evidence against a jointly charged person -> (4) not apply. s 56(4) also.

## Privilege ss 120-135
W1. s 120: all competent unless court considers prevented from understanding questions/giving rational answers by tender years, extreme old age, disease or similar. Lunatic not incompetent unless so prevented.
W2. s 121: witness unable to speak may write/sign in open court; not in open court -> not; deemed oral.
W3. s 123: Judge cannot be compelled as to own conduct in court / what came to knowledge in court; Magistrate except on special order of the General Division of the High Court; may be examined on other matters occurring in his presence.
W4. s 124(1): present or former spouse cannot be compelled to disclose, nor permitted to disclose without consent of maker/representative, a communication made during marriage; exceptions: suits between married persons; proceedings in which one is prosecuted for crime against the other. 124(2): no protection where communication relevant in criminal proceedings for a specified offence (child abuse offence against under 16; sexual or Chapter 16 offence against under 16, against domestic worker by employer/household member/agent, against vulnerable person by carer; abetment/conspiracy/attempt). Victim 16 or over and not otherwise within list -> protected. Communication made before marriage -> not within s 124.
W5. s 125: unpublished official records of State affairs not to be produced without permission of head of Department; published -> no privilege.
W6. s 126: public officer not compelled to disclose official-confidence communication when he considers public interest would suffer; if he does not consider so -> may be compelled. Same for Official Secrets Act organisation members.
W7. s 127: Magistrate/police officer not compelled to say where he got information of commission of an offence; revenue officer for revenue/excise offences. Revenue officer for other offence -> not protected.
W8. s 128(1): advocate/solicitor not permitted, without client's express consent, to disclose communication made in course and for the purpose of employment, contents of documents, advice. (2): not protected: communication in furtherance of illegal purpose; fact observed showing crime or fraud committed since commencement of employment. Illus (a) past forgery -> protected; (b) future forged deed -> not; (c) observed fraud since commencement -> not. After employment ended -> still. Implied consent only -> not enough. Communication not for purpose of employment -> not protected.
W9. s 128A: in-house legal counsel: protected, with entity's express consent; not protected: illegal purpose; fact observed showing crime/fraud; communication not for the purpose of seeking legal advice; document acquainted with otherwise than in course and for the purpose of seeking legal advice.
W10. s 129: ss 128/128A apply to interpreters and persons working under supervision.
W11. s 130: party giving evidence doesn't waive; party calling advocate as witness deemed to consent only if he questions him on matters which but for the question he would not be at liberty to disclose.
W12. s 131: no one compelled to disclose confidential communication with legal adviser unless he offers himself as witness, then only what court thinks necessary to explain his evidence.
W13. s 132(1): non-party witness not compelled to produce title deeds, pledgee/mortgagee documents, or documents tending to criminate him, unless agreed in writing. (2) party witness not bound to produce document not relevant or material to the case of the party requiring. (3) bank not compelled except s 174. s 133: not compelled to produce documents another could refuse to produce, except for identification, unless that other consents.
W14. s 134(1): witness not excused from relevant question on the ground it will criminate. (2) compelled answer cannot subject him to arrest/prosecution or be proved against him in criminal proceeding, except prosecution for false evidence by that answer. (4) accused giving evidence: cannot refuse on ground answer would prove the offence charged; and except questions solely or mainly relevant to credibility (not s 56 questions) cannot refuse on ground of exposure to other offence/penalty or exposing spouse. (5) spouse of accused likewise.
W15. s 135: accomplice competent witness; corroboration warning rule abrogated. s 136: no particular number of witnesses required.

## Examination ss 137-169
X1. s 138: court admits if fact, if proved, would be relevant; evidence admissible only upon proof of another fact -> that fact first unless undertaking accepted.
X2. s 140: cross-examination need not be confined to chief; re-examination directed to matters in cross; new matter in re-exam only with permission.
X3. s 141: person called to produce is not witness by producing; no cross-exam unless called as witness. s 142: character witnesses may be cross-examined and re-examined.
X4. ss 143-145: leading question suggests answer or disputed facts. Chief/re-exam: not if objected, except with permission; permitted without objection; permitted if introductory/undisputed/already proved. Cross: allowed unless putting very words or assuming unproved facts; court may prohibit if witness shows strong interest/bias in favour of cross-examiner.
X5. s 146: adverse party may object to oral evidence of contents of a document that ought to be produced until produced or secondary evidence entitled; Expl: oral evidence of others' statements about contents allowed if those statements are relevant.
X6. s 147: cross-examine on previous written statement without showing it; to contradict, attention must first be called to the parts; oral inconsistent statement: proof only after circumstances mentioned and witness asked, and only if he does not distinctly admit it; proved inconsistent statement is admissible as evidence of facts stated; such statement cannot corroborate its maker (147(7)).
X7. ss 148-154: cross questions to test accuracy/veracity/credibility, identity, shake credit by injuring character (even if it tends to criminate). Irrelevant-to-issue questions: proper if truth would seriously affect credibility; improper if remote in time/character that slight effect, or great disproportion; may infer unfavourable from refusal. s 151 reasonable grounds required (illus (a),(b),(d) yes; (c) random -> no). s 153 court may forbid indecent/scandalous questions unless they relate to facts in issue or matters necessary to determine them. s 154 court must forbid questions intended to insult/annoy or needlessly offensive.
X8. s 154A: only where accused charged with sexual offence or child abuse offence; then victim cross-examination/evidence subject to rules. Other offence -> not.
X9. s 155: answer to credit-only question cannot be contradicted. Exc 1: denies previous conviction -> may prove conviction. Exc 2: denies facts impeaching impartiality -> may be contradicted. If relevant to the issue -> contradiction allowed.
X10. s 156: court may permit questions to own witness. s 157: credit may be impeached by adverse party, or by party calling with court consent; (a) opinion of unworthiness; (b) bribery; (c) former inconsistent statements. Calling party without consent -> not.
X11. s 158: questioning on circumstances at/near time and place if court opines corroborative; s 159: former statement relating to same fact at or about the time it took place, or before an authority competent to investigate, may be proved to corroborate; long afterward and not before authority -> not.
X12. s 160: whenever s 32/33 statement proved, matters may be proved as if maker called and denied; s 160A: s 32(1) statement cannot corroborate its maker's evidence; s 32(1)(b) statement cannot corroborate original supplier's evidence.
X13. s 161: refresh memory from writing made by himself at the time or so soon afterwards that fresh; other person's writing read by witness within that time if known correct at that time; later not fresh -> no. Copy with permission if sufficient reason for non-production. Expert may use treatises. s 162: may testify to facts in such document though no recollection if sure correctly recorded. s 163: produce to adverse party if required.
X14. s 164: witness summoned to produce must bring document despite objection. s 165: party who calls for and inspects a document after notice bound to give it as evidence if the producer requires and it is relevant. s 166: party who refused production after notice cannot afterwards use it without consent of other party or order of court.
X15. s 167(3): Judge cannot compel what witness could refuse under ss 123-133; (4) Judge must not ask question improper under ss 150/151 or dispense with primary evidence except as excepted.
X16. s 169: improper admission/rejection not a ground for new trial/reversal if independently sufficient evidence to justify decision, or rejected evidence would not have varied decision; neither -> ground.

## Part 4 Bankers' books
K1. s 170: "bank" = company carrying on business of bankers in Singapore under a licence; unlicensed or not carrying on in Singapore -> not. Books used in ordinary business of the bank. "court" = General Division of the High Court. "legal proceeding" includes civil, criminal, inquiry and arbitration.
K2. ss 171-173: copy of entry received as prima facie evidence only if proved (a) ordinary book of bank at time of entry, (b) entry made in usual and ordinary course of business, (c) book in custody or control of the bank, AND (173) copy examined with original and correct. Any missing -> not received. Proof of 172 by officer of bank orally or affidavit; of 173 by person who examined, orally or affidavit.
K3. s 174: bank officer not compellable (bank not a party) to produce book or appear, unless judge orders for special cause. Bank is a party -> compellable. Order for special cause -> compellable.
K4. s 175: order for inspection on application of party; may be made without summoning bank; must be served on bank 3 clear days before it is to be obeyed unless court/Judge directs otherwise. 2 clear days without direction -> not enforceable yet.
K5. s 176: costs in discretion; may be ordered against bank where caused by its fault or delay; order enforced as if bank were a party.
