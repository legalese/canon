# Hostage-Taking Act 2010 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, with amendments to Act 17
of 2022 (in force 1 July 2022) shown on s 14.

**Checks:** one case file, 98 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**10 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. The Act has 14 sections, so
almost all of it is encoded: the offence and its punishment limbs (ss 2, 3),
extraterritoriality (s 4), the duty to disclose (s 5), the Public Prosecutor's
freeze, inspection and information orders and their offences (ss 6 to 8), consent to
prosecute (s 9), informers (s 10), officers' liability (s 11), arrest (s 12) and the
extradition deeming (s 14(1)). Not encoded: s 13 and s 14(2) to (8) (mutual
assistance and the Gazette notifications for Convention countries) beyond the flat
deemings, the Banking Act meaning of "customer information", and s 11(7) rules.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Who is to be compelled decides between a mandatory death-or-life sentence and 15 years

s 3(1)(c): hostage-taking to compel "the Government, the government of another State
or an international intergovernmental organisation" "shall be punished with death or
imprisonment for life". s 3(1)(d): the same conduct to compel "any other person" —
a family, a company — carries imprisonment up to 15 years. Both add fine or caning.
Where a captor presses a government and a private person at once, the text does not
say which limb applies; this encoding takes limb (c) — **an inference**. Asserted.

### 2. Everyone in Singapore must tell the police immediately, with no excuse written in

s 5(1): "every person in Singapore" who knows or believes their information may be of
material assistance in preventing a hostage-taking offence, or in securing an
offender's apprehension, prosecution or conviction, commits an offence by failing to
disclose it "immediately" to a police officer — up to $50,000 or 5 years or both. Unlike
s 7(3), no "reasonable excuse" defence appears. Good-faith disclosure is protected
against proceedings and against confidentiality duties of "law, contract or rules of
professional conduct" (s 5(2), (3)). Asserted.

### 3. Hostage-taking is triable here wherever and by whomever committed

s 4 deems "every person" who commits the act outside Singapore to have committed it in
Singapore. No nationality or residence condition is stated. Abetment, conspiracy and
attempt are all "hostage-taking offences" (s 2) and every one is deemed an extradition
offence (s 14(1)). Asserted.

### 4. The financial powers reach family, but only spouse and children

The Public Prosecutor's inspection order (s 7(1)) may rest on customer information about
the suspect, the "spouse, son or daughter", or a person believed to be a trustee or
agent; s 8(1)(a), (d), (f) reach the same family members. Parents and siblings are not
named. Only an officer "of or above the rank of assistant superintendent" may be
authorised. Asserted.

### 5. Secrecy yields to a s 8 order, except for national-security certificates on government papers

s 8(2) requires compliance "despite any obligation of secrecy". The only escape is
s 8(3): a ministry, department or statutory board head is excused if the Minister
certifies national security. The Comptroller of Income Tax and banks have no such
escape. Non-compliance is an offence with no reasonable-excuse defence written in
(s 8(5)). Asserted.

### 6. Informer protection lifts on different grounds in different proceedings

s 10(3): in proceedings for an offence under this Act the court may order disclosure
only if the informer wilfully made a false material statement; in any other proceedings,
only if justice cannot be fully done without it. The two grounds do not cross over.
Asserted.

### 7. Smaller points

A bank freeze lasts "not exceeding one month", and a complying bank is relieved of
liability (s 6). A non-complying bank faces a fine of up to $20,000 and nothing else.
All prosecutions need the Public Prosecutor's consent (s 9). Any offence under the Act,
including the failure to disclose, permits arrest without warrant (s 12). For a limited
liability partnership the officer's liability ground is "neglect", not "act or
default" (s 11(4)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- No case law on s 3 or s 5 was searched, nor how "immediately" has been read.
- The reading of "one month" in s 6(1) as a calendar month is an inference.
- The Convention itself, and the Geneva Conventions Act 1973 carve-out in s 3(2), were
  not read.
- ss 13 and 14(2) to (8) are left to the Extradition Act 1968 and the Mutual Assistance
  in Criminal Matters Act 2000, which were not read.
