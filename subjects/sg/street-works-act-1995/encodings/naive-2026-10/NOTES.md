**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, with amendments to Act 42

**Checks:** one case file, 102 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**11 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
landowner, builder, contractor or shopkeeper meets: stopping activity that damages
a public street (s 8), engineering works near road structures (s 8A), the
Authority's entry on private land and the owner's right to require acquisition
(ss 9, 10A(5), 11, 13(3)), works on public streets (s 17), obstructing streets and
footways (s 32A), damaging public streets (s 33), mandatory orders and appeals
(ss 35, 36), and the penalty, recovery and composition rules (ss 39, 40(2), 49, 52,
53(4)). The Authority's construction powers, private streets and deposits,
footways, utilities, backlanes and the recovery machinery are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Working on a public street without approval is not itself an offence; ignoring the order is, at $100,000

s 17(1) says a person "must not carry out any works" on a public street, public
bridge or street to be declared public without the Authority's prior approval, but
no subsection declares breach of (1) an offence. The criminal sanction attaches to
failing to comply with a s 17(4) order (cessation, removal, reinstatement or
alteration): a fine up to $100,000 plus $1,000 a day after conviction (s 17(8)).
Whether s 49's $2,000 general penalty reaches s 17(1) depends on whether breach of
(1) is "an offence" at all, which the text read does not say. Asserted (the approval
rule and the order penalty).

### 2. Damaging a public street is criminal only if it blocks use; paying for it is not conditional on anything

s 33(1) needs fault (intentional, reckless or negligent), no prior written approval,
and damage that "prevents or substantially prevents" passage or use. Intentional
damage carries up to $100,000; reckless or negligent damage up to $50,000; both up to
6 months. But s 33(3) lets the Authority recover "the full amount of the damage
caused" from anyone whose conduct caused it, "whether or not" proceedings are
brought, with no fault or blocking element stated. Asserted.

### 3. The 20-metre road structure safety zone

s 8A: no "engineering works" (basement excavation, piling, bore holes, ground
anchors, operating a crane or heavy equipment) within 20 metres of the outermost edge
of any road structure without prior approval. Fine up to $5,000 plus $100 a day after
conviction; no imprisonment. Reading "within ... 20 metres" as inclusive of 20 is an
inference. Asserted.

### 4. Five-footway obstruction: warn first, then remove, sell, and keep the surplus after 12 months

s 32A covers public streets, five-footways and private footways (not a public path
under the Active Mobility Act 2017 that is not on State land, s 32A(16)). The officer
must take reasonable steps to inform the owner, the person responsible or the
frontager and allow them to move it before removing it (s 32A(4)). Refusing without
reasonable excuse is a separate offence. Both carry $2,000 plus $100 a day. Sale
proceeds pay removal and storage costs first, then "all charges and fines"; any
surplus unclaimed within 12 months of the sale is forfeited to the Authority
(s 32A(11)). Asserted.

### 5. A long tenant counts as an owner who can force acquisition

s 11 lets an owner whose land was temporarily possessed under s 9 require the
Government to acquire it if the owner considers its rights substantially impaired.
"Owner" includes a mortgagee entitled to sell and "a person in occupation of the land
under a tenancy the term of which exceeds 7 years"; a 7-year tenant is not one. With
several owners, all must join; the notice is irrevocable. Entry under s 9 needs at
least 2 months' notice to every owner and occupier. Asserted.

### 6. Composition is capped at the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000

s 52(1)(a), for offences prescribed as compoundable. A $2,000 offence compounds for
at most $1,000; a $100,000 offence for at most $5,000. Which offences are prescribed
was not checked. Asserted.

### 7. A pending appeal suspends a mandatory order's daily fine

s 35(2)(a): $500 a day for failing to comply with a mandatory order, unless the
person proves due diligence. s 36(1): an appeal to the Minister within 14 days means
no fine liability "until after the determination or abandonment of the appeal".
Reading "until after" as governing the fine (and not only proceedings and work) is an
inference from the sentence. Asserted.
