## Why this Act, and why scoped

**13 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes reportable
deaths and the duty to report, when an inquiry is mandatory, and what an inquiry may
find: ss 5, 6, 25, 27 and the Second and Third Schedules. Appointments,
investigation, post-mortems, release of bodies, inquiry procedure and deaths outside
Singapore are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. An execution is not reportable, yet an inquiry into it is mandatory

The Second Schedule (item 5) excludes "death as a result of the lawful execution of a
death sentence" from reportable deaths, and s 6(2) excuses the custodian's report.
But s 25(1)(b) makes an inquiry into an execution **mandatory**. Asserted.

### 2. Reportable is not the same as inquired-into

A death after medical treatment, a suicide, a road accident or a death on public
transport is reportable, but none is in the Third Schedule, so an inquiry is at the
Coroner's discretion (s 25(2)). Workplace accidents, deaths in law-enforcement
operations, unknown causes, suspected homicides and unidentified bodies require an
inquiry unless the Coroner is satisfied the death was natural. Items 2 and 6 of the
Third Schedule were deleted by S 255/2023. Asserted.

### 3. Everyone has a duty to report, and the excuse is theirs to prove

s 5: "any person" aware of an apparently reportable death must report it to the
police. A reasonable excuse is a defence, but "the burden of proving which is on the
accused". The maximum is $1,500 or one month. A custodian must report a death in
custody within 24 hours (s 6). Asserted.

### 4. The Coroner finds facts, not fault

s 27(2): a finding must not "determine any question of criminal, civil or disciplinary
liability". The Coroner may still make recommendations and is not inhibited by the
likelihood that liability will be inferred. Asserted.
