## Why this Act, and why scoped

This row answers **REQ-0073** in `subjects/sg/requirements.jsonl`: Tier 2 of the
remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what
the Act decides for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper
question yet.

The row takes what a jeweller, gold dealer or watch seller meets: what counts as a
precious metal, stone or product and who is a regulated dealer (s 2, the Schedule); the
duty to register (s 6); refusal, lapse, regulatory action and appeal (ss 8, 9A, 10, 11,
13); the $20,000 cash threshold and the duties it triggers (ss 15, 16, 17); and the
maximum penalties and composition (ss 6, 16-22, 27, 28, 31). Not encoded: appointments,
conditions of registration, recovery of penalties, the content of the prescribed
due-diligence, record-keeping, programme and sanctions measures (the regulations were
not retrieved), directions beyond their penalty, investigation and seizure powers, and
the general provisions (ss 29-40).

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Cash is what matters, and $20,000 exactly is not enough

A "designated transaction" (s 15) is a sale for which "cash or a cash equivalent
exceeding the threshold amount" is received, and the threshold is "$20,000 or its
equivalent in value". A $20,000 cash sale is not designated; $20,001 is. "Cash" is
"currency notes and coins"; that card or bank payments fall outside is an inference
from that definition. Asserted.

### 2. Same-day cash sales are added up; cash purchases are not

Under s 15(b), 2 or more sales "in a single day" to the same customer (or to customers
known to act for the same person) count if the cash "in total" exceeds the threshold:
two $12,000 cash sales on one day are designated. The purchase limb (c) has no such
aggregation, and catches only a dealer who is a secondhand goods dealer buying from a
customer who is not a regulated dealer. Asserted.

### 3. Designation drives due diligence, reporting and refusal

Due diligence must be done before a designated transaction, on suspicion, or on doubt
about earlier due diligence (s 16(1)). If it cannot be completed the dealer must decline
or terminate and decide whether to file a suspicious transaction disclosure (s 16(5)).
The one licence to skip it is suspicion plus a belief that it "will tip off the
customer", and only where no prescribed s 16(3) bar applies. Each designated
transaction requires a cash transaction report (s 17(1)). Asserted.

### 4. The heaviest fines carry no prison; the reporting duty does

Failing to do due diligence (s 16(6)), keep records (s 18(5)), run an adequate
programme (s 19(3)) or apply sanctions measures (s 20(2)) is a fine up to $100,000 and
nothing more. Failing to file a cash transaction report is only $20,000, but with up to
2 years' imprisonment (s 17(4)). Dealing unregistered is $75,000 or 3 years, plus $7,500
a day after conviction (s 6(2)). The Registrar may compound a prescribed offence for up
to half the maximum fine (s 31). Asserted.

### 5. "Natural or otherwise", and a 2% alloy is gold enough

Part 2 of the Schedule lists diamond, sapphire, ruby, emerald, jade and pearl,
"(NATURAL OR OTHERWISE)"; reading that to take in laboratory-grown stones is an
inference. An alloy is a precious metal at "at least 2% in weight" of the listed metals.
A finished product is a precious product if at least 50% of its value comes from the
stone or metal, or it is priced above a prescribed value the Act does not state.
Pawnbrokers are excluded from "regulated dealer". Asserted.

### 6. Regulatory action bites at once

The Registrar may suspend for up to 6 months, cancel, or impose a financial penalty up
to $100,000 for a contravention that is not an offence or a breach of condition (s 10).
Representations get at least 14 days unless that is not practicable or desirable
(s 11(1)). The decision takes effect despite an appeal to the Minister (ss 11(4),
13(2)), and the Minister's decision is final (s 13(4)). Registration lapses when an
entity is wound up or dissolved, or a sole proprietor dies (s 9A). Asserted.
