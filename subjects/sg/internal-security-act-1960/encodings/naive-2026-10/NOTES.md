# Internal Security Act 1960 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
conventions and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, with amendments to Act 3
of 2021 (in force 1 July 2025) and Act 26 of 2022 (s 79 repealed, in force 12 June
2023) shown.

**Checks:** one case file, 113 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**11 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the offences
an ordinary person can commit under Part 2 and their penalties (ss 4-7, 22-27, 45),
the firearm offence in a security area (s 58), what bail, consent and the District
Court's powers make of them (ss 5(2), 58(4), 73, 80, 81), and the time limits and
safeguards that bind preventive detention (ss 8, 8B, 11, 12, 13, 13A). The
Minister's and President's discretions themselves, search and seizure,
entertainments, schools, the other security-area powers, s 74 detention of
suspects, lethal force, evidence and the Schedules are not encoded.

The deposit's arrangement of sections is out of step with the body in Part 2
Chapter 6: the arrangement lists s 44 as Arrest and s 45 as Enforcement of bonds,
but the body has s 44 attempts, s 45 abetment and general penalty, s 46 arrest and
s 47 bonds. This row follows the body.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Spreading a false report is an offence with no stated penalty, so the general $1,000 penalty applies

s 26 makes it an offence to spread "false reports" or make "false statements likely
to cause public alarm" by any means, and stops there. So do s 25 (inciting placards)
and s 7(2) (emblem and flag orders). s 45 supplies the penalty for any contravention
"for which no special penalty is provided": $1,000 or one year. Asserted.

### 2. A firearm in a security area carries death, and a charge excludes bail

s 58(1): carrying a firearm, ammunition or explosive in a security area without
lawful authority and without lawful excuse, "the onus of proving which shall be on
such person", is punishable "with death". s 58(4): no bail. Lawful authority (on-duty
police, security forces or prisons staff, a licence, an exemption) is lost if the
weapon is held for a purpose prejudicial to public security. A District Court cannot
try it (s 80). Asserted.

### 3. Detention is reviewed only for procedure

s 8B(2): no judicial review of any act or decision of the President or the Minister
under the Act "save in regard to any question relating to compliance with any
procedural requirement". The procedural requirements this row encodes: orders of at
most 2 years, extendable by further periods of at most 2 years "at a time" with no
stated cap on how many (s 8); a statement of grounds within 14 days of service
(s 11(2)); the advisory board's recommendation within 3 months of detention
(s 12(1)); a first review within 12 months, or 15 months where no representations
were made (s 13(1)); and, if the board recommends release, no further detention
without the President's concurrence (s 13A). Asserted.

### 4. Some maximum penalties exceed what a District Court can impose

s 80 lets a District Court try any non-capital offence but caps its sentence at
5 years or $5,000. The $10,000 fines for organising a quasi-military association
(s 5), training others to drill (s 6(2)) and possessing a subversive document
(s 27) are above that cap. Asserted.

### 5. Bail and consent follow the length of the sentence, with one exception

s 73(2) makes offences carrying more than 3 years non-bailable: in scope, the
5-year offences in ss 5, 6(2) and 27. s 81 requires the Public Prosecutor's consent
for offences carrying 7 years or more, which none of the Part 2 offences here does.
But s 5(2) requires consent for every s 5 prosecution, including the one-year
offence of mere membership. Asserted.

### 6. For repeat possession or import of a banned publication, the text states imprisonment only

s 23(1) and s 24 give a fine or imprisonment for a first offence but "imprisonment for
a term not exceeding 2 [or 3] years" for a subsequent one. The encoding treats that as
no fine available. That is an inference from the wording. Asserted.

### 7. The reverse burdens are heavy

s 22 (printing a banned publication): no conviction only if the accused proves the
printing was without his authority, consent and knowledge, without any want of due
care, and that he had no reason to suspect its nature. s 27(4): a document
purporting to be subversive is presumed so, and possession deems knowledge of its
contents; the accused must prove both that he was unaware of the contents and that
he never had reasonable cause to suspect. s 27(2) obliges anyone who receives a
subversive document to deliver it to the police without delay. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Where a person present at drilling to train others falls under both s 6(1)(b)
  ($2,000 / one year) and s 6(2)(a) ($10,000 / 5 years), the row takes the higher
  limit. That choice is an inference; the text does not say which charge is laid.
- The penalty table is hand-transcribed; it should be checked against the PDF.
- Article 151 of the Constitution, which ss 9, 11 and 13A rely on, was not read.
- No case law (for example on s 8B) or subsidiary legislation was retrieved.
- The rest of Part 3 (ss 59-61, life and death offences relating to supplies and
  consorting) is untouched.
