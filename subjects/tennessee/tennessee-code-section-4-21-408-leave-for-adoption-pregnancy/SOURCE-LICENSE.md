- **Terms**: NOT open on the only reachable source. LexisNexis asserts a blanket prohibition over everything on its sites: its copyright page states that no part of the materials may be copied, reproduced, translated or reduced to machine-readable form, in whole or in part, for any reason, and its General Terms and Conditions (effective 5 June 2026) govern use of the Online Services and the Materials in them. That is a platform claim, not a claim the State of Tennessee has made - the Tennessee Code Commission contracts with LexisNexis to publish the Code (T.C.A. secs. 1-1-105 to 1-1-107, verified present in the register's own Title 1 table of contents), and the free public access edition exists because of that contract. As a matter of US law the enacted text itself cannot be owned by anyone: under the government edicts doctrine confirmed in Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020) - which was decided against exactly this arrangement, a state code published by a Lexis affiliate under contract with a state code commission - statutory text, numbering and section catchlines produced by legislators in their legislative capacity are outside copyright, and Public.Resource.Org concerned the annotations, not the text. So the text of the Tennessee Code may be reused and adapted; what may not be taken is this publisher's copy of it, its editorial apparatus, its formatting and its platform. open_licence is set false because the only source this network can reach forbids copying on its face. In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.

## In-force status

UNCLEAR -- The Tennessee Maternity Leave Act - the only general private-sector leave entitlement in the Tennessee Code, giving eligible employees up to four months of leave for adoption, pregnancy, childbirth and nursing, with reinstatement. Tennessee has no general annual-leave, sick-leave or holiday-pay statute for private employers; state-employee leave sits in Title 8, Chapter 50 (Miscellaneous Provisions), and legal holidays are designated by Title 15 (Holidays and Days of Special Observance), both verified present and unmarked in the register's table of contents. General family and medical leave is federal (FMLA, 29 U.S.C. ch. 28). See the in_force caveat under Payroll/wages.
