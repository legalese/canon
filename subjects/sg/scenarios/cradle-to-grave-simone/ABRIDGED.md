# Cradle to grave, abridged — twenty beats

The same life as [`SCENARIO.md`](SCENARIO.md), cut to two beats a chapter. Each beat is a scene: where Simone is, how old she looks, what she asks, and what the law in this repository says back. The status words are those of the full scenario (`ENC`, `PART`, `NONE`, `REGS`, `POLICY`, `CASE`), and every beat here is flagged `"abridged": true` in [`facts.json`](facts.json), which also carries each character's age at every event.

The assistant is a character. It is the only one who does not age. Where a row refuses or runs out, that is a beat too: the assistant says what it has and names what it does not, and the audience learns that grounded means bounded.

## Chapter 0 — Born (2003)

**Beat 1 · 14 March 2003 · KKH.** A nurse clips a bracelet on a newborn. Two citizen parents. The assistant, addressed for the first time by her father, is asked the simplest question in the whole life.

- *Is she Singaporean?* — Constitution Art 121: a person born in Singapore after 16 September 1963 is a citizen by birth, subject to the clauses on parents. `ENC`.

**Beat 2 · 2 April 2003 · the registration counter.** Her father registers the birth and the name on day nineteen.

- *By when, and who may?* — Registration of Births and Deaths Act 2021 s 7 (the hospital notifies within 24 hours), s 8 (registration within 42 days), s 10 (naming). `ENC`, replayed under the 2021 Act; the 1937 Act applied on the day.

## Chapter 1 — Primary school (2006–2015)

**Beat 3 · 4 January 2010 · a school gate in Tampines.** Age six, oversized bag. Her mother hesitates at the gate.

- *Does she have to go?* — Compulsory Education Act 2000 s 2 (compulsory school age: above 6, under 15), s 3 (a citizen child born after 1 January 1996, resident in Singapore, must attend a national primary school; each parent commits an offence if she does not). `ENC`. Which school, and in which phase, is MOE policy: `POLICY`.

**Beat 4 · June 2011 · a passport photo booth.** Age eight, told not to smile.

- *Can an eight-year-old have a passport, and who applies?* — Passports Act 2007 s 6 (application), s 27 (a parent applies for a child under 16). `ENC`. On the drive home from Malacca the same assistant is asked what is duty-free: Customs Act ss 93 and 128J for the offence and the Green Channel presumption, `ENC`; the allowance itself, `REGS`.

## Chapter 2 — Growing up (2016–2024)

**Beat 5 · 14 March 2018 · her fifteenth birthday.** A letter about an identity card.

- *Do I have to register?* — National Registration Act 1965 s 6. `ENC`. (The same Act will ask her to re-register at 30 under s 8, and to report every change of address within 28 days under s 10; the assistant will remind her three times in this life.)

**Beat 6 · 14 March 2024 · her twenty-first birthday.** Cake, and a list of things that switched on overnight.

- *What can I do today that I could not do yesterday?* — vote: Parliamentary Elections Act 1954 s 5, `ENC`; be presumed to consent to organ donation unless she objects: Human Organ Transplant Act 1987 ss 5, 8, `ENC`; make a will: Wills Act 1838 s 4, `ENC` in both succession rows; make a lasting power of attorney: Mental Capacity Act 2008 s 11, `ENC`; marry without anyone's consent: Women's Charter ss 17(2), 21A, `ENC`.

## Chapter 3 — University (2022–2026)

**Beat 7 · 3 May 2025 · a polling station in a school hall.** Her first general election, two months after turning 22.

- *Do I have to vote, and what if I don't?* — Parliamentary Elections Act s 43: a non-voter's name is removed from the register and may be restored. `ENC`.

**Beat 8 · 5 May 2025 · an internship desk.** $1,200 a month, a student, a question her supervisor cannot answer.

- *Does the company have to pay CPF for me?* — CPF Act 1953 s 7 states the duty, `ENC`; the exemption for students in approved programmes sits in subsidiary legislation no row has retrieved, `REGS`. The assistant says exactly that, and this is the first time the audience sees it stop at an edge.

## Chapter 4 — First job (2026–2028)

**Beat 9 · 31 August 2026 · a phone screen on an MRT platform.** The first payslip. Four figures she does not recognise.

- *Is $840 right, and where does it go?* — CPF Act s 7 for the duty, `ENC`; the rates (17% + 20%) and the split (OA $966, SA $252, MA $336) are the First Schedule and the allocation rules, `PART`, so the assistant gives the figures as supplied facts with their date and source. Also on the slip: salary timing, Employment Act Part 3, `ENC`; the employer's Skills Development Levy, `NONE`.

**Beat 10 · 15 March 2028 · a laptop at midnight.** Her first real tax return.

- *How much tax on $54,600?* — Income Tax Act 1947 s 39 (CPF relief $10,920, earned income relief $1,000) and the Second Schedule rates: chargeable income $42,680, tax $737.60. `ENC`, computed by the row, before any Budget rebate.

## Chapter 5 — A flat and a marriage (2028–2029)

**Beat 11 · 20 February 2028 · the HDB Hub.** Engaged, not married, applying for a flat.

- *Are we allowed to apply before the wedding?* — the fiancé/fiancée scheme, income ceiling and grant are HDB policy, `POLICY`; the one statutory test, that neither of them owns or has sold property in the last 30 months, is Housing and Development Act 1959 s 50(1), `ENC`, and they pass it.

**Beat 12 · 18 May 2029 · the Registry of Marriages.** Twenty-six and twenty-nine.

- *May we marry, and does anyone need to consent?* — Women's Charter 1961 s 9 (both over 18), s 11 (neither already married), ss 17(2) and 21A (no consent needed at or over 21). `ENC`. *How long is our notice good for, and who may solemnise?* — ss 14-15 and 22: outside the row, `PART`; the assistant quotes the source text and says so.

## Chapter 6 — First child (2030–2031)

**Beat 13 · 22 August 2030 · a kitchen calendar.** Two weeks before the due date, two parents counting weeks.

- *When can I stop work, and who pays which weeks?* — Employment Act s 76 (16 weeks, up to 4 before confinement) and CDCSA ss 9-10 (employer pays the first 8, Government the last 8, capped at $10,000 per 4 weeks). *How much can Marcus take?* — paternity 4 weeks, CDCSA ss 12H-12J; shared parental 10 weeks, ss 12DA-12DD, split six and four. All `ENC`.

**Beat 14 · 1 October 2030 · a pram in a bank branch.** Opening Kai En's Child Development Account.

- *What does this child get?* — Baby Gift $10,000; Child Credits $2,000 a year for the years he turns 1 to 16, $32,000 in all; CDA First Step Grant $5,000 and co-matching to $5,000; a $10,000 PSEA top-up in 2047. The child-support row, `ENC`, with its own caption: announced at the 2026 National Day Rally, to run from 1 April 2027, not yet enacted.

## Chapter 7 — Keys, a second child, the long middle (2031–2040)

**Beat 15 · 15 August 2031 · an empty four-room flat.** Keys in hand, a bill on the counter.

- *How much stamp duty on $420,000?* — Stamp Duties Act First Schedule Art 3(a)(iv): $1,800 + $3,600 + $1,800 = $7,200; additional buyer's duty under Art 3(bi): nil. `ENC`. *Is Home Protection compulsory with a CPF-serviced loan?* — CPF Act Part 4A, `PART`.

**Beat 16 · 2 May 2035 · her parents' flat in Tampines.** Boon Keng's sixty-fifth birthday. Simone, 32, has a toddler on her hip and a question for the assistant on her father's behalf.

- *Does Dad get Silver Support?* — Silver Support Scheme Act 2015 ss 6-7: not on his lifetime contributions, `ENC`, the thresholds entering as facts. *When do his payouts start?* — CPF LIFE, CPF Act Part 3A, `PART`. The assistant's answers now reach a second generation.

## Chapter 8 — Mid-life (2040–2060)

**Beat 17 · 28 April 2040 · a show-flat balcony in Punggol.** A seven-year-old executive condominium, $1,300,000, the Tampines flat already sold.

- *May we buy it, and what does it cost in duty?* — Executive Condominium Housing Scheme Act 1996 ss 5, 9, 10 (citizens may buy inside the ten-year window; what the seller owed), `ENC`; buyer's stamp duty $36,600, additional duty nil because they sold first, `ENC`; the remission rules had they bought first, `REGS`; the agent's commission, Estate Agents Act s 44, `ENC`.

**Beat 18 · 19 August 2052 · a hospital corridor, then a dining table.** Boon Keng dies at 82 with a will. Simone, 49, is executor.

- *What do I do this week?* — register the death: Registration of Births and Deaths Act ss 19-23, `ENC`. *Is the will good, and what grant do I need?* — Wills Act ss 4, 6; Probate and Administration Act, `ENC` in both succession rows. *The flat?* — joint tenancy, to Siew Lan by survivorship, `CASE`. *His CPF?* — to his nominee under CPF Act s 25, outside the will, `ENC`; that it is outside the estate is case law, `CASE`.

## Chapter 9 — Old age (2062–2088)

**Beat 19 · 14 March 2068 · a two-room Flexi flat is still three years away; this is the condominium kitchen.** Simone turns 65.

- *What starts today, and what don't I get?* — CPF LIFE payouts, Part 3A, `PART`; MediShield Life premium subsidies, s 4 and regulations, `REGS`; Silver Support, ss 6-7, not eligible, `ENC`; Pioneer, Merdeka and Majulah packages, no, by birth year, `NONE` — a negative the assistant volunteers.

**Beat 20 · 2 November 2088 · the two-room flat.** Simone dies at 85. Kai En, 58, is executor. The assistant is asked the last questions by the boy from the pram.

- *What do I do this week?* — register the death, RBDA ss 19-23. *Her will?* — Wills Act; Probate and Administration Act. *Her CPF?* — split between two nominees as s 25(1) directs. *Her insurance?* — Insurance Act ss 132-133 nominations. *Estate duty?* — Estate Duty Act s 2A: none, for a death after 15 February 2008. All `ENC`. *The unexpired lease?* — HDB's short-lease terms, `POLICY`. The assistant answers, and does not age.

## Notes for the screenplay

- **Dates on screen.** Every beat has a real date. Put it on a title card; the law the assistant quotes is the law in force on that date in the current encodings, and the anachronisms in Chapters 0 to 3 (today's Acts replayed over 2003 to 2024) are honest and should be acknowledged once, early, in the assistant's voice.
- **Refusals are beats.** Beats 8, 9, 12 and 16 each have the assistant stop at a boundary and say what lies beyond it. Keep them; they are what distinguishes a grounded assistant from a confident one.
- **The two generations.** Beats 16 and 18 turn the assistant toward her parents; beat 20 turns it toward her son. The device that carries the assistant (a phone, a speaker, a pair of glasses) can change with the decade while the voice does not.
- **Money, sparingly.** Five figures carry the whole film: $840 (beat 9), $737.60 (beat 10), $7,200 (beat 15), $36,600 (beat 17), and the $32,000 of Child Credits (beat 14). Everything else can stay in `facts.json`.
