## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its **everyday-life relevance** (Tier 1), not by citation count. Community
Mediation Centres are where neighbour, family and community quarrels go, whether the
parties bring them or a Magistrate or tribunal sends them. This row takes what a party
meets: which disputes come in (ss 9(3), 11(1)), who may sit in (s 10(5)), when
attendance is voluntary (s 12), when a settlement binds (s 13(2)), who may speak for a
party (s 14), referral by a Magistrate (s 15(1)), how long records are kept (s 7(2)),
immunity (s 17(1)), orders against the Centre in later litigation (s 18(1)), privilege
and inadmissibility (s 19) and secrecy (s 20).

Not encoded: establishment, Directors, delegation and premises (ss 3-6), mediators'
appointment and pay (s 8), the outcome-reporting duties (ss 15(3), 15A(1)(b), 15B),
evaluation (s 16), s 17(2), s 22 and regulations (s 23). The Acts that s 12(2A), 15,
15A and 15B point to (Criminal Procedure Code 2010, Small Claims Tribunals Act 1984,
Community Disputes Resolution Act 2015) were not read.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. "Voluntary" mediation is compulsory once a court or tribunal sends you

s 12(1) says attendance and participation are voluntary and s 12(2) lets a party walk
out at any time. But s 12(2A), added by Act 43 of 2024 with effect from 24 March 2025,
switches both off for a session that takes place because of a referral under s 15
(Magistrate), CDRA s 13M or 30, or SCTA s 18A. A Magistrate may refer a private
criminal complaint "with or without the consent of both" sides (s 15(1)). Asserted.

### 2. Nothing agreed at the session binds unless it is written and signed as binding

s 13(2): no settlement "is binding on the parties ... unless it has been reduced to
writing with a statement signed by or on behalf of the parties to this effect". A
written settlement without that signed statement does not bind under this section.
Asserted.

### 3. What is said in mediation stays out of court, with three exits

s 19(3) and (4) make anything said, any admission, and any document prepared for a
session inadmissible before "any court, tribunal or body". The exits in s 19(5) are:
everyone present or named consents; proceedings about something disclosed under
s 20(c) (danger of injury or damage); or the court finds admission "necessary for the
fair disposal of the case or to save costs". The same "fair disposal or to save costs"
test governs a registrar's order for the mediator to attend court (s 18(1)). Asserted.

### 4. No lawyer or agent by default, and on a compulsory referral the paperwork is stricter

s 14(1): a party "is not entitled to be represented by an agent" unless the Director
thinks an agent would help, the agent knows the matter, and the Director approves.
For a s 12(2A) referral, s 14(1A) adds a written authority from the party (including to
agree terms) and the agent's written consent. Section 14 does not apply at all to a
Small Claims Tribunal referral (s 14(4)). Officers of companies, strata management
corporations, statutory bodies and registered societies may represent them without
approval (s 14(2)); an individual has no such carve-out. Asserted.

### 5. Only family, social or community disputes, never a seizable offence, and only with the Director's consent

s 11(1) admits "a family, social or community dispute that does not involve a seizable
offence", and s 9(3) lets the Director decline any dispute. Both are "subject to"
the referral provisions. That a referral needs neither the Director's consent nor the
s 11(1) subject-matter test is an inference from the "subject to" words, not something
the text says in terms. Treating a commercial debt claim as outside the three
categories is also the encoder's reading; the Act does not define them. Asserted on
those readings.

### 6. Privilege covers the session, not what parties say about it afterwards

s 19(1)-(2) give a session and documents sent to arrange one the defamation privilege
of judicial proceedings, but not "a publication made otherwise than" at the session,
under s 19(1)(b), or as provided by s 20. A later social-media post or a complaint to
the other party's employer is outside it. Asserted.

### 7. Secrecy has six gateways and no penalty in this Act

s 20 lets a mediator, Director, staff member, evaluator or researcher disclose only on
consent, for administering the Act, to prevent danger of injury or property damage, to
refer parties with their consent, for research that does not identify people without
consent, or under a court order or written law. s 20 itself states no penalty, and
none was found elsewhere in the deposit. Asserted (gateways only).

### 8. Records are kept three years

s 7(2): records of a dispute must be kept for 3 years after settlement, withdrawal or
termination. Counted here as 36 whole months, with the duty ending at 36: that boundary
is the encoder's. Asserted.
