## Why this Act

**14 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. It is short, so this row takes
it whole. The Public Prosecutor's investigative powers (ss 7, 8, 10) are summarised,
not modelled.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Paying a ransom is a crime

s 5(2): whoever "pays or provides funds for the payment of any ransom" commits an
offence punishable by up to 7 years and a fine. That includes the victim's family and
anyone who lends them the money. s 12 then says a witness who paid is not, for that
reason alone, unworthy of credit. The Act criminalises the family and relies on its
evidence. Asserted.

### 2. Knowing and staying silent is a crime, and the excuse is yours to prove

s 9(1): anyone aware of a kidnapping, or of an intention to commit one, must inform the
police "forthwith". A reasonable excuse is a defence, but "the burden of proving which
shall lie upon the person so aware". The maximum is $1,000 or 12 months. Asserted.

### 3. The penalty ladder

- **s 3:** kidnapping for ransom is punishable by death or life imprisonment, with
  caning if not sentenced to death.
- **s 4:** receiving ransom knowingly carries up to 10 years and caning.
- **s 5(1):** negotiating to obtain ransom carries up to 10 years and caning.
- **s 5(2):** paying or funding ransom carries up to 7 years and a fine.

Asserted.

### 4. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 6:** the Public Prosecutor may freeze a bank account for up to one month (encoded
  as 31 days). A bank manager who ignores the order faces $3,000 or 3 years.
- **s 13:** unexplained wealth may corroborate testimony that the accused received a
  ransom, including wealth held by others on the accused's behalf.
- **s 14:** an informer's identity is protected unless the informer knowingly lied or
  justice requires disclosure.
