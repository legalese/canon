## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its **everyday-life relevance** (Tier 1 of the remaining Acts), not by
citation count. It is the law under which a post, a chat group, an account or an app
that an ordinary person uses or runs can be ordered down or blocked because it is
suspected of furthering a crime, above all a scam. This row takes the Part 2
directions (the ones that reach individuals) and who may receive them (ss 6, 7), who
may challenge them and when (ss 15-18, 42, 43(1)), the offence of not complying, its
defences and its maximum penalties (ss 50, 54), and police demands for information
(s 48, with the s 53 penalty in a comment). Five offences are tested against the
First Schedule.

Not encoded: Part 4 (designated online services, codes of practice, rectification
notices, implementation directives) and the Part 5 appeals to the Minister; Part 6
orders (ss 28-37); Reviewing Tribunal composition and procedure beyond ss 42 and
43(1); service of notices; s 47; ss 51-53 penalties; arrestability (s 55); immunity
(s 57); and most of the First Schedule.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. For scams, the bar is lower twice over

s 6(1)(a) needs a designated officer to "reasonably suspect" that a specified offence
**has been committed** and that online activity furthers it. s 6(1)(b), for scam or
malicious cyber activity offences, needs only that the officer "suspects or has
reason to believe" that the activity is "preparatory to, or in furtherance of" the
offence: suspicion need not be reasonable, and nothing need yet have been committed.
Asserted.

### 2. Some scam offences are not specified offences, and that changes what police can demand

The Securities and Futures Act items (s 204 for contravening s 197 and others) appear
only in First Schedule Part 2 (scam offences), not in Part 1 (specified offences). A
Part 2 direction can therefore be given against activity furthering them under
s 6(1)(b), but the s 48 police power to demand information requires a **specified**
offence, so it does not reach them. Asserted.

### 3. No hearing, no suspension, and appeal only after reconsideration

s 13(3): no one affected need be heard before a direction is given. Reconsideration
must be sought within 30 days of being given the direction (s 16(2)(b)) and does not
suspend it (s 16(3)). Only after a decision to affirm or substitute may the appellant
appeal, on a prescribed fee, to a Reviewing Tribunal (s 18), which must not consider
an appeal made more than 30 days after the notice of decision unless it finds that
equitable (s 43(1)). Appealing is expressly not a defence to non-compliance
(s 54(1)(b)). The time limits and the appeal-is-no-defence rule are asserted; s 13(3)
is quoted only.

### 4. A reposter cannot challenge; the poster can, but not a stop communication direction

The s 15 table lets the "originator" of the material challenge a disabling or access
blocking direction, and s 2 defines originator to exclude "any other person who
reposts identical copies". For a stop communication direction only the recipient may
challenge. The table's columns are shifted in the deposit; appellants were matched to
rows by order. Asserted.

### 5. The Tribunal has three grounds and no others

s 42(1): it "may only cancel" a Part 2 direction if s 6(1) was not satisfied, the
recipient was not one allowed by s 7, or compliance is not reasonably practicable.
Disproportion or the lawfulness of the content are not listed. Asserted.

### 6. The individual who posted faces the heaviest prison term, and has the narrowest defence

Only a stop communication direction can be given to a person who controls the
material (s 7). Failing to comply carries up to $20,000 and 2 years for an individual
($500,000 otherwise) (s 50(6)), the longest imprisonment in s 50; disabling and
account restriction carry 12 months for an individual, $1 million otherwise and a
further $100,000 a day after conviction (s 50(7)); access blocking and app removal
carry $20,000 a day capped at $500,000, with no imprisonment (s 50(8)). The s 54(3)
"reasonably practicable" defence applies "except in relation to a stop communication
direction". Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Part 4 (designated online services and codes of practice) is the Act's main tool
  against platforms and is untouched.
- Part 6 orders, their grounds of cancellation (s 42(2)-(4)) and ss 51-53 penalties
  are not encoded.
- Whether Penal Code s 420 lies outside Chapters 5, 5A and 23 was assumed, not read.
- No regulations (prescribed manner, fees), Reviewing Tribunal rules, Gazette orders
  or case law were retrieved.
