## Register access

PARTIAL - table-of-contents level only, on a commercial platform, because the State's own register is unreachable. The Tennessee General Assembly's hosts are all refused at the TCP layer from this network: www.capitol.tn.gov (170.142.7.20) and wapp.capitol.tn.gov returned 'connect ECONNREFUSED 170.142.7.20:443' from the agent fetch tool and HTTP code 000 from curl with the honest user agent 'canon-legislation-indexer/1.0 (legalese/canon corpus metadata survey)'; www.tnga.gov and www.tn.gov/sos/acts.html likewise returned 000; sos.tn.gov and publications.tnsosfiles.com returned HTTP 403. The only reachable source for the consolidated Code is the state-sponsored free public access edition that LexisNexis publishes under its contract with the Tennessee Code Commission, at https://www.lexisnexis.com/hottopics/tncode/, which redirects to an advance.lexis.com container. That platform was loaded in an ordinary browser session with no sign-in; NO CAPTCHA and no bot challenge was presented at any point, and nothing was bypassed. WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THIS RECORD: verification for every Act below is TABLE-OF-CONTENTS LEVEL ONLY. The TOC tree was expanded node by node and the exact Title, Chapter, Part and section headings recorded, including the [Reserved] and [Repealed] markers the TOC itself prints; no section text was opened or copied. The platform publishes NO CURRENCY DATE on the table of contents, so no as-at date could be captured, and repeal of an individual section could not be checked beyond what the TOC marks. Two further caveats a human should know: the Lexis page frame is mislabelled 'Georgia General Assembly' in its own header (an apparent template artefact - the source itself is named 'Tennessee Code Table of Contents PAW- ET TABLE OF CONTENTS' and the document title is 'Tennessee Code Unannotated - Free Public Access'), and during one TOC interaction a page script fired a copy handler that wrote to the operating-system clipboard, so the user should check their clipboard before pasting. Justia, FindLaw and Casetext were NOT used. About 20 page interactions, none rapid.

## Topics with no legislation here

- **Personal tax/withholding**: Tennessee levies no general personal income tax and operates no state wage withholding, so there is nothing of substance to encode for this topic. The Hall income tax on interest and dividends was phased out: Title 67, Chapter 2 (Income Taxation) is still carried in the register's table of contents, and its own section list - verified by expanding the TOC - ends with 'sec. 67-2-124. Effect of reduction to rate of tax - Annual reduction of tax - Elimination of tax.', which is the elimination provision; the tax never applied to wages and imposed no employer withholding obligation in any event. The register's Title 67 (Taxes And Licenses) otherwise consists of petroleum, privilege and excise, property, sales and use, severance and transfer taxes. Personal income tax withholding for a Tennessee worker is therefore federal only: 26 U.S.C. ch. 24 and FICA under 26 U.S.C. ch. 21. A human should confirm the current status of ch. 67-2 on the General Assembly's own site when it is reachable.
- **Passenger compensation**: No state statute. Compensation for denied boarding, delay and cancellation is federal: 14 C.F.R. part 250 and 49 U.S.C. ch. 417 for air, 49 U.S.C. for rail. The register's Title 65 table of contents carries carrier chapters (ch. 12 Operation of Railroads, ch. 15 Motor Carriers, ch. 19 Ridesharing, ch. 20 Miscellaneous Provisions Relating to Carriers) that regulate carriers for safety, licensing and rates; none creates a passenger compensation entitlement.
- **Customs/duties**: Federal matter. Customs duties and tariffs are exclusively federal (Tariff Act of 1930, 19 U.S.C.); U.S. Const. art. I, sec. 10, cl. 2 bars states from laying imposts or duties on imports or exports.
- **Work permits/immigration**: Federal matter. Immigration status and work authorisation are governed by the Immigration and Nationality Act, 8 U.S.C., and I-9 verification by 8 U.S.C. sec. 1324a. Tennessee's adjacent provisions ride on the federal scheme rather than creating a work-permit regime: Title 50, Chapter 1, Part 7 (Tennessee Lawful Employment Act, the state E-Verify mandate) and Tenn. Code Ann. sec. 4-21-409 (Employee not authorized to work in the United States), both verified present and unmarked in the register's table of contents.
