# Architects Act 1991 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
conventions and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, with amendments to Act 15
of 2026 (in force 1 May 2026) and Act 25 of 2024 (in force 1 October 2025) shown.
The arrangement of sections at the head of the deposit is out of step with the body
from Part 4 on (it puts "Qualifications for registration" against no number and
"Conviction final and conclusive" against s 31G). This row follows the body, whose
cross-references agree with it: the Disciplinary Committee's powers are s 31G.

**Checks:** one case file, 202 assertions satisfied, 0 errors.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**9 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what an
architect, a client, a builder or an engineer meets: who may prepare, sign, supply
and offer architectural work and use the title (ss 3, 10-14), the experience routes
to registration (s 15), removal and return (ss 17B, 17C), the annual practising
certificate (ss 18, 19), and the ceilings on sanctions (ss 26A(2), 31G, 34). The
Board's constitution, the licensing tests for corporations and partnerships in s 20,
ss 22-25, Part 7 procedure, mutual recognition (s 15A) and offences by bodies
(ss 35, 35A) are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Hiring an unqualified architect is the client's offence too

s 12(1A): "a person must not engage the architectural services of any other person
in relation to any building works" unless that person is a registered architect with
a practising certificate (or a conditionally registered one acting within
conditions). The fine is up to $2,000, and $5,000 for a second conviction. Read
literally, (1A) names only registered architects, so engaging a **licensed
corporation** for building works also falls outside it, even though s 10(5)(a)(iii)
lets that corporation supply the services. The opening words "Subject to the
provisions of this Act" may cure this; whether they do is not decided here (an
inference left open). The encoding follows the literal text. Asserted.

### 2. Supervision lets you draw, never sign

s 10(1)(b) lets anyone prepare building plans "under the direction or supervision" of
a practising architect. s 10(2) has no such exception: only a registered architect
with a practising certificate may sign and submit, and (2A) makes a document signed
in contravention "invalid". A conditionally registered foreign architect may prepare
and supply, but not sign or submit. Asserted.

### 3. A builder may offer architectural services but not hold itself out

s 10(5A) lets a builder "supply or offer to supply" architectural services for works
it undertakes, if a practising architect or licensed corporation or LLP provides
them. But s 10(3)(b) lets only a practising architect or "an architectural practice"
hold out as authorised to supply them, and a builder is neither. Asserted.

### 4. A suspended architect is treated as having no certificate

s 31G(10): during a suspension the architect is "not ... to be regarded as having in
force a practising certificate". Every privilege in Part 3 falls away, and engaging
or employing that architect becomes an offence under s 12. Asserted.

### 5. Three routes to registration, and a 2026 change on early experience

s 15(2): 2 years' experience with 12 continuous months in Singapore and a
professional practice examination; or 5 years with 2 in Singapore and a continuous
12 months within the 5 years before applying, plus an examination; or 10 years, an
international commendation or award, and an examination. From 1 May 2026, experience
gained before qualifying counts for a degree holder only if it began on or after
1 May 2026 and was in Singapore (s 15(2AB)); for a person qualifying under s 15(1)(c)
it may count wherever and whenever gained (s 15(2AD)). Asserted.

### 6. Renew by 1 December, or pay more

s 18(1): an architect must apply "not later than 1 December" to practise in the next
year. A late renewer may still get a certificate on an additional fee, for the next
year if applying in December, or for the remainder of the year if applying on or after
1 January (s 18(5)). A first application after registration may be made at any time
(s 18(2)). The Board may cancel a certificate on any refusal ground in s 18(4) "whether
or not that ground existed" when it was issued, but only after a hearing (s 19).
Asserted.

### 7. The penalties are modest, the professional sanctions are not

Unlawful practice under s 10(6) is $5,000, or $10,000 and 6 months for a repeat
offender (no imprisonment for a body corporate). A Disciplinary Committee may suspend
for up to 2 years, fine up to $50,000 and award costs up to $10,000 (s 31G(2), (3)).
The Board may fine a licensed practice up to $100,000 in lieu of revocation
(s 26A(2)). Asserted.

### 8. Naval and landscape architects are outside the Act altogether

s 3(2): "This Act does not apply to naval architects and landscape architects." The
text does not limit this to their own fields, so the encoding reads it literally: the
Act's prohibitions do not reach them. That reading is an inference. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The extent of s 3(2) and the effect of "Subject to the provisions of this Act" on
  s 12(1A) need a reader of the case law; none was searched.
- s 20 licensing conditions, s 22 reporting duties and the Part 7 complaints process
  are not encoded.
- The Minister's approved qualifications (s 15(1)(b)), exemption orders (ss 15(3),
  36) and the Board's rules (s 38) were not retrieved.
