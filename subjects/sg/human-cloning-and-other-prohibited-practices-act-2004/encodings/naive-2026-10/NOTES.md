## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Splitting an embryo makes a clone, so placing a split twin embryo is cloning

s 2(4): an embryo "that results from the technological process known as embryo
splitting is not to be taken as created by a process of fertilisation". A human embryo
clone is any human embryo that is a genetic copy of another human and not created by
fertilisation, and s 2(2) does not require an identical copy. So an embryo split from an
ordinary IVF embryo is a clone, and placing it in a woman contravenes s 5, punishable by
up to $100,000 or 10 years (s 18). Asserted.

### 2. Creating a cloned embryo is not itself prohibited; placing it, or growing it past 14 days, is

s 5 prohibits placing a clone "in the body of a human or the body of an animal"; s 7
prohibits developing an embryo not created by fertilisation "for a period of more than
14 days". Nothing in Part 3 prohibits creating a clone or keeping it to day 14. A cloned
embryo on day 10 contravenes neither s 7 nor s 8; on day 15 it contravenes both, and is
a prohibited embryo. That the Act therefore leaves research on cloned embryos up to day
14 to other law is an inference; the text simply does not reach it. Asserted.

### 3. You may not pay for eggs, sperm or embryos — and a discount counts as payment

s 13(1) forbids giving, offering, receiving or offering to receive "valuable
consideration" for the supply of a human egg, sperm or embryo, and s 13(2) makes the
contract void. s 13(5) defines valuable consideration to include "any inducement,
discount or priority in the provision of a service", so trading eggs for cheaper IVF is
caught. Reimbursing reasonable expenses (collection, storage, transport) is not, and a
clinic charging for the service of receiving, storing, processing and implanting donated
material is outside "supply" altogether (s 13(4)). Treating the part of a payment above
reasonable expenses as consideration is an inference. Asserted.

### 4. The 14-day line is counted without time frozen, and the 8-week line ends the Act's reach

Every 14-day period in ss 2, 7 and 8 excludes "any period when the development is
suspended", so a frozen IVF embryo with five days of culture is a five-day embryo
whatever its age. A "human embryo" is one developing "for less than 8 weeks"; after
that, nothing in Part 3 that turns on "human embryo" applies. Asserted.

### 5. Every Part 3 practice carries the same maximum, and obstruction one-twentieth of it

s 18 sets one penalty — a fine up to $100,000, up to 10 years' imprisonment, or both —
for ss 5, 7 to 12 and 13(1) alike: paying an egg donor beyond expenses is in the same
band as implanting a clone. Failing without reasonable excuse to comply with an
enforcement officer, giving false information (s 14(6)) or obstruction (s 16) carry
$5,000 or 12 months. Asserted.

### 6. A seized article can be kept for 6 months, longer with a court, or until trial ends

s 14(3), (4): retention for 6 months or a longer period a Magistrate's Court allows; if
relevant proceedings start within that period, until they conclude. Officers who
consented, connived or were neglectful are guilty with their company, partnership or
association (s 17). Asserted.
