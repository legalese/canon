# Source license

The text of **Hawaii Revised Statutes, Chapter 235 — Income Tax Law** is published on the [Hawaii Revised Statutes, published by the Hawaii State Legislature (Legislative Reference Bureau, Revision of Statutes Division)](https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/docs/hrs.htm).

- **Terms**: Not open. The State of Hawaii's terms of access and use, which by their own definition apply to every official state site whose address contains 'hawaii.gov' (so, to the Legislature's HRS site), bar automated access entirely and bar commercial duplication, and say nothing permitting adaptation. That is a genuine restriction on the register, and it is the reason the corpus cannot simply harvest the HRS. The underlying legal position is different: under the government edicts doctrine (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)) the text of the Hawaii Revised Statutes is uncopyrightable, so a copy lawfully obtained elsewhere may be reused and adapted freely. The restriction is on this delivery channel, not on the statutes. I could not read any copyright or terms page on capitol.hawaii.gov itself, because the host is blocked; the terms quoted are the statewide portal terms.
- **Terms checked**: 2026-09-23, at https://portal.ehawaii.gov/page/terms-of-use/
- **From the terms**: "You specifically agree not to access, or attempt to access, or allow any employee, agent or contractor to access or attempt to access any of the Services through any automated means (including, but not limited to, use of scripts, web crawlers or screen scrapers) and shall ensure that you and your agents, employees and contractors comply with the instructions set out in any robots.txt file present on the site."
- **Reuse and adaptation permitted, per the index check**: no, or not established
- **Attribution required**: Content must not be used 'in any manner likely to give the impression of official approval by the State of Hawaiʻi'; the great seal and coat of arms are separately restricted by HRS § 5-6.
- **Source**: https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol04_Ch0201-0257/HRS0235/HRS_0235-.htm

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.

## Language

Hawaiian and English are both official languages of the State (Haw. Const. art. XV, § 4), but that article provides that Hawaiian is required for public acts and transactions only as provided by law, and the Hawaii Revised Statutes are enacted and published in English. English is therefore the authentic language of the statutes.
