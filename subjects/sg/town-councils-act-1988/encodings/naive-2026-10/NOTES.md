## Why this Act, and why scoped

**27 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it, 16 of them for s 4, which
establishes Town Councils: other Acts make them responsible for HDB common property.
This row takes what an HDB resident pays or is liable for: lift upgrading
contributions, conservancy arrears and parking offences.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A flat can be sold for unpaid conservancy charges

s 66: conservancy and service charges unpaid 90 days after a written demand become a
**charge on the flat**, and the Town Council then has a mortgagee's power of sale
"even though the charge is not registered". Sale needs HDB's approval and a 90-day
notice, and is barred while the owner's movable property in the flat would cover the
debt. Improvement contributions follow the same path after 3 months (s 34). Asserted.

### 2. The lift-upgrading contribution has a floor, not a ceiling

s 32(3), (4): the Town Council determines each owner's contribution, its decision "is
final", and it "must **not be less than**" what HDB would have determined for similar
works. Nothing caps it above. Asserted.

### 3. 75% of all owners' voting value binds the rest, and liability follows ownership at determination

s 31(3): works proceed only with votes worth at least 75% of the total voting value
of **all** eligible owners (abstention counts against) and the Minister's approval.
Every owner then pays, including those who voted against (s 32(2)). "Owner" means
the owner when the contribution is **determined** (s 32(5)), so a buyer who completes
after the works but before the determination pays for them. Asserted.

### 4. A spouse can be made to pay a dead owner's contribution as their own debt

s 33(2): where no representation has been taken out for a deceased owner, or the
representatives do not apply to transfer the flat, the Town Council may require the
spouse or any beneficiary aged 21 or over "to pay the improvement contribution **as if
it is a debt owed by him or her**", with a right to recover from the estate. Asserted.

### 5. One parking ticket, one statutory declaration

s 70: the vehicle owner is guilty of a parking offence in the Town as if they were
the driver, unless they name the driver by statutory declaration within 7 days, or
show the driver could not be traced, or the car was stolen. s 70(5): a declaration
"which relates to more than one parking offence is not regarded as a statutory
declaration" for that purpose. Asserted.

### 6. Where the sale money goes

s 35: costs of sale; the CPF Board; HDB lease moneys; secured improvement
contributions; conservancy charges and unsecured Town Council contributions,
equally; HDB's unsecured contributions; later mortgages; the owner. A bank holding a
later mortgage ranks behind the Town Council's unsecured conservancy arrears.
Asserted.
