## Why this Act, and why scoped

**13 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes whether a
licence is needed and what happens without one: the First Schedule, ss 4, 28 and 32.
Licensing procedure, conditions, demerit points, arts classification, appeals,
enforcement and the s 23 exemption orders (not retrieved) are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A foreign preacher brings a church service under licensing

First Schedule para 3(c): religious ceremonies in consecrated premises are outside the
Act only "where any discourse, sermon, lecture, talk or address ... is only given by
persons who are citizens or permanent residents of Singapore". With a choir, the
service is a listed entertainment. Add a visiting foreign preacher and the exclusion is
lost. Asserted.

### 2. Talks are outside the Act; music is in

Para 3(f) excludes "any lecture, talk, address, debate or discussion". Para 2(f)
includes "any reproduction or transmission ... of any music, song or speech" by means
other than telephony, wherever the public has access. Read literally, that includes
background music in a shop. The s 23 exemption orders, which presumably take that
out, were not retrieved. Asserted, without the exemptions.

### 3. The defendant must prove the event was private

s 32: "the onus of proving that the public ... did not have access ... lies on the
person alleging the fact". The organiser who claims the event was private has to prove
it. "Any class of the public" and "gratuitously or otherwise" (para 2) make public
access easy to establish. Asserted.

### 4. Performers are not liable

s 28(2): no one provides or assists in providing an entertainment "merely by reason of
the person having taken part". Organisers and venue operators are liable: $20,000 for
running without a licence or while it is suspended, $10,000 for breaching a condition.
Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **Para 3(d):** members-only meetings of companies, unions, political associations and
  societies are excluded. Admitting non-members loses the exclusion.
- **Para 3(b), (e):** Government and statutory-board entertainments are excluded.
