## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its **everyday-life relevance**: anyone in Singapore who speaks, posts,
forwards or prints something unflattering about another person, or is the subject of
it, is inside this Act. An automated count found none of the 527 deposited Singapore
Acts citing it by its slug title. That count misses Acts cited by short or older
titles, so it says nothing about importance.

This row covers when slander and malicious falsehood need no proof of special damage
(ss 3–6), innocent publication and the offer of amends (s 7), the partial-truth rules
for justification and fair comment (ss 8, 9), apology in mitigation (s 10(1)), reports
of court proceedings (s 11), what a "newspaper" is and the Schedule privilege (ss 2,
12, 13), election statements (s 14) and indemnity agreements (s 15). Not encoded:
s 7(4) (High Court control of an accepted offer, indemnity costs), s 10(2) (newspaper
payment into court), s 12(4), the procedural ss 16–20 and s 21.

The Act modifies the common law; it does not state it. That libel is actionable
without special damage and slander usually is not is common law, and the encoding
labels it as an inference.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Section 7(1)(b), read literally, asks only that the defendant published the words

The deposited text says the defence is to prove that the words "were published by
the defendant or were published by the defendant innocently in relation to the
claimant". Read literally, the first limb is met by every defendant. The encoding
takes the evident sense (innocent publication), and says so in a comment. The
annotation "[Act 25 of 2021 wef 01/04/2022]" sits under that paragraph; whether the
words are a drafting slip in that amendment is an inference, not checked.
The innocence reading is asserted.

### 2. The slander exception for imputing unchastity protects women only

s 4: words imputing "unchastity or adultery to any woman or girl" need no special
damage. The same spoken rumour about a man gets no help from s 4. Asserted.

### 3. The trade exception needs a job held at the time

s 5 removes the need for special damage for words disparaging the claimant in an
office, profession, trade or business "held or carried on by him at the time of the
publication", whether or not the words are spoken of him in that capacity. A spoken
slur on a retired doctor's past practice does not qualify. Asserted.

### 4. A broadcast is permanent; so a broadcast falsehood about a flat needs no proof of loss

s 3 treats broadcasting for general reception as publication "in a permanent form",
and s 6(2) carries that into malicious falsehood. A spoken false claim that a flat for
sale is haunted needs special damage proved (it does not touch a business); the same
claim broadcast does not (s 6(1)(a)). Asserted.

### 5. A blog or an online-only portal is not a "newspaper"

s 2: a "newspaper" is a paper "printed for sale" and "published in Singapore" at
intervals "not exceeding 36 days". The Schedule privilege in s 12 attaches to
publication "in a newspaper", extended by s 13 only to licensed broadcasting
stations in Singapore. A free sheet, a quarterly, and a blog reporting the same public
meeting fall outside s 12. Asserted. Whether other privilege reaches them is common
law and not encoded.

### 6. Section 12(3), literally, removes protection only when both public tests fail

It excludes "any matter which is not of public concern and the publication of which
is not for the public benefit". Read literally, a matter of public concern but not for
the public benefit keeps the privilege. The encoding follows the words; how a court
reads the conjunction was not checked. Asserted.

### 7. An offer of amends must be sworn, prompt and kept open

s 7(2): the offer must be expressed to be made under s 7 and carry an affidavit of
the facts relied on, and only those facts may be proved. An unaccepted offer is a
defence only if made "as soon as practicable" after notice and not withdrawn, and a
publisher who is not the author must prove the author wrote "without malice"
(s 7(6)). An accepted offer bars proceedings only once "duly performed". Asserted.

### 8. Partial truth is enough, within limits

s 8: with two or more distinct charges, justification survives an unproved charge
that does not "materially injure" given the proved ones; s 9 does the same for fair
comment on facts partly proved. With a single charge s 8 does not help. Asserted.

### 9. Court reports must be contemporaneous, public and local

s 11: absolute privilege covers a "fair and accurate and contemporaneous" report of
proceedings "publicly heard" before a court "within Singapore", surviving a later
reversal unless the publisher knew or ought to have known of it. A report a year
later, of a hearing in chambers, or of a foreign court is outside s 11 (foreign
Commonwealth court reports in a newspaper are Schedule Part 1 matter instead).
Asserted.

### 10. Candidates and indemnities

s 14: a candidate's statement is not privileged merely because it is material to an
issue in the election. s 15: an indemnity against libel liability is unlawful only if
the person knew the matter was defamatory and did not reasonably believe there was a
good defence. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The common-law framework (libel versus slander, the elements of justification, fair
  comment, malice, qualified privilege generally, the Reynolds-type defence) is
  outside the Act and is not encoded.
- The s 7(1)(b) wording should be checked against the pre-2021 text and the amending
  Act 25 of 2021.
- No case law was searched, including on the reading of s 12(3).
- The Rules of Court on pleading defamation, and the Protection from Harassment Act
  and online-falsehood legislation that people now meet alongside this Act, were not
  read.
