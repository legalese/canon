## Why this Act, and why scoped

**23 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the duties and
offences that bind people other than licensees: digging near cables (Part 5) and the
Part 6 offences. Licensing, entry on land, codes and directions, control of designated
licensees, special administration and dispute resolution are not encoded. The general
penalty section was not read, so penalties are given only where an offence section
states its own.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Cutting a cable by accident while digging can cost twenty times more than cutting it on purpose

s 68(1): wilfully damaging telecommunication plant carries a fine of up to $50,000 or 3
years. s 68(2), "despite subsection (1)": anyone who, while carrying out earthworks,
"damages or suffers to be damaged" a cable faces up to **$1 million** or 5 years. No
mental element is required, only a due-diligence defence. s 68(3) makes the employer
liable too, unless it proves no consent, connivance or neglect. s 69 adds compensation
"whether wilfully, negligently, accidentally or otherwise". Asserted.

### 2. Three conditions before digging near a cable

s 33(1): 7 days' written notice, cable information obtained and the licensee consulted,
**and** detection by a licensed cable detection worker. Missing any one is an offence
($100,000 or 3 years). An emergency dig is allowed if notified within 7 days after
(s 33(2)). An unlicensed detector cannot sue for their fee (s 35(2)). Asserted.

### 3. s 36 is limited to machinery

s 36 (dig without first ascertaining cable locations) applies only to digging "with any
mechanical equipment or explosive". Hand digging is outside s 36 but still inside s 33.
Asserted.

### 4. Possessing a radio is presumed to be operating it

s 54(1) forbids **possessing** radio-communication equipment without a licence or
regulatory exemption. Under s 55(2), possession is presumed to be operation, and under
s 55(3) the **occupier** of premises with unlicensed equipment commits an offence unless
they could not with reasonable diligence have known. What counts as exempt is in the
regulations, which were not retrieved. Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 57(3):** seized equipment that is not prosecuted is forfeited unless claimed within
  2 months.
- **s 62(2)(b):** a licensee's employee may trace a call at the subscriber's request.
- **s 64:** possessing or supplying a device for dishonestly obtaining a service carries up
  to 10 years.
