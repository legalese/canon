## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its **everyday-life relevance**: it decides which childcare centres and
kindergartens must be licensed, who may run them, and what happens to children and
parents' fees when a centre closes. This row takes the definition of a regulated
service and the exclusions (s 2, s 3, the Schedule), unlicensed operation (s 6), lapse
(s 15), the financial-penalty cap (s 16(2)(g)), wind-down directions (s 18(3)),
disqualified key appointment holders (s 19), older children (s 20(2)), the maximum
penalties and composition (ss 6, 17, 18, 19, 35, 38, 39, 45, 42) and appeals (s 43).
Application and transfer procedure, security deposits, staff and educator approvals
(Parts 3 and 4) beyond appeal rights, codes of practice, entry powers, corporate
liability, service of documents and all regulations are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Four children is not a centre; five is

s 2 defines the regulated service as care or education "habitually of 5 or more
children who are below 7 years of age, for a fee, reward or profit" by someone "not a
relative or guardian of all the children". Four paid children, five children minded
occasionally, six minded for free, or five grandchildren minded by their grandmother
all fall outside it. Asserted.

### 2. Much of what parents think of as "pre-school" is outside the Act

The Schedule excludes enrichment centres that "solely" teach arts, sport, recreation or
tuition; special-needs therapy centres; playgroups for under-4s of 2 hours or less;
Private Education Act institutions covering "both pre-primary and full-time primary"
levels; licensed children's homes; places of safety; and Government-operated centres
(s 3(a)(i)). An enrichment centre that also provides childcare, or an international
school offering pre-primary only, is not excluded on this reading. Asserted.

### 3. A former director can be ordered to wind down a closing centre

When a centre's licence ends or it closes, s 18(3)(c) lets the Chief Licensing Officer
direct "any person who was a key appointment holder ... in the period of 6 months
immediately preceding" the event, if no current one can comply. Directions may require
refunds to parents of deposits or fees due (s 18(4)(d)); failing to comply is an
offence ($10,000 or 12 months). Asserted for the 6-month window.

### 4. A licence dies with its holder, or with its lease

s 15(1): a licence lapses on an individual licensee's death, a body's ceasing to exist,
loss of the right to occupy the premises, or no enrolment for a prescribed period, and
s 15(2) refunds nothing. Conviction is a ground for revocation (s 16(1)(f)), not lapse.
Asserted.

### 5. Penalties are modest, and composition halves them

Unlicensed operation is $10,000 or 12 months, plus $1,000 a day after conviction
(s 6(3)); most other offences are $5,000 or 6 months, and the records offence (s 35)
is fine only. Composition is capped at the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000
(s 42), so never more than $5,000 here. The administrative financial penalty is
$5,000 per contravention (s 16(2)(g)). Asserted.

### 6. Most decisions can be appealed, but not all, and appeal does not suspend them

s 43 lists the appealable decisions; a security-deposit requirement (s 12) and a
refusal of permission to take children aged 7 to 13 (s 20(2)) are not among them. The
Minister's decision is final (s 43(10)) and the decision appealed against must be
complied with meanwhile unless the Minister directs otherwise (s 43(12)). Asserted for
the list; s 43(10) and (12) are not encoded.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The Early Childhood Development Centres Regulations (prescribed offences, periods,
  fees, criteria, compoundable offences) were not retrieved; many rules turn on them.
- s 20(2): treating a child of 14 or older as not receivable is an inference from s 20(1).
- s 16(2)(g) "for each contravention" was read as $5,000 times the count.
- No case law or MSF/ECDA guidance was searched.
