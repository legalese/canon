# israel/

Israeli legislation, one subject-id per body of law, following the subject-sidecar shape
described in [`subjects/README.md`](../README.md).

**Licence: open.** There is no copyright in Israeli statutes, regulations, Knesset records
or court decisions (Copyright Act, 5768-2007, s 6). Hebrew is the authoritative language;
English translations are unofficial. Israel publishes no free official *consolidated* text:
the official version is the original publication in Reshumot plus every amending Act, so the
consolidated Hebrew text each subject points to is a working text, not an official one.

## Topics with no statute here

- **Passenger compensation**: Only air travel has a compensation statute. The Aviation Services Law covers scheduled and charter flights to, from and within Israel, and it is genuinely strong. There is no equivalent primary statute for rail, bus or light rail: surface passenger rights are set by licence conditions and by subordinate legislation made under the Transport Ordinance (פקודת התעבורה [נוסח חדש]) and the Public Transport rules, plus Ministry of Transport service standards — regulation, not statute. Recorded as a partial gap so nobody looks for a missing Act.
- **Utility billing**: Partial gap, recorded for honesty rather than because an Act is missing. Both Acts listed are the correct primary instruments and both confer the tariff power, but the rules a consumer would actually cite in a billing dispute — meter reading, estimated bills, consumption bands, arrears, deposits, restriction and disconnection of supply — are in Electricity Authority rules (כללי משק החשמל) and Water Authority rules (כללי תאגידי מים וביוב) made under those Acts. An encoding limited to the statutes will capture the institutions and the tariff-setting duty but not the billing entitlements. Telecoms billing is a third strand again, under the Communications (Bezeq and Broadcasts) Law, which is outside this group's topics and was not verified.
- **Leave/holiday pay**: PARTIAL gap, for the 'holiday pay' component only. The two paid-leave statutes above (Annual Leave Law 5711-1951 and Sick Pay Law 5736-1976) plus the Women's Employment Law 5714-1954 for maternity/parental leave cover the topic's statutory core. But pay for public and religious holidays (דמי חגים) is NOT conferred by any Knesset statute: in Israel it comes from general collective-agreement extension orders (צווי הרחבה) made under the Collective Agreements Law, and from individual collective agreements, with the Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951 supplying only the premium rate for work actually performed on a holiday or the weekly rest. Separately, the MONEY that makes maternity/parental leave paid is a National Insurance benefit (דמי לידה) under the National Insurance Law [Consolidated Version], 5755-1995, which belongs to a social-security topic rather than this one. A complete 'holiday pay' encoding for Israel therefore needs extension orders, which are not statutes and are not in this index.
- **Payroll/wages**: PARTIAL gap, noted for completeness rather than as a missing statute. The three Acts above are the operative statutory core (payment mechanics and delayed-wage compensation; the minimum-wage formula; overtime and rest-day premium rates). Two things a payroll encoding still needs and will not find in them: (a) the current shekel minimum-wage figure, which is set by the updating mechanism together with extension orders and Ministry of Labour notices rather than printed in the Minimum Wage Law; and (b) payroll tax and social-insurance withholding, which sit in the Income Tax Ordinance [New Version], 5721-1961 and the National Insurance Law [Consolidated Version], 5755-1995 - both outside this group's four topics.
- **Work permits/immigration**: PARTIAL gap. The two Acts above are the correct operative statutes (employer duties and permit offences; visas and residence permits). But the conditions attaching to each visa class, and the annual sectoral quotas for foreign workers, are set by subordinate legislation under the Entry into Israel Law - principally the Entry into Israel Regulations, 5734-1974 and the annual quota orders (צווי הכניסה לישראל (מכסה שנתית)) - and by Population and Immigration Authority procedures (נוהלים), none of which are statutes. Anyone encoding work-permit eligibility as opposed to work-permit offences will be working mostly outside the statute book.
- **Customs/duties**: The rates of customs duty, purchase tax and the exemptions are not in any statute. They are in subordinate legislation: צו תעריף המכס והפטורים ומס קנייה על טובין, התשע״ז–2017 (Customs Tariff and Exemptions and Purchase Tax on Goods Order, 5777-2017), made under the order-making power in s 5 of the 1937 Customs Tariff and Exemption Ordinance together with s 1 of the Customs Tariffs and Excise (Variation of Tariff) Law, 5709-1949. Verified by fetching the Hebrew Wikisource page צו תעריף המכס והפטורים ומס קנייה על טובין: the page exists but is a stub gateway - it carries the instrument's name and year, states that the order is published on the Tax Authority website (מפורסם באתר רשות המסים) and links to the Customs Authority classification query system, and does NOT reproduce the rate schedules. It was last edited 25 August 2024. There is thus no consolidated free text of the operative tariff on Wikisource, and the tariff is amended by further orders frequently. Anyone encoding Israeli customs must take the schedule from the Tax Authority tariff system, and must treat the statute layer (1937 Ordinance s 3(a) charge + 1949 Law vires + Customs Ordinance [New Version] machinery) as separate from the rate layer. The 1937 Ordinance's own Schedule is marked (הושמטה) in the consolidated text for exactly this reason.
- **Personal tax/withholding**: The withholding DUTY is statutory - Income Tax Ordinance [New Version] ss 164-170 (Part X, Chapter One, ניכויים על חשבון המס), verified from the section headings on the Wikisource page - but the operative withholding mechanics are regulatory. The deduction tables, rates, thresholds, credit points, treatment of multiple employers and the employer's reporting and remittance duties sit in תקנות מס הכנסה (ניכוי ממשכורת ומשכר עבודה), התשנ״ג–1993 (ק״ת תשנ״ג, 408), verified as existing on Hebrew Wikisource by fetching that page, with parallel regulations for deduction from payments to a foreign resident, from interest and dividend, and from payments for services and assets. These are regulations (תקנות), not Acts, so they are recorded here rather than in the acts list. Practical consequence: the answer to 'does withholding sit in the Ordinance or in regulations' is BOTH, and an encoding needs the regulations to compute anything.
- **Pensions/social security**: No statute gap in the three Acts listed, but one structural gap worth recording: compulsory occupational pension coverage for employees is imposed by a general extension order (צו הרחבה) of a collective agreement under the Collective Agreements Law, 5717-1957, not by any statute. The Supervision of Financial Services (Provident Funds) Law, 5765-2005 regulates the funds; it does not compel enrolment. Not verified by fetch here because it falls outside the four topics assigned (it is a labour-law instrument), and it is flagged rather than indexed.
