# Source license

The text of **Wyoming Statutes, Title 40 - Trade and Commerce, Chapter 12 - Consumer Protection, Article 1 - In General** is published on the [Wyoming Legislature, Legislative Service Office - Wyoming Statutes (text-only NXT edition)](https://wyoleg.gov/NXT/gateway.dll?f=templates&fn=default.htm).

- **Terms**: Open in substance, but stated nowhere. The LSO site carries no copyright notice, no terms-of-use page and no licence of any kind - the footer links are Home, About the Legislature, Legislative Service Office, Contact Us, Disclaimer and Privacy Policy, and the Disclaimer page asserts no rights at all; it only disclaims official status and accuracy. Nothing on the register restricts reuse or adaptation. Under the US government edicts doctrine (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)) the text of the Wyoming Statutes as enacted is not copyrightable, so reuse and adaptation are lawful. TWO WARNINGS. (1) The free text-only edition is expressly NOT the official record: 'The information obtained from this site is not the official record, nor intended to replace the official record, of the Wyoming Legislature.' (2) The ANNOTATED edition is commercial and restricted: the LSO's own Statutes page says 'The online version of the Wyoming Statutes Annotated is provided for non-commercial use pursuant to a contractual arrangement with LexisNexis and will be updated quarterly.' That non-commercial restriction attaches to the LexisNexis-hosted annotated edition and its annotations - not to the LSO's own text-only statutes - but it means the corpus should take its Wyoming text from the LSO NXT edition or the LSO title PDFs, never from the LexisNexis site.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
