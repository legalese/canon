# NOTES — sg/penal-code-1871, encoding row `legalese`

**Status: `draft`.** One section, encoded by hand in inert style on 2026-08-26. No claim of
fidelity is made, and no human review has been sought.

## What it is

`culpable-homicide-301.l4` encodes **section 301** — *"Culpable homicide by causing the
death of a person other than the person whose death was intended"*, the Code's transferred
malice provision, as substituted by the Criminal Law Reform Act 2019 and in force since
1 January 2020.

The section is a pair of **counterfactual substitutions**, and the encoding is built to keep
that pairing visible. Two people are in play throughout, and the section names them only by
relative clause:

- **INTENDED** — "the person whose death he intended or knew himself to be likely to cause"
- **ACTUAL** — "any person whose death he neither intends nor knows himself to be likely to
  cause", i.e. the person he in fact killed

Subsection (1) runs the substitution on the **description** channel: the description borne
as against ACTUAL is replaced by the one it would have borne as against INTENDED.
Subsection (2) runs it on the **defence** channel: the accused may rely on any defence that
would have availed him as against INTENDED. Each channel therefore needs two fields on the
`Killing` record — what the law says apart from this section, and what it would have said in
the counterfactual the section points at.

`the description apart from this section` is a `MAYBE` on purpose. Whether ss 299–300
describe the killing of ACTUAL *at all*, when every fault element was aimed at INTENDED, is
precisely the question s 301 exists to close; a total function there would assert an answer
the Code does not give.

## Inert style, and what the asyndetic operators are doing

The encoding follows the inert style of the `cheating-415` files in l4-ide
(`jl4/examples/ok/inert/`): verbatim prose rides as inert string literals — identity for
their boolean context, `TRUE` under AND and `FALSE` under OR — and only the operative atoms
are lifted into named leaves. The connectives are the statute's own:

- `...` (asyndetic AND) carries the sentence's commas;
- `..` (asyndetic OR) inside the first group is the section's "**or**";
- `NOT` wrapped around a `..` group **is** the section's "**neither … nor**".

Per the 2026-08-03 inert-label ruling, inert strings here are **mortar only** — the chapeau
and the connective glue — never shadow copies of the atom beneath them. Reading the rule top
to bottom reconstructs the subsection with no word appearing twice.

## Two rules are marked NOT part of s 301

Recorded plainly so no reader takes them for statute:

1. The `ELSE` limb of the description transfer. The section says what happens when its
   circumstances are made out and is silent otherwise; the `ELSE` is the residual general
   law, supplied so the function is total.
2. `a defence or exception in law is available to the accused person`, the disjunction that
   adds the ordinary defences to the ones subsection (2) grants. "May rely on" is additive,
   not substitutive — the accused does not *lose* what he had — but that is inference from
   the wording, not the wording.

## What this row does *not* have

- **No `cases/`, `projections/`, `registers/`, `report/` or `gates/`.** The scenarios are
  inline `#EVAL`/`#ASSERT` directives in the `.l4`, not lifted into machine-evaluated dated
  case rows; nothing has been projected to DMN or BPMN; there is no fork register recording
  its interpretive choices; HG1 has not been sought and no signature exists.
- **No law-time axis.** There are no dated arms and no `EVAL UNDER RULES EFFECTIVE AT`
  coverage, so the encoding cannot answer as-at questions. It reads as the single vintage in
  force from 1 January 2020. The pre-2020 s 301 is materially different — the current
  subsection (2) did not exist — so this is a real gap, not an absence of movement to cover.
- **No encoding of ss 299–300.** The five descriptions of culpable homicide are declared as
  an ontology with an `@ref` to their source and are otherwise opaque; nothing here computes
  which one a killing bears. s 301 does not either, which is why the shape is honest, but it
  does mean the encoding cannot be run end-to-end from primary facts.
- **No encoding of s 26G.** Its subsection (1) expressly disapplies the general
  transferred-fault regime "except where section 301(1) applies", and its subsection (5) is
  the model subsection (2) here was cut from. The interlock is noted in the file header and
  nowhere enforced.

## Open questions a reviewer should look at first

1. **Is `culpable homicide by causing the death of any person` the right atom?** The section
   presupposes that culpable homicide *has been committed* against ACTUAL and then supplies
   its description. Whether that presupposition is itself satisfiable without s 301 doing
   work is the interpretive knot; the encoding takes it as a given fact of the scenario,
   which is the reading the section's own grammar suggests but not the only one available.
2. **Is the additive reading of "may rely on" in subsection (2) correct?** See above; it is
   marked as inference, and it is the one place where the encoding says more than the text.
3. **Illustration (d)** asserts that where the accused knew himself likely to kill ACTUAL,
   subsection (2) hands him nothing — the subsection (1) gate fails, so the counterfactual
   defence does not travel. That follows from the drafting, but it is the boundary a real
   dispute would be fought on and deserves a second opinion.
