**Checks:** one case file, 50 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**4 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. Most of the Act sets up and
runs the Authority (Parts 2 to 6). What an ordinary business meets is Part 7, the
import of essential construction materials such as granite and sand. This row takes
Part 7 (ss 27 to 42 and s 46), plus the s 52 composition ceiling and the s 50(4) right
to refuse an officer who will not identify himself or herself. Not encoded: the
Authority's constitution, functions, staff, finance and transfer provisions, licence
condition procedure (s 33), authorised analysts and certificate evidence (s 39),
search and seizure (s 43) apart from its penalty, forfeiture (s 44), the register
(s 45), service of documents (s 54) and every regulation.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Unwashed marine-dredged sand is not "sand"

s 27 defines "sand" to exclude "any unwashed or unprocessed marine-dredged sand or
gravel", so that material falls outside the licensing scheme unless the Minister
gazettes it. Granite and sand count only if suitable for building, street or railway
works and in short domestic supply. Material that stays on the conveyance it arrived
on, without being landed or transhipped, is not imported. Asserted.

### 2. Two layers of permission, then isolation

A business importing these materials needs a licence (s 28: up to $500,000, plus
$10,000 a day if the offence continues after conviction). Each consignment also
needs an import permit, and the whole consignment must match the permit's
description (s 29). On arrival every consignment is deemed under an isolation order
unless that was waived in writing before entry (s 36(2), (3)). While isolated it may
not be used in works, or supplied knowing it is for works (s 30). Asserted.

### 3. The s 29 defence has a notice condition

The licensee can rely on another person's act or default only after serving written
notice on the prosecutor "7 clear days before the hearing", unless the court gives
permission (s 29(4)). Asserted.

### 4. Failed material: customers must be told across a window that runs past the certificate

If a sample taken under s 36(6) fails, the licensee must notify everyone supplied
from the day the consignment entered up to the seventh day after the Authority
received the analyst's certificate, both days inclusive (s 38(5)). Asserted on day
numbers.

### 5. An appeal holds back only a financial penalty

Orders under s 40 take effect 7 days after service (s 40(5)). Under s 40(6), an
appeal suspends only a financial penalty under s 40(2)(b), until the Minister confirms
the order or the appeal is dismissed or withdrawn. The text gives revocation and
suspension no such pause. Appeals run 14 days (s 42). A revoked importer is barred
for 12 months (s 40(7)(c)). Asserted, except that month 12 itself is not tested
because the text does not settle it.

### 6. Composition is capped at the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000

s 52(1). Which offences are compoundable is left to regulations that were not
retrieved, so only the ceiling is encoded. Asserted.
