## Why this Act, and why scoped

**6 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. It is an institutional Act:
it sets up the Authority and lists its functions, but the regimes the Authority
administers (health products, tobacco, radiation) are in other Acts. This row takes
the provisions with decision content: execution under seal (s 4(3)), constitution,
terms and meetings (ss 5, 7, 9), the definitions of "health product" and "tobacco
product" (s 11(5)), the acts that need the Minister (ss 12(b), (c), 22, 41), personal
and information-service immunity (s 16), the financial year (s 18), and the three
provisions that reach the public: the symbol offence (s 38), secrecy (s 39) and
composition (s 40). Committees, delegation, staff, funds and the 2001 transfer
provisions (ss 30-36) are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Secrecy carries a smaller fine but a longer prison term than copying the logo

s 39(2): breach of secrecy, fine up to $2,000 or imprisonment up to **12 months**.
s 38(2): using a symbol identical with, or confusingly like, the Authority's, fine up
to **$10,000** or imprisonment up to 6 months. The secrecy duty binds anyone who "is
or has been" a member, officer, employee, agent or committee member, so it survives
leaving the Authority. It yields to performing duties, a court's lawful requirement,
or a written law that requires or allows the disclosure. Asserted.

### 2. "Health product" is a defined term here, and it reaches cosmetics

s 11(5) defines "health product" by two limbs: use by humans (represented, likely to
be taken, or in a class ordinarily intended for humans), and a purpose that is
therapeutic, preventive, palliative, diagnostic, **cosmetic**, or otherwise for human
health and wellbeing. The listed purposes include "cleansing, fragrancing,
deodorising, beautifying" skin, hair, nails or teeth. A shampoo is in; a veterinary
product (no human limb) and a kitchen knife (no health purpose) are out. Asserted.
Whether other Acts borrow this definition was not checked.

### 3. The functions are not enforceable in court, and good-faith staff cannot be sued personally

s 11(3): nothing in s 11 imposes "any form of duty or liability enforceable by
proceedings before any court". s 16(1) bars personal suits against members, officers,
employees and anyone acting under the Authority's direction for good-faith acts in
executing this Act "or any other written law". s 16(2) goes further for public
information services: it shields the **Authority itself** from loss caused by errors
"however caused" if made in good faith in the ordinary course of duty. s 16 is
asserted; s 11(3) is quoted in NOTES only.

### 4. The Minister's approval is needed for property, companies, outside borrowing and regulations

s 12(b) (acquiring property) and (c) (forming or joining a company, partnership or
joint venture) need the Minister's approval; s 22 needs the Minister's consent to borrow from
anyone but the Government; s 41 regulations need the Minister's approval. Contracts, fees,
joining international bodies and staff loans (whose purposes the Authority itself
approves, s 12(i)) do not. Whether the approval in s 12(b) also governs the later
power to "sell, lease, mortgage" the property is not settled by the text and is not
encoded. Asserted.

### 5. Five members are a quorum, and the presiding member votes twice on a tie

s 5: a Chairperson and 6 to 10 other members, all appointed by the Minister; s 7: terms
of up to 3 years, renewable, and resignation on at least one month's written notice.
s 9: quorum 5; decisions by majority of those present and voting; the presiding
member has a deliberative vote and, on equality, a casting vote. Treating a vote at an
inquorate meeting as not carried is an inference; the Act states no consequence, and
s 9(6) says vacancies and defective appointments do not invalidate proceedings.
Asserted.

### 6. Composition is capped at $1,000 but depends on regulations not in the deposit

s 40: the Chief Executive or an officer the Chief Executive authorises may compound an offence
"prescribed as a compoundable offence" for up to $1,000; payment bars further
proceedings. The Act prescribes none itself, so whether s 38 or s 39 offences are
compoundable turns on regulations that were not retrieved. Asserted.
