# Gas Act 2001 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, with amendments to Act 27
of 2024 (latest annotated in force 1 September 2025) shown.

**Checks:** one case file, 138 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**8 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the rules a
business, contractor, gas worker or consumer actually meets: who needs a gas licence
(s 6), who may do gas service work and who may engage them (ss 11-13), pipes on
private land (ss 30, 31), earthworks near gas pipes (s 32), damage to gas plant and
submarine pipelines (ss 32A, 32B), the meter-tampering presumption (s 47), notice of
meter connection (s 50), restoring a disconnected supply (s 52), ignition rules on the
job (s 64), officers' liability (s 89), and the penalties (ss 6, 32, 32A, 32B, 47, 49,
50, 64, 91, 92). The Authority's licensing discretion, licence modification, network
duties, entry powers, the gas importer provisions, the Gas Network Code, control of
designated licensees, severe gas shortage, competition and appeals are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Hiring a professional engineer for gas work may itself be an offence, on the words

s 11(1) lets a registered professional engineer with a practising certificate do gas
service work without a gas service worker licence, and s 11(3) exempts gas
transporters, transport agents and their employees. But s 13 makes it an offence to
employ or instruct "any person who is not a licensed gas service worker" to carry out
gas service work, with no matching carve-out. Read literally, the engineer may do the
work but whoever instructs them commits an offence. The converse gap also appears:
s 13 asks only whether the worker is "a licensed gas service worker", not whether the
licence covers the class of work, so engaging a worker licensed for a different class
is not a s 13 offence even though the worker breaches s 11. Whether a court would read
s 13 this way is not known (inference from the words only). Asserted.

### 2. Running an unlicensed gas business carries no prison term; unlicensed gas fitting does

s 6(6): conveying, retailing, shipping, importing gas or running an LNG terminal without
a licence carries a fine up to $1 million plus $25,000 a day, and **no imprisonment**.
Unlicensed gas service work (s 11) has no penalty of its own, so s 91 applies: $10,000
**or 12 months**, plus $250 a day. Asserted.

### 3. Damaging an onshore gas pipe is an offence without fault

s 32A(1)(a), (b): anyone who "removes, destroys or damages" a licensee's gas plant or
pipe commits an offence, with no mental element; the maximum is $1 million or 5 years.
The only answer is the s 32A(4) defence of reasonable precautions and due diligence,
and if that defence rests on location information from the gas transporter, it is lost
without the court's permission unless notice identifying the supplier was served on
the prosecutor 14 clear days before the hearing (s 32A(5), mirrored in s 32(9)).
Hindering a pipe without damaging it (s 32A(1)(c)) needs wilfulness or recklessness.
Submarine pipelines (s 32B) need wilfulness, recklessness or negligence, but carry
$2 million. Asserted.

### 4. Seven days' notice, unless it is an emergency

s 32: earthworks near a transporter's gas plant or pipe need 7 days' written notice
(the Authority may modify the period), the pipe's location and consultation. A person
who reasonably believes the works are necessary for safety may start at once but must
give written notice within 24 hours. The transporter on its own network is outside
s 32(1). Maximum $100,000 or 5 years. Asserted.

### 5. A broken meter seal plus custody is enough to presume tampering

s 47(4): a device near the meter, a hole not from wear and tear, a damaged or removed
licensee seal or lock, or disturbed licensee paint marking, together with custody or
control of the meter, presumes tampering "until the contrary is proved". The fine is
up to $50,000 plus 3 times the value of the gas, or 5 years. Asserted.

### 6. Smaller rules people meet

- s 6(2): conveying gas within premises one has an interest in, or reselling retailer
  gas for use there, needs no licence; shipping, retailing and town gas production need
  a licence only from a date the Minister gazettes. Asserted.
- s 30(4): a leaseholder with at least 7 years unexpired counts as an "owner" who may
  require a disused pipe to be removed at the transporter's expense. Asserted.
- s 31(3): the cost of diverting a pipe from under a structure falls on the owner or
  occupier where they built without approval, to the extent of their benefit
  otherwise, and on the transporter in all other cases. Asserted.
- s 50: 48 hours' notice before connecting or disconnecting a meter or internal pipe,
  to the shipper or retailer where one supplies the premises, otherwise to the
  transporter; $5,000. Asserted.
- s 52: supply need not be restored until the consumer leaves the premises, or both
  makes good the default and pays the reconnection expenses. Asserted.
- s 64(7): no naked flame or hotwork near a known leak, with no exception. Asserted.
- s 92: composition is capped at $3,000, but only for offences prescribed by
  regulations, which were not retrieved. Asserted (the cap only).
