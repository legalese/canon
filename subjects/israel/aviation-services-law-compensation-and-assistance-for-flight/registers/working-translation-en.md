# THE LAW

## Section 1 — Definitions
*(amended: 5780)*

In this Law —

- **"the temporary provision" (״הוראת השעה״)** — *[the consolidated text records this definition as*
  ***lapsed*** *(פקעה) and does not reproduce its words; see translator's note 2]*;
- **"benefits" (״הטבות״)** — each of the following: refund of consideration, an alternative flight
  ticket, monetary compensation and assistance services;
- **"refund of consideration" (״השבת תמורה״)** — as its meaning in section 3(a)(2);
- **"tour package" (״חבילת תיור״)** — a combination of at least two travel agency services, among them
  the booking or sale of a flight ticket;
- **"intermediate stop" (״חניית ביניים״)** — a stay at an intermediate destination for the purpose of a
  continuing flight to the final destination;
- **"flight" (״טיסה״)** — a flight taking off from the territory of the State of Israel or to it,
  including a flight that includes an intermediate stop;
- **"cancelled flight" (״טיסה שבוטלה״)** — each of the following, provided that a change in the flight
  number shall not in itself be regarded as a cancelled flight:
  - (1) a flight that did not take place;
  - (2) a flight that took off with a delay of at least eight hours from the time stated in the flight
    ticket, or with a delay prescribed under section 6(h);
- **"final destination" (״יעד סופי״)** — the destination stated in the flight ticket on a direct
  flight, or — on a flight that includes an intermediate stop — the last destination stated in the
  flight ticket;
- **"flight ticket" (״כרטיס טיסה״)** — a confirmation of an undertaking to carry a passenger by air,
  issued by a flight operator, an organiser or a person authorised by them for that purpose, even if
  the confirmation was issued as part of a tour package; for this purpose, "confirmation" — including
  in an electronic message;
- **"alternative flight ticket" (״כרטיס טיסה חלופי״)** — as its meaning in section 3(a)(3);
- **"organiser" (״מארגן״)** — a person who hires capacity in an aircraft, in whole or in part, for the
  carriage of passengers and their baggage, for the purpose of selling it to others;
- **"flight operator" (״מפעיל טיסה״)** — an operator of an aircraft for the purpose of carrying
  passengers and their baggage, for consideration, from the State of Israel, to it, or within its
  territory;
- **"travel agency services provider" (״נותן שירותי סוכנות נסיעות״)** — a person who provides, for
  consideration, or not for consideration but on a regular basis, a service among travel agency
  services;
- **"monetary compensation" (״פיצוי כספי״)** — as its meaning in section 3(a)(4) or (5), as the case
  may be;
- **"qualifying ground" (״עילה מזכה״)** — a ground entitling a passenger to receive benefits as stated
  in sections 5 to 9;
- **"the Airports Authority" (״רשות שדות התעופה״)** — the Airports Authority established under the
  Airports Authority Law, 5737-1977 (חוק רשות שדות התעופה, התשל״ז–1977);
- **"travel agency services" (״שירותי סוכנות נסיעות״)** — as defined in the Tourism Services Law,
  5736-1976 (חוק שירותי תיירות, התשל״ו–1976);
- **"assistance services" (״שירותי סיוע״)** — services to a passenger listed in section 3(a)(1);
- **"the Minister" (״השר״)** — the Minister of Transport, National Infrastructures and Road Safety.

## Section 2 — Conditions for entitlement to benefits

**(a)** A passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued for a flight in respect of which a
qualifying ground exists is entitled to the benefits, in whole or in part, if he presented himself on
time at the check-in counter (Check-in Counter) of the flight operator at the airport, and if no such
check-in counter was allocated for that flight — presented himself on time before a person appointed on
behalf of the flight operator under section 8C(a1) of the Aviation Services Licensing Law, 5723-1963
(סעיף 8ג(א1) לחוק רישוי שירותי התעופה, התשכ״ג–1963); however, a passenger is not required to present
himself on time as stated, if the flight was cancelled; in this subsection, **"presenting himself on
time" (״התייצבות במועד״)** — presenting himself at the time prescribed for that purpose and brought to
the passenger's knowledge by the flight operator, the organiser or the travel agency services provider,
provided that the passenger shall not be required to present himself more than three hours before the
time scheduled for the take-off of the flight, and if no such time was prescribed — at least 90 minutes
before the said take-off time.

**(b)** Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (a), a passenger shall not be entitled to
benefits —

- (1) if he received notice from the flight operator, the organiser or the travel agency services
  provider concerning his transfer to a flight other than that for which the flight ticket was issued
  to him, and he reached his final destination at the time that was stated in the original flight
  ticket;
- (2) if he received the flight ticket without payment, or if the flight ticket was purchased at a
  special fare not made available to the public directly or indirectly, unless the ticket was issued to
  him by virtue of his membership in a benefits programme of a flight operator or an organiser.

## Section 3 — Types of benefits
*(amended: 5780, 5780-2, 5781)*

**(a)** The benefits under this Law are:

- **(1)** assistance services without payment, as detailed below:
  - (a) food and beverages in accordance with the waiting time (in this Law — **food and beverages**);
  - (b) accommodation in a hotel if a stay of one night or more is required, or if a stay longer than
    the stay planned by the passenger is required (in this Law — **accommodation services**);
  - (c) transport services between the airport and the hotel at which the passenger stays as stated in
    sub-paragraph (b), and if the passenger chose to stay at another place within a reasonable distance
    from the airport, during the period of time stated in that sub-paragraph — transport services
    between that place and the airport (in this Law — **transport services**);
  - (d) two telephone calls, and also the sending of a message by facsimile or electronic mail at the
    passenger's choice (in this Law — **communication services**);
- **(2)** refund of consideration in the amount paid for a flight ticket, including every payment paid
  for the flight ticket, and among these fees, levies, taxes and other compulsory payments, all subject
  to the provisions of subsection (b); a flight operator or an organiser shall refund the consideration
  to the passenger **within 21 days** from the day on which the passenger or the travel agency services
  provider as stated in section 15, as the case may be, applied to it in writing;
- **(2A)** *[the consolidated text records this paragraph as* ***lapsed*** *(פקעה) and does not
  reproduce its words; see translator's note 3]*;
- **(3)** an alternative flight ticket to the passenger's final destination, on conditions as similar as
  possible to the conditions of the original flight ticket issued to him and at the earliest time
  possible, or at a later time in coordination with the passenger, and subject to the existence of
  vacant places on the flight;
- **(4)** monetary compensation as stated in the First Schedule; a flight operator or an organiser
  shall pay the said compensation to the passenger **within 45 days** from the day on which the
  passenger or the travel agency services provider as stated in section 15, as the case may be, applied
  to it in writing; the compensation shall be paid in cash, by bank transfer or by cheque, and if the
  passenger consented thereto expressly and in writing — by travellers' cheques or by another means of
  payment as agreed with him;
- **(5)** monetary compensation on account of a change in the conditions of the flight ticket, as
  stated in the Second Schedule; a flight operator or an organiser shall pay the said compensation to
  the passenger **within 21 days from the date of the flight**, in cash, by bank transfer or by cheque,
  and if the passenger consented thereto expressly and in writing — by travellers' cheques or by
  another means of payment as agreed with him.

**(b)** As regards refund of consideration —

- **(1)** where a flight ticket was purchased for a flight that includes an intermediate stop which is
  operated by the same flight operator, and a qualifying ground occurred on a segment of the flight —
  the passenger shall be entitled to receive the full consideration paid for the flight ticket even if
  a segment of the flight was actually performed, provided that the passenger did not reach the final
  destination; where the passenger reached the intermediate destination on such a flight and decided not
  to fly to his original final destination on account of the qualifying ground, he shall be entitled to
  receive from the flight operator, without consideration, also a flight ticket back to the point of
  origin, in addition to the refund of consideration;
- **(2)** where a return flight ticket was purchased, including a return flight ticket for a flight that
  includes an intermediate stop, and a qualifying ground occurred on the outbound flight from the point
  of origin — the passenger shall be entitled to receive the full consideration paid for the flight
  ticket; where a qualifying ground occurred on the flight back from the destination to the point of
  origin — the passenger shall be entitled to receive **half** the consideration paid for the flight
  ticket; as regards such a flight ticket for a flight that includes an intermediate stop — where the
  qualifying ground occurred on the outbound flight, and the passenger reached the intermediate
  destination, and because of the qualifying ground decided not to fly to his original final destination
  on account of the qualifying ground, he shall be entitled to receive from the flight operator, without
  consideration, also a flight ticket back to the point of origin, in addition to the refund of
  consideration;
- **(3)** where a flight ticket was issued to a passenger as part of a tour package, its price shall be
  calculated in accordance with the distance of the flight on which the qualifying ground occurred, the
  type of the flight and the type of the service class in which the passenger flew on the flight, all as
  detailed in the Third Schedule, unless it was proved that a different amount was paid for the flight
  ticket;
- **(4)** subject to the provisions of paragraphs (1) and (2), a passenger shall not be entitled to a
  refund of consideration if he chose to receive an alternative flight ticket or if he chose to fly on
  the flight on which the qualifying ground occurred, provided that if he was offered a flight ticket
  for a flight from another airport and he accepted that offer — he shall also be entitled to
  reimbursement of expenses on account of transport to that airport.

**(c)** In subsection (b) —

- **"intermediate stop" (״חניית ביניים״)** — a stay at an intermediate destination, for **not more than
  24 hours**, for the purpose of a continuing flight to the final destination;
- **"return flight ticket" (״כרטיס טיסה הלוך ושוב״)** — a flight ticket for a flight taking off from a
  point of origin to a particular destination and also for a flight from that destination back to the
  point of origin, provided that the flights are operated by the same flight operator;
- **"flight operator" (״מפעיל טיסה״)** — the flight operator that issued the flight ticket, including an
  organiser or any person on their behalf.

## Section 4 — Liability of a flight operator and of an organiser

A flight operator or an organiser from whom the passenger receives a service shall be regarded, even if
there is no agreement between it and the passenger, as the person performing, on behalf of the person
with whom the passenger contracted, the duties imposed under this Law.

## Section 5 — Refusal to carry a passenger on a flight
*(amended: 5780)*

**(a)** A flight operator or an organiser that refuses to carry a passenger to whom a flight ticket has
been issued, including on account of overbooking, shall first approach the passengers registered on the
flight and check whether there is among them a passenger willing to give up his place on the flight in
return for consideration to be agreed between that passenger and the flight operator or the organiser;
in this subsection, **"overbooking" (״רישום יתר״)** — the registration of booked places in an aircraft,
confirmed by a flight operator, an organiser or any person on their behalf, in a number exceeding the
capacity of the aircraft.

**(b)** A passenger whom a flight operator or an organiser refused to carry and who did not agree to
give up his place on the flight in return for agreed consideration as stated in subsection (a), is
entitled to receive from the flight operator or from the organiser assistance services and monetary
compensation as stated in the First Schedule, and also, at the passenger's choice — a refund of
consideration or an alternative flight ticket.

**(c)** Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (b), a flight operator or an organiser that offered
the passenger an alternative flight ticket, and the passenger accepted that offer, may **reduce by half**
the amount of the monetary compensation to which the passenger is entitled under the provisions of that
subsection, except in the circumstances stated in section 6(d), provided that the delay in the time of
landing at the passenger's final destination as against the original time for landing at that
destination is as detailed below:

- (1) up to four hours — if the distance of the flight does not exceed 2,000 km;
- (2) up to five hours — if the distance of the flight exceeds 2,000 km and does not exceed 4,500 km;
- (3) up to six hours — if the distance of the flight exceeds 4,500 km.

**(d)** The provisions of this section shall not apply if, on security grounds, on account of the
passenger's state of health or on account of a concern for harm to the safety of the flight, the
passenger cannot be carried on the flight, or if he does not have proper travel documents in his
possession.

**(e)** Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (d), a passenger shall be entitled to receive from
a flight operator or an organiser that refused to carry him on security grounds as stated in that
subsection, monetary compensation under subsection (b), provided that all of the following are
satisfied in his case:

- (1) he arrived at the airport at least three hours before the time of take-off stated in the flight
  ticket or the scheduled time of the flight;
- (2) he cooperated in the security check conducted in respect of him, and at the conclusion of the
  check it was found that he could be carried;
- (3) his travel documents are in order.

## Section 6 — Cancelled flight
*(amended: 5780, 5780-2, 5781)*

**(a)** A passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued for a flight that was cancelled shall be
entitled to receive from the flight operator or from the organiser the following benefits:

- (1) assistance services;
- (2) a refund of consideration or an alternative flight ticket, at the passenger's choice;
- (3) monetary compensation as stated in the First Schedule.

**(a1)** *[the consolidated text records this subsection as* ***lapsed*** *(פקע) and does not reproduce
its words; see translator's note 3]*.

**(b)** A flight operator or an organiser that offered the passenger an alternative flight ticket as
stated in subsection (a)(2), and the passenger accepted that offer, may **reduce by half** the amount of
the monetary compensation to which the passenger is entitled under the provisions of subsection (a)(3),
provided that the delay in the time of landing at the passenger's final destination as against the
original time for landing at that destination is as detailed below:

- (1) up to two hours — if the flight is over a distance not exceeding 2,000 km;
- (2) up to three hours — if the flight is over a distance not exceeding 4,500 km;
- (3) up to four hours — if the flight is over a distance exceeding 4,500 km.

**(c)** Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (a), a passenger whose flight was cancelled shall
not be entitled to monetary compensation as stated in the First Schedule, if the flight operator or the
organiser proved that the passenger received notice thereof from the flight operator, from the organiser
or from the travel agency services provider —

- **(1)** at least 14 days before the time of the flight stated in the flight ticket;
- **(2)** between seven and 14 days before the time of the flight stated in the flight ticket, and the
  flight operator or the organiser offered him an alternative flight whose time of take-off is at most
  two hours before the time of the flight stated in the original flight ticket, and whose time of
  landing at the final destination is not later than four hours after the original time for landing at
  that destination;
- **(3)** less than seven days before the time of the flight stated in the flight ticket, and the flight
  operator or the organiser offered him an alternative flight whose time of take-off is at most one hour
  before the time of the flight stated in the original flight ticket, and whose time of landing at the
  final destination is not later than two hours from the original time for landing at that destination.

**(d)** The provisions of subsection (c) shall not apply if the passenger refused to fly on an
alternative flight offered to him because the offer was not made to a person joining him on the flight
according to the passenger's notice, or because the passenger was prevented from flying on the
alternative flight on grounds of security, religion or a medical limitation.

**(e)** Without derogating from the provisions of subsection (c), a passenger whose flight was cancelled
shall not be entitled to monetary compensation as stated in the First Schedule, if the flight operator
or the organiser proved that one of the following was satisfied:

- (1) the flight was cancelled on account of special circumstances that were not within its control,
  and even had it done everything in its power — it could not have prevented its cancellation on account
  of those circumstances;
- (2) the flight was cancelled on account of a protected strike or lockout;
- (3) the flight was cancelled in order to avoid desecration of the Sabbath or a festival.

**(f)** A flight operator or an organiser that notifies a passenger of a cancelled flight shall present
to him alternatives for reaching the final destination.

**(g)** The burden of proof concerning the giving of the notice to the passenger of a cancelled flight
shall lie on the flight operator or on the organiser.

**(h)** The Minister, with the consent of the Minister of Industry, Trade and Employment, may increase
or reduce the number of hours stated in the definition "cancelled flight", provided that he shall not
change the number of hours by more than four hours as against the number so stated; if the Minister
reduced the number of hours, he shall prescribe adjustment provisions as regards benefits on account of
a flight that took off late.

## Section 7 — Flight that took off late

**(a)** A passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued for a flight that took off with a delay of
at least two hours from the time stated in the flight ticket is entitled to receive from the flight
operator or from the organiser food and beverages and communication services.

**(b)** Without derogating from the provisions of subsection (a), a passenger to whom a flight ticket has
been issued for a flight that took off with a delay of five hours and less than eight hours from the
time stated in the flight ticket is entitled to a refund of consideration or to an alternative flight
ticket, at his choice; a passenger who was offered an alternative flight ticket for a flight due to take
off on the following day and who chose to accept it, is entitled to receive also accommodation services
and transport services; however, if the flight took off with such a delay on account of a protected
strike or lockout, the passenger shall be entitled to a refund of consideration and also to food and
beverages and to communication services, only.

## Section 8 — Flight whose time of take-off was brought forward
*(amended: 5780)*

**(a)** A passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued for a flight whose time of take-off was
brought forward by more than five hours and not more than eight hours, and who received notice thereof
from the flight operator, from the organiser or from the travel agency service provider less than 14 days
before the time of the flight stated in the flight ticket, shall be entitled to a refund of
consideration or to an alternative flight ticket, at his choice.

**(b)** A passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued for a flight whose time of take-off was
brought forward by more than eight hours, and who received notice thereof as stated in subsection (a),
shall be entitled to the following benefits:

- (1) monetary compensation as stated in the First Schedule, except in the circumstances stated in
  section 6(e)(1) and (2);
- (2) a refund of consideration or an alternative flight ticket, at his choice.

## Section 9 — Change in the conditions of the flight ticket

**(a)** Where a flight operator or an organiser decided to transfer a passenger to a class of a higher
level than that stated in the flight ticket issued to him, it shall not demand additional payment from
the passenger on account of such a transfer.

**(b)** Where a flight operator or an organiser decided to transfer a passenger to a class of a lower
level than that stated in the flight ticket issued to him, it shall pay the passenger monetary
compensation on account of a change in the conditions of the flight ticket, as stated in the Second
Schedule, in an amount to be calculated as a percentage of the price of the flight ticket that the
passenger paid, and if the flight includes an intermediate stop — in an amount equal to the price paid
for the flight ticket multiplied by the ratio between the distance of the flight on which the passenger
was transferred to a class of a lower level as stated and the total distance of the flight; where the
flight ticket was purchased as part of a tour package — the price of the flight ticket for the purposes
of this subsection shall be calculated under the provisions of section 3(b)(3).

## Section 9A — Change of the benefits to a passenger on account of a special situation
*(amended: 5785)*

**(a)** Where a special situation has been declared and the declaration has been in force for more than
14 days, the Minister may, after consultation with the Minister of Economy and Industry and with the
approval of the Economic Affairs Committee of the Knesset, prescribe, by order, as regards a flight the
time stated in the flight ticket issued in respect of which falls within a period that he shall
prescribe (in this section — **the determining period** (״התקופה הקובעת״)), one or more of the following
provisions:

- **(1)** that the accommodation services to which a passenger is entitled under the provisions of this
  Law, except section 5, shall be limited to a number of nights that the Minister shall prescribe and
  which shall **not be fewer than two**, provided that he is satisfied that, on account of the special
  situation, there is a significant impairment of the supply of passenger flights from Israel and to it;
- **(2)** that the entitlement to an alternative flight ticket which a flight operator or an organiser is
  obliged to offer for the passenger's choice on account of a flight cancellation as stated in section 6,
  shall apply with such modifications as he shall prescribe, in order to assist the passenger to reach
  the final destination stated in the original flight ticket issued to him on conditions as similar as
  possible to the conditions of the original flight ticket issued to him, and at a time as close as
  possible to the time stated in the original flight ticket, including an entitlement to a flight ticket
  from a different point of origin or to a different destination from those stated in the original flight
  ticket; if the Minister prescribed an order under this paragraph, he shall prescribe in it provisions
  as regards the duty of a flight operator or an organiser to notify the passenger clearly of his
  entitlement to choose between a refund of consideration and an alternative flight ticket under this
  paragraph;
- **(3)** one of the following:
  - **(a)** that the period of advance notice of the cancellation of a flight or of bringing it forward
    by more than eight hours, on account of which a passenger is not entitled to monetary compensation as
    stated in sections 6(c)(1) and 8(b)(1), shall be **less than 14 days and not less than three days**,
    provided that the Minister is satisfied that, on account of the special situation, there is a
    significant impairment of the ability of flight operators or organisers to plan a flight schedule,
    including to change the planned flight schedule;
  - **(b)** that there shall be **no entitlement to monetary compensation** of a passenger whose flight
    was cancelled or brought forward as detailed in the provisions under sections 6(a)(3), 8(b)(1) or 18,
    provided that the Minister is satisfied that, on account of the special situation, there is a
    significant impairment of the ability of flight operators or organisers to plan a flight schedule,
    including to change the planned flight schedule, over a time span of two weeks in advance, and that,
    in view of the severity of the said impairment, shortening the period of advance notice as stated in
    sub-paragraph (a) is not sufficient.

**(b)** An order as stated in subsection (a) may be prescribed in respect of all flights or some of them.

**(c)** As regards the determining period the following provisions shall apply:

- (1) it shall apply during the period in which a special situation has been declared, and it may apply
  from the day of its commencement;
- (2) it shall not exceed **60 days**, and as regards the denial of compensation under subsection
  (a)(3)(b) — shall not exceed **45 days**;
- (3) the Minister may extend it where the conditions in subsection (a) are satisfied, and the provisions
  of this section shall apply to the extension;
- (4) where the declaration of the special situation was cancelled or lapsed, the determining period
  shall end on the day of the cancellation or the lapse, as the case may be; the Director General of the
  Ministry of Transport and Road Safety shall publish on the Ministry's internet site and in Reshumot a
  notice of the date of the end of the determining period;
- (5) where the circumstances on account of which the order under subsection (a) was prescribed have
  ceased to exist, the Minister shall prescribe, by order, the end of the determining period without
  delay; an order under this paragraph does not require consultation with the Minister of Economy and
  Industry or the approval of the Economic Affairs Committee of the Knesset.

**(d)** Where an order under subsection (a) was prescribed, and the time of the flight stated in the
flight ticket, which was issued to the passenger before the prescribing of the order, falls within the
determining period, the following bodies shall give notice to the passenger of the changes under the
order in the benefits to which he is entitled:

- (1) the flight operator or organiser that issued the flight ticket to the passenger;
- (2) the travel agency services provider that sold the flight ticket to the passenger, if it received
  notice of the said changes from the flight operator or from the organiser.

**(e)** In this section, **"special situation" (״מצב מיוחד״)** — each of the following:

- (1) a special home-front situation (מצב מיוחד בעורף) under section 9C of the Civil Defence Law,
  5711-1951 (סעיף 9ג לחוק ההתגוננות האזרחית, התשי״א–1951);
- (2) a civil emergency event (אירוע חירום אזרחי) under section 90B of the Police Ordinance
  [New Version], 5731-1971 (סעיף 90ב לפקודת המשטרה [נוסח חדש], התשל״א–1971);
- (3) a state of emergency prescribed under a law for the purpose of dealing with a dangerous infectious
  disease.

## Section 10 — Issuing a flight ticket for a flight whose times were not approved in advance

**(a)** A flight operator, an organiser or a person authorised by them for that purpose shall not issue
to a passenger a flight ticket in which the time of the flight stated is not the time that the Airports
Authority approved in advance and by reason of which the flight was not due to take off at the time so
stated, unless —

- (1) the passenger received notice of the approved flight time from the flight operator, from the
  organiser, or from a person authorised by them for that purpose, at least three months before that
  time;
- (2) the Airports Authority approved retroactively the time of the flight stated in the flight ticket.

**(b)** The provisions of this section shall not apply to a travel agency services provider that is not
an organiser.

## Section 11 — Exemplary damages
*(amended: [Yalkut HaPirsumim notices])*

**(a)** The court may award to a passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued damages that are not
dependent on damage (in this section — **exemplary damages** (״פיצויים לדוגמה״)), in an amount not
exceeding **10,000 new shekels** *[base figure as enacted in 2012; the consolidation states that in 2026
the figure is NIS 12,240 — verified against the official Yalkut notice, see "Amounts as at 2026"]*, if
it found that one of the following was done knowingly:

- **(1)** a flight operator or an organiser —
  - (a) did not give benefits to a passenger whom it refused to carry, contrary to the provisions of
    section 5;
  - (b) did not give benefits to a passenger whose flight was cancelled, contrary to the provisions of
    section 6;
  - (c) did not give benefits to a passenger whose flight took off late, contrary to the provisions of
    section 7;
  - (d) did not give benefits to a passenger whose flight's time of take-off was brought forward,
    contrary to the provisions of section 8;
  - (e) did not give monetary compensation on account of a change in the conditions of the flight ticket
    as stated in the Second Schedule, to a passenger who was transferred to a class of a level lower
    than that stated in his flight ticket, contrary to the provisions of section 9(b);
- **(2)** a flight operator, an organiser or a person authorised by them for that purpose issued to a
  passenger a flight ticket in which the time of the flight stated is not the time that the Airports
  Authority approved in advance, and by reason of which the flight was not due to take off at the time
  so stated, contrary to the provisions of section 10, and it was cancelled or took off with a
  divergence exceeding two hours from the time stated in that flight ticket.

**(b)** In coming to determine the amount of the exemplary damages, the court shall have regard, among
other things, to the considerations detailed below, and shall not have regard to the amount of the damage
caused to the passenger as a result of the cancellation of the flight or the change in its time of
take-off or in its conditions:

- (1) enforcement of the Law and deterrence from its breach;
- (2) encouraging the passenger to realise his rights;
- (3) the fact that the breach is a repeated breach;
- (4) the gravity of the breach and its circumstances;
- (5) the monetary value of the transaction in connection with which the breach was committed.

**(c)** Nothing in the provisions of this section shall derogate from the right of a passenger to
benefits on account of that same breach.

## Section 12 — Calculation of distances

The distances stated in this Law shall be calculated in accordance with the shortest distance between
two points on the surface of a sphere.

## Section 13 — Updating of amounts

The amounts stated in this Law shall be updated on **1 January of each year** (in this section — **the
updating day**) in accordance with the rate of increase of the new index as against the base index, and
shall be **rounded to the nearest amount that is a multiple of ten new shekels**; the Director General of
the Ministry of Transport, National Infrastructures and Road Safety shall publish the updated amounts in
a notice in Reshumot and on the Ministry's internet site; in this subsection —

- **"the new index" (״המדד החדש״)** — the index last published in the month of November preceding the
  updating day;
- **"the base index" (״המדד הבסיסי״)** — the index published in the month of January 2012;
- **"index" (״מדד״)** — the consumer price index published by the Central Bureau of Statistics.

## Section 14 — Duties of information and disclosure

**(a)**

- **(1)** A flight operator or an organiser shall display, at a place where it receives the public, a
  notice detailing the passenger's right to benefits in the case of a refusal to carry him on a flight,
  a delay of a flight, the bringing forward of a flight or its cancellation (in this section —
  **information for the passenger** (״מידע לנוסע״)), at a conspicuous place and in clear and legible
  letters.
- **(2)** A flight operator, an organiser and a travel agency services provider that offer flight tickets
  for sale shall publish information for the passenger on their internet sites, if any, in a prominent
  manner.
- **(3)** The holder of a licence to operate an airport under the Aviation Law, 5771-2011 (חוק הטיס,
  התשע״א–2011), shall place at every airport that it operates signage displaying information for the
  passenger, at a conspicuous place, in clear and legible letters.
- **(4)** The Minister may prescribe provisions as regards information for the passenger, and among
  these as regards the wording of the notices and the signage as stated in this subsection.

**(b)** A passenger to whom a flight ticket has been issued for a flight in respect of which a qualifying
ground exists is entitled to receive from the flight operator or from the organiser a document detailing
his right to benefits.

**(c)** A flight operator or an organiser that issues a flight ticket to a passenger shall give him
particulars concerning the location at the airport of the person appointed under the provisions of
section 8C(a1) of the Aviation Services Licensing Law, 5723-1963 (סעיף 8ג(א1) לחוק רישוי שירותי התעופה,
התשכ״ג–1963), for the purpose of assisting passengers to realise their rights in accordance with the
provisions of this Law, and concerning the means of contacting him; the duty to give such particulars
shall apply also to a travel agency services provider that sells a flight ticket to a passenger, provided
that it received the particulars from a flight operator or from the organiser.

## Section 15 — Assistance of a travel agency services provider in obtaining benefits

## Section 17 — Contracting out — in the passenger's favour

# Translator's notes for the encoders

## A. Ambiguities and places where the English cannot carry the Hebrew

1. **"השבת תמורה" — "refund of consideration".** The Hebrew is "restoration of the consideration", a
   contract-law phrase, not a consumer "refund". Section 3(a)(2) defines it as the sum paid for the
   ticket *including* fees, levies, taxes and other compulsory payments. Encode it as the gross ticket
   price, not the fare component. Do not treat the English word "refund" as importing any additional
   condition.
2. **"הוראת השעה" (s 1) is a defined term whose definition has lapsed.** The consolidation prints only
   "(פקעה)" — expired. The words of the definition are not available from the consolidated text and are
   **not** reconstructed here. If the encoding needs historic COVID-era behaviour, the three 5780/5781
   amending Acts must be read directly (PDFs listed in the header).
3. **Two further lapsed units: s 3(a)(2A) and s 6(a1).** Same position — the consolidation prints
   "(פקעה)" / "(פקע)" with no text. Encode section 3(a) as a list with a gap at (2A), and section 6 with a
   gap at (a1); do **not** renumber, because sections 9A(a)(3) and others cross-refer by number.
4. **Section 9A(a)(3)(a) cross-refers to "sections 6(c)(1) and 8(b)(1)" as the source of the 14-day
   advance-notice period.** Section 6(c)(1) does state 14 days. Section 8(b)(1) does **not**: the 14-day
   condition for a brought-forward flight sits in section 8(a), which section 8(b) incorporates by the
   words "received notice thereof as stated in subsection (a)". The citation in 9A is therefore loose.
   Decide explicitly whether the ministerial power shortens the 14 days in section 8(a) (the only place it
   appears) or is inoperative as to section 8.
5. **Two different Ministers, and one office that no longer exists.** Section 6(h) requires the *consent*
   of the "Minister of Industry, Trade and Employment" (שר התעשייה המסחר והתעסוקה) — an office renamed
   Minister of Economy and Industry. Section 9A(a) requires *consultation* with the "Minister of Economy
   and Industry" (שר הכלכלה והתעשייה). Consent and consultation are different legal tests; do not merge
   them. Whether the section 6(h) reference now reads as the Minister of Economy and Industry is a
   question of the Interpretation Law and government-transfer-of-powers orders, not of this text.
6. **The Minister's own name is inconsistent across the Law.** Section 1 and section 13 say "Minister /
   Ministry of Transport, National Infrastructures and Road Safety"; section 9A(c)(4) and the Iron Swords
   provision say "Ministry / Minister of Transport and Road Safety". Treat these as the same office; the
   difference is drafting history, not two offices.
7. **Section 3(b)(2), return tickets: "half the consideration" for the inbound leg.** The Hebrew fixes a
   flat one-half, irrespective of the actual fare split between legs. Encode the literal one-half, not a
   pro-rata apportionment. Contrast section 9(b), which *does* use a distance-ratio apportionment for an
   intermediate-stop downgrade.
8. **"חניית ביניים" is defined twice, differently.** In section 1 it has **no time limit**. In section
   3(c), for the purposes of section 3(b) only, it is capped at **24 hours**. Both definitions must exist
   in the encoding, scoped correctly, or refunds for long stopovers will come out wrong.
9. **Section 3(b)(2) contains a redundancy in the Hebrew** — "because of the qualifying ground decided not
   to fly to his original final destination on account of the qualifying ground" (בשל העילה המזכה ... עקב
   העילה המזכה). This is duplicated drafting, not two separate conditions. One causal condition only.
10. **"פיצויים לדוגמה" — rendered "exemplary damages".** The Hebrew is literally "damages by way of
    example"; the statutory gloss is "damages that are not dependent on damage". Section 11(b) *forbids*
    the court from having regard to the actual loss. This is not common-law exemplary damages and is not a
    liquidated sum: it is a discretionary cap-bounded award. Encode as a range 0 to the cap, gated on
    knowledge ("ביודעין" — knowingly), with the five listed considerations as inputs, not as conditions.
11. **Section 5(c) halving is excluded "in the circumstances stated in section 6(d)".** Section 6(d)
    concerns a passenger who refused an alternative flight for companion, security, religious or medical
    reasons. Importing it into the refusal-to-carry section is awkward but is what the Hebrew says. Do not
    silently substitute section 6(e).
12. **Section 5(e) partially reverses section 5(d).** Section 5(d) disapplies the whole section on
    security, health or flight-safety grounds, or for improper travel documents. Section 5(e) then restores
    *monetary compensation only* (not assistance services, not a refund, not an alternative ticket) where
    the refusal was on **security** grounds and all three of its conditions hold — including condition (2),
    that the security check concluded that the passenger *could* be carried. That condition makes
    section 5(e) apply to a narrow case: a passenger cleared by security but still refused carriage.
13. **Section 5(e)(1): "three hours at least" sits beside section 2(a)'s "not more than three hours".**
    Section 2(a) caps what an operator may demand at three hours; section 5(e)(1) requires the passenger to
    have arrived at least three hours ahead to keep security-refusal compensation. They are consistent but
    pull in opposite directions; encode both.
14. **Section 6(b) thresholds are expressed differently from section 5(c).** Section 5(c) bands are
    "does not exceed 2,000" / "exceeds 2,000 and does not exceed 4,500" / "exceeds 4,500" — mutually
    exclusive. Section 6(b) bands read "not exceeding 2,000" / "not exceeding 4,500" / "exceeding 4,500" —
    (1) and (2) **overlap** for a flight under 2,000 km. Read sequentially (first match wins), as in the
    Schedules. Flag this as a deliberate encoding decision.
15. **Section 6(c)(2) "between seven and 14 days" (בין שבעה ל־14 ימים).** Whether the endpoints are
    inclusive is not stated. Paragraph (1) covers "at least 14 days" and paragraph (3) "less than seven
    days", so 7 and 14 are already accounted for at the boundaries; the natural reading is that (2) covers
    7 ≤ n < 14. State the chosen convention in the encoding.
16. **Section 6(e)(1), "special circumstances that were not within its control".** No definition, no
    list, and no "extraordinary circumstances" recital of the kind EU Regulation 261/2004 carries. This is
    an open-textured defence and must be modelled as an external fact, not derived.
17. **Section 6(e)(3), Sabbath/festival cancellations, has no EU analogue** and is a complete answer to
    compensation. Note that sections 6(e)(1) and (2) are excluded from section 8(b)(1) compensation but
    **6(e)(3) is not** — a flight brought forward more than eight hours to avoid Sabbath desecration still
    attracts compensation. Confirm this asymmetry is intended before smoothing it.
18. **Section 7(b) has a gap.** Section 7(a) bites at a two-hour delay; section 7(b) at "five hours and
    less than eight hours"; at eight hours the flight becomes a "cancelled flight" under section 1 and
    section 6 applies instead. Between two and five hours only food, beverages and communication services
    are owed. There is no provision at all for a delay of exactly the eight-hour boundary being treated
    twice — it falls to section 6. Encode the boundary explicitly.
19. **Section 8(a) window is "more than five hours and not more than eight hours".** A flight brought
    forward by five hours or less gives no entitlement at all under section 8. Do not round.
20. **Section 9(b) percentages describe class *pairs*, and the fourth row overlaps the second and third.**
    First→business 60%, business→tourist 80%, first→tourist 90%, and first-or-business→tourist over
    4,500 km 100%. For a first→tourist downgrade over 4,500 km both row 3 (90%) and row 4 (100%) match.
    The distance-qualified row must take precedence; encode row order or an explicit specificity rule.
21. **Section 9(b) does not say what happens to a downgrade between classes not listed** (for example
    premium economy, or business→premium economy). The Second Schedule is closed. Treat an unlisted
    downgrade as outside the Schedule and flag it.
22. **Section 4 makes a non-contracting operator perform another's duties.** It is a deeming provision
    creating a performance obligation, not a transfer of the contractual relationship. Encode as an
    additional duty-bearer, not as a substitution.
23. **Section 20 bars only benefits "of the same type" already received under foreign law.** So a
    passenger who received EU 261 cash compensation is not thereby barred from Israeli assistance
    services. "Type" must be encoded at the level of the four benefit categories in the section 1
    definition of "benefits", not at the level of a single money claim.
24. **Section 17 (contracting out only in the passenger's favour) and section 16 (no derogation from any
    law) both act as one-way ratchets** and will need to be represented as constraints on the encoding
    rather than as computable rules.
25. **Section 2(b)(2), "a special fare not made available to the public directly or indirectly".** Vague
    and fact-dependent; keep as an external predicate. The frequent-flyer proviso is a carve-back *into*
    entitlement.
26. **Section 12 distance rule is a great-circle distance** ("the shortest distance between two points on
    the surface of a sphere"). No Earth radius, no ellipsoid, no rounding rule is given, and the Law does
    not say whether a multi-segment itinerary is measured segment by segment or origin to final
    destination. Section 9(b) implies segment-and-total measurement; the Schedules say "one-way flight
    distance". Pick a convention and record it.
27. **Section 13 rounding is to the nearest multiple of NIS 10**, with no stated tie-break at an exact
    NIS 5 midpoint.
28. **The Hebrew definitions in section 1 are in Hebrew alphabetical order**, which this translation
    preserves; the resulting English order is not alphabetical. Two are out of Hebrew order too
    ("פיצוי כספי" before "עילה מזכה", and "השר" placed last). No legal significance.
29. **Section 23 has no text in the consolidation** because it was a textual amendment folded into the
    Aviation Services Licensing Law, 5723-1963. Keep the number as a placeholder; do not encode a rule for
    it.
30. **Gender.** The Hebrew uses masculine forms throughout for the passenger; this is the ordinary
    statutory convention and carries no restriction. The masculine pronouns in this translation follow the
    Hebrew and should not be encoded as a condition.

## C. Future or conditional commencement

31. **Nothing in the Law is awaiting commencement.** Section 24(a) brought the whole Law into force on
    16 August 2012, and section 9A came into force with its amending Act, published 11 February 2025 (that
    Act states no commencement date, so it takes effect on publication — confirm against the
    Interpretation Law, 5741-1981, rather than on this note).
32. **Section 25 is spent** (ended 1 January 2015) and the section 1 / 3(a)(2A) / 6(a1) coronavirus
    provisions have **lapsed**. An encoding that models time should keep all of them as historic states
    rather than deleting them, because section 19 gives a four-year limitation period and the Iron Swords
    provision of 2025 reaches back to flights in 2023.
33. **The Iron Swords provisions are retrospective in effect but forward-limited in one respect.**
    Section 2(a)–(b) of the amending Act removes compensation for flights already flown in 2023–2024;
    section 2(c) preserves judgments given before commencement, even non-final ones, and appeals from them;
    section 3 forbids any section 9A order reaching flights dated before 11 February 2025. Encode the
    2(c) carve-out — it is the only thing standing between the retrospective removal and a decided case.
34. **Section 2(d) of the amending Act is conditional and open-ended:** it runs from commencement "so long
    as" the special home-front situation declared on 7 October 2023 remains in force, and it is displaced
    the moment the Minister makes a section 9A(a)(2) order. Its end date is therefore not a date at all —
    it is a state of the world, and it needs an external input.
35. **The section 13 indexation is a standing conditional rule,** not a one-off. Every amount in the Law
    changes on 1 January each year by operation of law, and the Yalkut notice is declaratory of the new
    figure, not the source of it. Model the Schedules and section 11(a) as functions of the effective date,
    keyed to published notices, with the 2012 base as the anchor. Hard-coded literals will be wrong within
    a year.
36. **Section 21 lets the Minister change any Schedule by order** (subject to section 22(b) approval by the
    Knesset Economic Affairs Committee), independently of indexation. The Schedules are not stable
    constants even in structure.

## D. The effect of section 9A (the ministerial power during a special situation)

37. **What it is.** Section 9A, inserted by ס״ח תשפ״ה, 304 in February 2025, is a **derogation power**. It
    does not itself change any entitlement. It authorises the Minister of Transport, by order, to reduce
    passengers' entitlements under the Law for flights falling in a defined window, once a "special
    situation" has been declared and has run for more than 14 days.
38. **Gates on its exercise, all cumulative.** (i) a declared special situation — one of three: a special
    home-front situation under s 9C of the Civil Defence Law, 5711-1951; a civil emergency event under
    s 90B of the Police Ordinance [New Version], 5731-1971; or a state of emergency under a dangerous-
    infectious-disease law; (ii) that declaration in force **more than 14 days**; (iii) consultation with
    the Minister of Economy and Industry; (iv) approval of the Knesset Economic Affairs Committee; (v) a
    substantive satisfaction test that differs per limb; (vi) the order made by order (בצו), and by
    s 22(b) subject to committee approval.
39. **What an order may do — three limbs, and only one of the two sub-limbs of the third.**
    - *(a)(1)* cap **accommodation services** at a stated number of nights, floor of **two**, and expressly
      **not** touching section 5 (refusal to carry). Satisfaction test: significant impairment of the
      *supply of flights* to and from Israel.
    - *(a)(2)* modify the **alternative-flight-ticket** entitlement on cancellation under section 6,
      including permitting a ticket from a different origin or to a different destination. No satisfaction
      test is stated for this limb — note the asymmetry. Any such order **must** include a duty to notify
      the passenger clearly of the choice between refund and alternative ticket.
    - *(a)(3)(a)* shorten the **advance-notice period** that defeats compensation from 14 days to as little
      as **three days**. Satisfaction test: significant impairment of the ability to plan or change a
      flight schedule.
    - *(a)(3)(b)* **abolish monetary compensation outright** for cancelled or brought-forward flights under
      ss 6(a)(3), 8(b)(1) and 18. Satisfaction test is higher: the same planning impairment measured over a
      **two-week** horizon, **plus** a finding that shortening the notice period under (a)(3)(a) would not
      be enough.
    - The word "one of these" (אחת מאלה) in (a)(3) means (a) and (b) are **mutually exclusive**. The
      Minister may do one or the other, not both. Limbs (1), (2) and (3) may be combined.
40. **Duration limits.** The determining period may begin from the first day of the special situation, may
    not exceed **60 days**, and may not exceed **45 days** where compensation is abolished under
    (a)(3)(b). It is extendable, but only on the same conditions and with the same approvals. It ends
    automatically if the declaration is cancelled or lapses, with a published notice of the end date; and
    the Minister must end it without delay, by order, once the circumstances cease — that ending order
    needs **no** consultation and **no** committee approval.
41. **Scope.** An order may cover all flights or only some (s 9A(b)). Section 5 accommodation is protected
    from limb (1); nothing else in the Law is expressly protected.
42. **Notice duty for tickets already issued.** Where an order catches a flight whose ticket was issued
    before the order was made, the issuing operator or organiser must notify the passenger of the changed
    benefits, and so must the travel agency services provider that sold the ticket — but only if it was
    itself told by the operator or organiser (s 9A(d)(2)). That conditional duty is easy to miss.
43. **No retrospective reach.** Section 3 of the amending Act bars an order as to any flight ticketed for a
    date before 11 February 2025.
44. **Encoding shape.** Model section 9A as a parameterised override layer over sections 5–8 and 18, keyed
    to (i) whether an order is in force, (ii) which limbs it engages, (iii) the determining period, and
    (iv) the flight date. It must be possible to evaluate the Law with **no** order in force — which is the
    position as far as this translation could establish, since no section 9A order was located.
45. **Not verified:** whether any order under section 9A has in fact been made since February 2025. No
    search of Kovetz HaTakanot or the Ministry's site was carried out for this translation, and the Civil
    Aviation Authority page was previously recorded as returning HTTP 403. Encoders should check before
    assuming the base entitlements are undisturbed.
