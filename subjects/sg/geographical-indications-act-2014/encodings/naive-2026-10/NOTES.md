## Why this Act, and why scoped

**5 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
geographical indication is and who may sue (ss 2, 3), what is actionable (s 4),
groundless threats (s 8), the exceptions (ss 11 to 16), the offences and their
composition (ss 24, 25, 29, 30, 59A(3), 73, 81), and registration: who may apply,
the Schedule, refusal, duration, cancellation and transfer (ss 38, 40, 41, 50, 52,
53). Remedies and delivery-up, declarations, the Registry's administration,
examination and opposition procedure, homonymous indications, most of the border
measures in Part 6, evidence, costs and rules are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Wines and spirits are protected without anyone being misled; other goods mostly are not

For an ordinary indication, s 4(2)(a) catches use on goods from elsewhere only "in
a manner which misleads the public". For a wine or spirit indication, s 4(2)(c)
and (d) catch any use on a wine or spirit from elsewhere "whether or not" the true
origin is stated, the indication is translated, or it carries "kind", "type",
"style" or "imitation". So "X-style" wine is actionable with no confusion at all.
Asserted.

### 2. Registration lifts other Schedule goods to the wine-and-spirit level, but only in the same category

s 4(6) gives the same protection to a REGISTERED indication identifying an
agricultural product or foodstuff in a Schedule category, used on goods "of the
same category" (s 3(2) confines s 4(6) to registered indications). A registered
cheese indication used on another cheese from elsewhere is actionable without
misleading; on meat, or unregistered, it is not. Naming a genuine ingredient is
excepted (s 4(5), (9)). Asserted.

### 3. Only Schedule categories can be registered, and the list is short

s 40(1) and s 41(1)(b): registration only for goods in the Schedule, which lists
fifteen categories from Wines to Coffee (as amended by S 523/2026). Tea,
handicrafts and textiles are not on it; they can be protected only by the
unregistered route. Asserted (Tea; the other two are contrast values only).

### 4. Exceptions turn on registration in both directions

The disuse exception in s 11(e) is for an indication "not registered under this
Act", so registration removes it. The plant-variety and animal-breed exception in
s 15(b) is in Division 2, which by s 3(2) applies only to registered indications.
Prior users have cut-off dates: 15 April 1994 for wines and spirits (s 12), 1
January 2004 for other registered indications (s 16). They need 10 years of
continuous use in Singapore before the date, or good-faith use before it, and must
be a "qualified person" with a Singapore connection. Asserted.

### 5. A trade mark containing an indication is safe after five years, unless in bad faith

s 14: no action after 5 years beginning immediately after the earlier of the date
the use became generally known in Singapore and the date the mark was registered
(if published by then). Bad-faith use or registration removes the bar. Counting the
five years as + 50000 on a YYYYMMDD date is an inference about calendar arithmetic.
Asserted.

### 6. Falsely claiming registration carries a fine only; falsifying the register carries 5 years

s 25: a fine not exceeding $10,000, no imprisonment. Using "registered" is deemed a
claim to Singapore registration unless it is shown both that the reference is to a
foreign registration and that the indication is in fact so registered there. s 24
(false entries or copies): $50,000 or 5 years. Composition under s 81 is capped at
the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000, so $1,000 for a summons offence
(s 29) and $5,000 for falsification. Which offences are compoundable is left to
regulations, which were not retrieved. Asserted.

### 7. Groundless threats: the threatener must justify, and a registration notice is not a threat

s 8(3), (4): the person threatened gets relief "unless the defendant shows" the act
falls in s 4, and even then if the claimant shows an exception applies. Threats
about applying the indication or importing are outside s 8(1), and "mere
notification" of a registration is not a threat (s 8(5)). Asserted.

### 8. Cancellation can reach back

s 52(6): cancellation for breach of s 41 or fraud means the indication is "deemed
never to have been registered". For loss of protection at origin, lack of
commercial activity in Singapore, or becoming a common name, rights end from the
application date or an earlier date when the ground existed (s 52(7)). Registration
lasts 10 years, renewable for 10 years each time (s 50), and can be transferred only
to someone entitled to apply under s 38 who consents (s 53). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The Geographical Indications Rules (prescribed periods, fees, compoundable
  offences, renewal windows) were not retrieved.
- What S 523/2026 changed in the Schedule is not visible in the deposit.
- s 4(3) and (7) (literally true but falsely suggesting another origin), Part 6
  border seizures, and the qualification and limitation procedures (ss 46, 48A) are
  not encoded.
- "Agricultural product or foodstuff" in s 4(6) is a flag here, not decided per
  category (for example, non-edible oils or natural gum).
