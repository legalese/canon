## Why this Act, and why scoped

**9 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the
licensing gates a person or business meets (who is a private investigator, a
security officer, a security service provider), the offences protecting security
officers, false-alarm charges, and the caps and deadlines in licensing procedure:
ss 3, 5, 6, 13, 14, 16, 17A to 17E, 18 to 20, 23, 24, 26, 28, 33 and 35. Client
identification and record-keeping (ss 10, 12), security assignment approvals
(s 11), employee notification duties, the fit-and-proper factors, enforcement
powers, service of documents and the Protection from Harassment Act tie-ins
(s 17F) are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Selling an intruder alarm is not a security service; installing or advising on one is

s 18(1)(b)(i) carves out of "designing, selling, importing or exporting" security
equipment "basic household or automotive security items at approved classes of
retail outlets **or intruder alarm systems**". But intruder alarm systems are
item 7 of the Schedule, so installing or servicing one is a security service under
(a), and the product-advice carve-out in (c) mentions only basic household and
automotive items, so advising which alarm to buy is one too. s 18(2) separately
excludes a seller of self-install systems. Asserted.

### 2. Employing an unlicensed guard costs five times what being one does

The unlicensed security officer or private investigator faces $10,000 or 2 years
(ss 6(2), 14(2)); the employer of one faces $50,000 or 2 years (ss 8(2), 16(2)),
as do unlicensed agencies (ss 7(2), 15(2)). An unlicensed security service
provider faces only $10,000 (s 19(2)). Asserted.

### 3. Abuse of a guard is protected only if the abuser knew, and not if the guard is also a public service worker

s 17C (re-worded by Act 23 of 2025 to add "sexual or indecent" and
communications) needs intent and a resulting harassment, alarm, distress or
humiliation. s 17E adds that no offence is committed unless the accused knew or
ought to have known the target was a security officer on duty. s 17C(4) takes the
target out of s 17C where he or she is also acting as a public service worker
under the Protection from Harassment Act 2014. The accused may prove on a balance
of probabilities that the conduct was reasonable. A prior harassment conviction
(including under the PHA or the repealed MOA ss 13A to 13D) doubles the fine to
$10,000 and the term to 2 years (s 17D). Asserted.

### 4. Offences against guards are non-arrestable unless a police officer sees them

s 28(1A) treats ss 17A, 17B and 17C as non-arrestable "unless a person offends in
the view of a police officer", while every other offence under the Act is
arrestable by the licensing officer without warrant (s 28(1)). Asserted.

### 5. Who is not a private investigator

s 5(2) excludes an employer's in-house vetting of staff and applicants, business
research and market research by employees, and work done as part of law
practice, public accountancy, credit rating or insurance (and their employees).
A firm is never itself a "private investigator": s 5(1) says "any individual". An
unpaid search is not one either. Asserted.

### 6. The false-alarm charge falls on the alarm company, and only if it failed to verify

s 20(3): the police attendance charge is payable by a prescribed licensed security
service provider that installed or maintained the system, and only where the
licensing officer is satisfied it failed to take all reasonably practicable
measures to verify the alarm before calling. A false alarm is an activation "by
reasons other than fire or intruders"; vehicle and personal alarms are not alarm
systems (s 20(6)). Asserted.

### 7. Deadlines and caps

A licence lasts at most 5 years (s 23(c)); a suspension at most 6 months
(s 24(2)(a), (7)(b)). Appeals to the Minister run 14 days (s 26), and the
Minister's decision is final. Revoking or suspending a private investigator's or
security officer's licence takes effect only 14 days after notice (s 24(9)) and,
if appealed, not until the Minister confirms or the appeal ends (s 26(5)). Only
those two licences may be suspended immediately, without a hearing, on a charge
or conviction for a prescribed offence (s 24(5)). Composition is capped at the
lower of half the maximum fine and $2,000 (s 33(1)), so for nearly every offence
in the Act the cap is $2,000. Asserted.

### 8. No licence at the time, no fee

s 35: an unlicensed provider cannot sue for its fee unless it held a valid
licence "at the time of providing the service". A later licence does not cure it.
Asserted.

## Inferences, labelled

- s 20(4)(b) as deposited reads "is reckless about whether it would cause a
  person to fear ..." without repeating "gives a false alarm". The encoding
  requires a false alarm in both limbs. That is an inference about the drafting.
- s 24(9) names only private investigator's and security officer's licences. The
  encoding gives agency and provider licences no 14-day deferral, an inference
  from silence.
- s 6 is read with s 5(2): an excluded person carries out no "function of a
  private investigator".
