# Source license

The text of **Vermont Statutes Annotated, Title 8 (Banking and Insurance), Chapter 129 - Insurance Trade Practices** is published on the [Vermont Statutes Online - Vermont General Assembly (Office of Legislative Counsel)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/).

- **Terms**: The Vermont General Assembly's site carries a bare footer notice 'Copyright 2026 State of Vermont. All rights reserved.' and, on every statutes page, the caveat that the online text is 'an unofficial copy of the Vermont Statutes Annotated'. The official compilation is the Vermont Statutes Annotated, published under contract by LexisNexis (the site links to 'Vermont Statutes at Lexis/Nexis' at lexisnexis.com/hottopics/vtstatutesconstctrules/), so a printed or Lexis-hosted VSA volume carries the publisher's own copyright in annotations, notes and arrangement. The Legislature's own terms page ('Disclaimers--Privacy, Security, and Legal Notices', Office of Legislative Council) sets out only a liability disclaimer, an accuracy disclaimer and a prohibition on tampering with the site; it imposes no restriction on copying, redistributing or adapting the statutory text, and there is no licence, fee or permission requirement anywhere on it. Under the US government edicts doctrine (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)) the text of the Vermont Statutes as enacted is not copyrightable at all, so the footer's 'All rights reserved' cannot reach the enacted text - reuse AND adaptation of the statutory text are permitted. Annotations and editorial apparatus from the VSA are a different matter and must not be taken from Lexis.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
