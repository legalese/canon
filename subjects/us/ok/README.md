# oklahoma/

Oklahoma (US state) legislation, one subject-id per body of law, following the subject-sidecar shape described in [`subjects/README.md`](../README.md).

The subjects here were laid out on 2026-09-23 for the legislation governing twelve everyday topics. Each was checked against the Oklahoma Legislature - Oklahoma Statutes (Oklahoma State Legislature, oklegislature.gov); the check is recorded in each subject's `registers/source-bundle/*.meta.json`. No statute text has been deposited and nothing is encoded yet.

**Licence category: unclear.** **Metadata only until resolved.** No human has recorded a licence decision for this jurisdiction in `tools/topics/round3/licence-decisions.json`. The register's own terms, quoted above, are the evidence to decide on.

## Register access

PARTIALLY accessible - read carefully. The Oklahoma Legislature's own site worked over plain HTTPS GET with the honest user agent 'canon-index-builder/1.0 (+https://github.com/legalese/canon)'; no challenge, no rate limiting, about 25 requests spaced 2 s apart. Two significant limitations. (1) The judicial branch's OSCN statute browser (www.oscn.net and oscn.net, 65.71.189.80), which is the usual section-by-section official Oklahoma statute interface, refused all connections on port 443 from this environment (ECONNREFUSED from the agent fetch tool, timeout from curl, 3 attempts); no workaround was attempted. (2) On oklegislature.gov the Statutes are served ONLY as whole-title documents - https://www.oklegislature.gov/OK_Statutes/CompleteTitles/osNN.pdf (plus .doc and .rtf) - which are statute text and were therefore deliberately never downloaded. There is no per-title or per-chapter HTML table of contents: /OSSTATUESTITLE.HTML links straight to the PDFs, /osStatuesTitle.aspx is an ASP.NET postback form, and the CompleteTitles/ directory listing (which was read) contains only osNN and ocNN files with no TOC files. Verification therefore rests on two register-native sources: the official titles index at /OSSTATUESTITLE.HTML for title numbers, title names and the currency date, and the Legislature's own full-text statute search at https://search.oklegislature.gov/query-statutes.php (a GET form taking Title and SearchText), used to confirm that each Act's short title appears in the current statutes of the expected title and to obtain the section numbers that carry it. That search returns citations and a result count but no section catchlines and no per-section status flag, so section-level headings could not be read and are not asserted. A DATE DISCREPANCY WITHIN THE REGISTER: /OSSTATUESTITLE.HTML states 'The Oklahoma Constitution and Oklahoma Statutes were last updated on November 18th, 2025.' while /tsrs_os_oc.aspx on the same site states the same thing 'on October 4th, 2024'; a human should establish which feeds the search index.

## Topics with no legislation here

- **Termination/severance**: No state statute, verified negatively on the register: a search of Title 40 (Labor) for 'severance pay' returned 0 results. Oklahoma is an at-will state, wrongful discharge is a common-law tort (the Burk tort), and collective redundancy notice is federal (WARN Act, 29 U.S.C. ch. 23).
- **Passenger compensation**: No state statute creating a passenger compensation entitlement. Air passenger compensation is federal (14 C.F.R. part 250 for oversales, 49 U.S.C. ch. 417) and rail is federal (49 U.S.C.). Okla. Stat. tit. 13 is titled 'Common Carriers' on the register's titles index and deals with carrier duties and liability generally, not delay or denied-boarding compensation; its contents could not be read at section level because the register serves Title 13 only as a complete-title document and OSCN was unreachable.
- **Customs/duties**: Federal matter. Customs duties and tariffs are exclusively federal (Tariff Act of 1930, 19 U.S.C.); U.S. Const. art. I, sec. 10, cl. 2 bars states from laying imposts or duties on imports or exports.
- **Work permits/immigration**: Federal matter. Immigration status and work authorisation are governed by the Immigration and Nationality Act, 8 U.S.C., and I-9 verification by 8 U.S.C. sec. 1324a. Oklahoma has employer verification provisions in Title 25 (the Oklahoma Taxpayer and Citizen Protection Act of 2007), but these are state add-ons to the federal E-Verify system rather than a work-permit regime, and they were not verified at section level because the register serves Title 25 only as a complete-title document.
