# Land Surveyors Act 1991 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation (version in force from
1 October 2025), with amendments to Act 25 of 2024 (wef 1 October 2025) shown.

**Checks:** one case file, 118 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**10 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
surveyor, a surveying firm or a client meets: who may survey, certify, hold out
and sue for fees (ss 3, 10), registration (s 12), the practising-certificate year
(s 15), licensing a limited corporation (s 17(1), (9)), reports of changes (s 19),
the limits on discipline (ss 25, 26(6), 27(2), 29), the appeal routes and the
offences (ss 10(5), 30(2), 34). The Board's constitution, licences for unlimited
corporations, partnerships and LLPs, insurance, costs, fees and rules are not
encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Registered foreign surveyors are defined, but s 12A is not in the deposit

Act 25 of 2024 inserted a definition of "registered foreign surveyor" — "a person
registered as a foreign land surveyor under section 12A" — and s 34 now penalises
wilfully procuring registration as one. But there is no s 12A in the deposited
body, and the arrangement of sections lists none. (Inference, not from the text: either the deposit is missing
it or the section is not yet in force.) Not
asserted.

### 2. Suing for fees while unqualified is not an offence; it just fails

s 10(5) makes contravening s 10(1), (2) or (4) an offence (fine up to $10,000),
but not s 10(3). Under s 10(3) the person who did the work simply "is not entitled
to recover in any court" the fee. Asserted.

### 3. Who may certify a survey and who may advertise are different lists

Only an authorised surveyor (a land surveyor employed by the Singapore Land
Authority) or a registered surveyor with a practising certificate may certify a
survey (s 10(1)). A licensed firm is not named. But the list of those who may
use "RS" or hold themselves out as "authorised to supply survey services" (s 10(2))
names licensed firms and partnerships wholly of registered surveyors, and leaves
out the authorised surveyor. Read literally. Asserted.

### 4. Fee recovery depends on the capacity, and a licensed partnership is not named

s 10(3)(c) lets "a licensed corporation or a licensed limited liability
partnership" recover fees, but not a licensed partnership as such. Its partners
recover under (a) (registered surveyors) or (b) (allied professionals, "only by
reason of being a partner"). A registered surveyor working as an employee is in
none of the paragraphs. An authorised surveyor is not listed, though s 3(1) takes
the Government's own acts out of the Act. Asserted, on the literal reading.

### 5. A suspended surveyor is treated as uncertified

s 25(5): while a suspension is in force the surveyor is "not ... to be regarded as
having in force a practising certificate", so cannot certify, hold out, survey
unsupervised or sue for fees. Anyone, registered or not, may do survey work
"under the direction or supervision" of a qualified surveyor (s 10(4)(c)).
Asserted.

### 6. Penalties: $20,000 for a surveyor, $50,000 for a firm; and no jail

The Board may fine a surveyor up to $20,000 (s 25(2)(a)) and a firm up to $50,000
(s 27(2)(a)), both annotated as amended by Act 25 of 2024 (the earlier amounts are not in the deposit), with the money now going to the
Consolidated Fund (ss 25(4A), 27(2A)). Suspension is capped at 12 months and needs a
hearing with at least 3 Board members present (s 25(1), (3)). The criminal
offences are fines only: $10,000 (ss 10(5), 34) and $5,000 (s 30(2)). Asserted.

### 7. Orders wait 30 days, and longer if appealed

An order of cancellation, suspension or revocation "does not take effect until the
expiry of 30 days" after the Board informs the person, and, if appealed in that
time, only when confirmed or the appeal is dismissed (ss 25(6), 27(5)). Appeals go
to the General Division of the High Court, whose decision is final, except refusal
of a licence (s 17(8)) and licence conditions (s 18(4)), which go to the Minister.
No time limit is stated for the s 18(4) appeal; it is "in the prescribed manner".
Asserted.

### 8. A surveyor cannot resign their way out of discipline

s 26(6): no order removing a name at the surveyor's own request is to be made if
disciplinary action is pending or an Investigation Committee is looking into the
surveyor's conduct. After cancellation, re-registration may be considered only
"after the expiry of at least one year" (s 29). Asserted.

### 9. The renewal deadline is 1 December; December is late but still next year

A renewing surveyor must apply "not later than 1 December" (s 15(1)). Applying
later in December still gets a certificate for the next year, on an additional fee
(s 15(5)(a)). Applying on or after 1 January gets the remainder of the year
(s 15(5)(b)). A first certificate after registration may be sought at any time
(s 15(2)). Asserted.

### 10. Drafting slips in the deposited text

s 27(1) has two paragraphs labelled "(c)" (the second is the tail of the first),
and s 34 has two labelled "(b)" (the second is the "by making or producing ... any
false or fraudulent representation" limb). These are read as one paragraph each.
Whether they come from the PDF-to-text conversion or the source is not checked.
