## Why this Act, and why scoped

**8 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a holder,
applicant or parent meets: validity and extension (ss 8, 9, 14, 15), the refusal
grounds that turn on a past conviction, repeated losses, a concurrent passport or a
child (ss 19(2)(c)(ii), 20(2)(c)(ii), 25, 26, 27), the bar on acting on a withdrawn
request (s 24(4)), the appeal (s 28), the duties to report a loss and to surrender on
losing citizenship (ss 45, 46), the maximum penalties for the main offences, and
composition (s 52). The general discretion to refuse or cancel, the cancellation grounds,
emergency documents, enforcement powers, the foreign travel document offences and
Part 6 (beyond the s 56 penalty) are not encoded. No regulations were retrieved.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The Controller may refuse any passport, and the listed grounds are only examples

s 19(1): the Controller "may, by written notice, refuse a Singapore passport to any
citizen of Singapore". The grounds in s 19(2) apply "Without affecting subsection (1)".
The same pattern governs travel documents (s 20) and cancellation (s 21). So every
"ground to refuse" asserted here is sufficient, not necessary. The general discretion
itself is not encoded.

### 2. A conviction counts for 10 years against a passport, 3 against a travel document

s 19(2)(c)(ii) looks back "10 years before the application" for a passport; s 20(2)(c)(ii)
looks back 3 years for a travel document. The conviction must be under this Act, the
repealed Act, or a prescribed arrestable offence. A conviction 5 years old is a ground to
refuse a passport but not a travel document. Treating exactly 10 (or 3) years as outside
the window is an inference: the text does not settle the boundary. Asserted.

### 3. Running a passport-selling business carries a presumptive minimum of 2 years

s 42(1): selling a passport is $10,000 or 10 years. s 42(2): engaging "in the business or
trade" of selling brings "imprisonment for a presumptive minimum term of not less than
2 years but not more than 15 years" and also a fine up to $20,000 [15/2019]. That is the
only minimum term in the offences read. "Sell" includes barter, exchange and offering to
sell (s 42(3)). Buying or offering to buy is separately $10,000 or 10 years (s 44).
Asserted.

### 4. A lost passport must be reported within 14 days; on losing citizenship, surrender within 24 hours

s 45: a holder who knows the document is lost, stolen or destroyed must report it to an
authorised officer within 14 days, or commits an offence ($3,000 or 2 years). s 46: a
holder who knows he has ceased to be a citizen must surrender the passport within 24
hours. For a child holder (below 16, s 2(1)) the offence is that of the person with
parental responsibility. A reasonable excuse is a defence to both. Asserted.

### 5. No second passport while one is valid, and no passport for a child without consent

s 26(1): the Controller "must not" issue a passport while the person holds a still-valid
passport or travel document, unless the Minister's directions allow it (s 26(2)). s 27: no
passport or travel document for a child unless the person with parental responsibility
(or, for a child in care, the Director-General of Social Welfare, a protector or the
care-giver) consents, or a court order permits travel. Asserted.

### 6. A withdrawn request binds the Controller

s 24(4): if a competent authority withdraws its request, or the Controller no longer
considers it current, the Controller "must not" cancel or refuse on it. A request may be
made even about a non-citizen (s 24(2)(c)). Asserted.

### 7. No appeal against a decision made because of a Singapore arrest warrant

s 28(1): refusals to issue or extend, and cancellations, are appealable to the Minister
within 14 days of notification, or a longer period the Minister allows in exceptional
circumstances "whether before or after the end of the 14 days". The Minister's decision is
final. A decision made because of a warrant of arrest issued in Singapore is excluded.
The encoding treats an endorsement (s 10) as not appealable, because s 28(1) does not
list it. Asserted.

### 8. Biometric passports and temporary travel documents must not be extended

s 9 and s 15. An ordinary passport lasts at most 6 years (s 8(1), for those issued on or
after 1 April 2005), a temporary travel document one year (s 14(1)). The Minister may
shorten either in a particular case, or lengthen the period by Gazette notification
(s 8(4) was annotated S 759/2022). That notification was not retrieved, so the "6 years"
is the Act's figure only. Asserted.

### 9. Composition is capped at $500

s 52: the Controller may compound a prescribed compoundable offence for "a sum not
exceeding $500". Which offences are prescribed is left to regulations. Asserted.
