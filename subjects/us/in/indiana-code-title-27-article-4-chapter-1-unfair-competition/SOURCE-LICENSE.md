# Source license

The text of **Indiana Code Title 27, Article 4, Chapter 1 (Unfair Competition; Unfair or Deceptive Acts and Practices)** is published on the [Indiana General Assembly - Indiana Code (published by the Legislative Services Agency)](https://iga.in.gov/laws/current/ic).

- **Terms**: No copyright notice, licence or terms-of-use statement appears anywhere on the register: the footer of iga.in.gov ends with the Statehouse address and no copyright line, and a scan of the site's FAQ page for the words copyright, reproduce, public domain and terms of use returned nothing. The statute text is therefore unencumbered on its own terms, and under the government edicts doctrine (Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)) the text of the Indiana Code is not copyrightable at all: reuse and adaptation are permissible. Two cautions. First, the statewide State of Indiana terms page at www.in.gov could not be read from this environment, so a state-portal-level assertion cannot be ruled out. Second, the official Indiana Code is compiled by the Legislative Services Agency, while the annotated editions (Burns Indiana Statutes Annotated from LexisNexis, and West's Annotated Indiana Code) are commercial products whose annotations and editorial matter are protected - only the bare Code as published on iga.in.gov should be taken.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
