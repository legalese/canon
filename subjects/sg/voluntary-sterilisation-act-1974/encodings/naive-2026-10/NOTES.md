## Why this Act, and why scoped

Chosen for its **everyday-life relevance** to ordinary people in Singapore (Tier 1),
not by citation count: anyone considering a vasectomy or tubal ligation meets it, as
do the parents of a young unmarried adult and the family of a person who lacks
mental capacity. The Act is short, so the row takes nearly all of it: the
definition (s 2), consent and court orders (s 3(2)), the explanation (s 3(3)), place
and practitioner (s 4), secrecy (s 5), coercion (s 7), immunity (s 8), conscientious
objection (s 10) and the three offences' maximum penalties. Not encoded: inspection
(s 6), the Penal Code declaration (s 9, quoted only), regulations (s 13), the Mental
Capacity Act 2008 tests imported by s 3(4) (taken as given facts), and anything
"prescribed" (no regulations were retrieved).

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. An unmarried person under 21 needs a parent; a married one does not

s 3(2)(b): a married person below 21 may be sterilised on their own consent. s
3(2)(c): an unmarried person below 21 needs their own consent **and** that of at
least one parent or guardian. At 21 marital status stops mattering (s 3(2)(a): "21
years of age or older"). A parent's consent without the person's own is not enough.
Asserted.

### 2. Lacking capacity, only a court can authorise it, and who may apply turns on marriage

s 3(2)(d), (e): for a person who lacks capacity, nobody's consent suffices; the
court (General Division of the High Court or a Family Court, s 2) must declare the
treatment "necessary in the best interests" of the person. For a married person the
application must be by the spouse; for an unmarried person, by a parent or guardian
— at any age. Read literally, an order made on a parent's application for a
**married** adult does not satisfy (d). Asserted.

### 3. The doctor's immunity ignores where the operation was done and whether it was explained

s 8 protects a practitioner from civil and criminal liability "so long as" the s 3(2)
conditions are met and the treatment is not negligent. The s 3(3) explanation and
certification and the s 4 place and authorisation rules are not conditions of it.
Likewise the s 3(5) offence is triggered only by a failure of the s 3(2) conditions.
Asserted (immunity on consent and negligence only; the s 3(3) and s 4 rules encoded
separately).

### 4. Section 4 has no penalty of its own

s 4 requires a health institution (an acute hospital or ambulatory surgical centre
licensed under the Healthcare Services Act 2020, or a specialist medical clinic) and
a practitioner authorised by its management, with prescribed qualifications in a
specialist clinic. The Act sets no penalty for breaching it and has no general
penalty section. Whether another law (for example the Healthcare Services Act 2020)
reaches a breach was not checked: that is left open, not inferred. Asserted (the
place rules themselves).

### 5. Secrecy, coercion and objection

s 5: those who keep the records or take part must not disclose anything about the
treatment without the patient's express consent, except to prescribed persons for
prescribed purposes — $10,000 or 12 months. s 7: compelling or inducing someone by
coercion or intimidation to be sterilised against their will — $10,000 or 5 years,
the same as the s 3(5) offence. s 10: no one has a duty, by contract or law, to
participate if they have a conscientious objection, and no emergency exception is
written in. Asserted.

### 6. The definition excludes castration unless medically necessary

s 2: "treatment for sexual sterilisation" is surgical sterilisation "that does not
involve removal of the reproduction glands or organs unless the removal is necessary
for medical or therapeutic reasons". Asserted.
