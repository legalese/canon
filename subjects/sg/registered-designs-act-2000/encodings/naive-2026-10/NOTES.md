**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation. The deposit labels itself
"Informal Consolidation – version in force from 9/3/2025"; the latest amendment
annotated is Act 5 of 2025 (wef 9 March 2025), in Part 6A, which this row does not
encode.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**8 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the rules a
designer, a business selling products, or an adviser to either actually meets: what a
design is, who owns it, novelty and the grace period, Convention priority, duration,
infringement and its exceptions, the innocent infringer, late ownership challenges,
groundless threats and the offences: ss 2(1), 4, 5–7, 8A, 12(1)(c), 21, 23(4), 30, 31,
36(3), 39, 44, 65, 66 and 68. Application procedure, the pre-2017 disclosure rules
(s 8), artistic works (ss 9, 22), revocation procedure, co-ownership, delivery up,
Government use, the Registrar, the Hague Agreement and border measures are not
encoded. Every "prescribed" period and fee is left to rules not retrieved.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Marking a product "registered" is worth nothing without the number

s 39(1) bars damages and an account of profits against a defendant who proves it was
not aware, and had no reasonable grounds for believing, that the design was
registered. s 39(2): the word "registered" on the product does not give reasonable
grounds "unless the registration number of the design accompanied" it. An injunction
is not barred. Asserted.

### 2. The grace period depends on who disclosed, and confidential disclosure has no time limit

s 8A(1) disregards a disclosure by the designer, by another using the designer's
information, or through an abuse of the designer, only within the 12 months before
filing. A disclosure "under conditions of confidentiality" carries no time limit at
all. A disclosure by an independent stranger is not on the list, so it destroys
novelty (s 5(2)(b)). The section applies only to disclosures on or after 30 October
2017 (s 8A(2)); earlier ones fall under s 8, not encoded. Reading "within 12 months"
as at most 12 whole months is an inference. Asserted.

### 3. Fifteen years at most, in three five-year blocks

s 21(1): 5 years from registration; (2) "a second and third period of 5 years" on
application and fee. Unrenewed, registration "ceases to be in force" (s 21(4)), with a
late restoration window whose length is prescribed (s 21(5), not encoded). Asserted.

### 4. Functional, must-match and must-fit features are not design, and copying them does not infringe

The definition of "design" in s 2(1) excludes methods of construction, features
"dictated solely by the function", features dependent on the appearance of a larger
product, connecting features, and bare colour. s 30(6) says reproducing an excluded
feature under para (b) or (c) does not infringe. Asserted.

### 5. Infringement is commercial; private, research and parallel-import uses are free

s 30(1)–(2): making or importing for sale, hire or trade, and selling or hiring, in
Singapore without consent. s 30(5): private non-commercial acts and evaluation,
analysis, research or teaching do not infringe. s 30(7): goods placed on the market
anywhere by or with the owner's consent may be imported and sold. A person who used
the design in good faith in Singapore before registration may continue (s 31(1)) but
may not license others (s 31(3)). The encoding treats the s 31 right as taking the act
outside infringement; that is an inference from "has the right to continue". Asserted.

### 6. A threat about selling is actionable; a threat about making or importing is not

s 44(2): the threatened person gets relief unless the threatener proves infringement
and the claimant fails to show invalidity. s 44(3): no action for threats about "the
making or importing of anything"; (4) a bare notice of registration is not a threat;
(5) a solicitor acting for a client is not liable. Asserted.

### 7. The employer owns, unless agreed otherwise; a computer design belongs to whoever arranged it

s 4(2), (3): an employee's design made in the course of employment belongs to the
employer "subject to any agreement to the contrary". s 4(5): with no human designer,
the person who made the arrangements is deemed the designer. Asserted.

### 8. Ownership challenges close after two years, unless the owner knew

s 23(4): after 2 years from the certificate, no transfer order on the ground that the
registered owner was not entitled, unless it is shown the owner knew. Reading "after
the end of 2 years" as strictly more than 2 is an inference. No infringement
proceedings lie for acts before the certificate issues (s 36(3)). Asserted.

### 9. Offences: falsifying the Register is the serious one; composition is capped at $5,000

s 65: $50,000 or 5 years. s 66(1), (3): falsely claiming registration, or marking goods
after the right expired, $10,000 or 12 months. s 68(1): composition for "the lower of"
half the maximum fine and $5,000; which offences are compoundable is prescribed.
Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The prescribed periods (renewal window, late restoration), fees and the list of
  compoundable offences were not retrieved.
- s 8 (pre-30 October 2017 disclosures and exhibitions) and the s 8B transitional
  window are not encoded, so the novelty rule is only right for later disclosures.
- Novelty itself ("immaterial details", "variants commonly used in the trade") and
  "not substantially different" are taken as given facts, not decided.
- No case law was searched.
