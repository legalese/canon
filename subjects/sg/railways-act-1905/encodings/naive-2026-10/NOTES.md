## Why this Act, and why scoped

**11 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the parts a
passenger, a consignor or a member of the public meets: what the Act applies to (s 1),
the railway's liability as a carrier and its limits (ss 36 to 45 and the Schedule),
and the passenger and public offences (ss 62 to 90). Construction and land, opening
and inspection, returns, property tax, legal proceedings, accidents, rates and general
rules, the manager's disciplinary fines, detention and sale of goods, occupation
crossings and motor or aircraft services are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Two offences now carry 20 and 7 years, with fines still of $1,000 and $200

Act 21 of 2025 (in force 17 August 2026) is annotated on ss 86 and 87. s 86
(obstructing the line, or throwing things at a train, with intent to endanger) now
reads $1,000 or 20 years or both; s 87 (a wilful act or omission endangering safety)
reads $200 or 7 years or both. Every other fine in ss 64 to 90 sits between $20 and
$500, and the longest other imprisonment is one year (s 83(1)). Asserted.

### 2. The Act does not apply to the MRT or the cross-border link

s 1 applies the Act to "all railways in Singapore, except" a rapid transit system
under the Rapid Transit Systems Act 1995 and a cross-border railway under the
Cross-Border Railways Act 2018. Asserted.

### 3. Liability caps are fixed sums from another age

A horse is capped at $350 a head; mules, donkeys and horned cattle $100; sheep, goats,
dogs and other animals $20 (s 37). A motor car or traction engine $200; a coach,
carriage, ghari or omnibus $100; a motor-bicycle $50; any other vehicle, including a
lorry, $10 (s 38). Luggage $100, $50 or $25 an article by class of ticket (s 39).
Parcels with Schedule articles (jewellery, watches, bank notes, works of art, silks,
opium and more) worth over $100 are capped at $100 (s 40). Each cap lifts only on a
declaration of higher value, and for vehicles on payment of 1% of the excess.
Asserted. (That a declaration lifts the cap to the declared value is an inference for
ss 37 to 39; s 40(2) says it in terms.)

### 4. Goods: the railway must be negligent, but the claimant need not show how

s 36 says the railway is "in no case" liable for goods unless the loss was caused by
the negligence or misconduct of its agents or employees, and the goods were booked and
paid for. s 43(1) (as amended by Act 25 of 2021) says the claimant need not "prove how
the loss ... was caused". Read together (an inference), the burden of disproving
negligence seems to fall in practice on the railway. The s 36 conditions are asserted;
s 43(1) and the interaction are not encoded.

### 5. Claims die at six months, and only in writing

s 44: no refund of an overcharge and no compensation for animals or goods unless the
claim was made in writing within 6 months from delivery for carriage. Asserted.

### 6. A full train favours long journeys and Government business

s 63: fares are accepted only on condition that there is room. If there is not, the
longest-distance ticket wins, then the earliest ticket; officers and troops on duty
and anyone on Government business go first regardless. Asserted.

### 7. Fare dodging costs a surcharge of cents

s 62(3): a passenger without a ticket pays the fare plus a special charge of 50, 25 or
10 cents by class. Fraud on fares is a separate offence with a fine of up to $100
(s 64). Asserted.
