## Why this Act, and why scoped

**24 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it, 16 of them for s 46, the
power to make the Family Justice Rules. This row takes the parts a family litigant
meets: privacy, the court's powers to stop repeat applications, and appeals. The
courts' constitution, the Youth Courts and the Rules are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Family appeals have no money threshold

s 23(2): "an appeal lies ... from **any** decision of a Family Court exercising
jurisdiction of a ... civil nature", subject only to the Second Schedule's
interlocutory orders and protection orders under the Protection from Harassment Act
(s 23(2A), (2B)). A $10,000 maintenance order can be appealed as of right. A $10,000
civil judgment from a District Court needs permission (Supreme Court of Judicature
Act s 21, threshold $60,000; see that row). Asserted.

### 2. A barred filing is dismissed without anyone being heard

s 11A: where a further application would likely be without merit given the party's
past conduct, or would harm the child's welfare, the court may bar filing without
permission. s 11A(8): anything filed in breach "is to be treated as dismissed or
expunged -- (a) without the Court having to make any further order; and (b) without
the need for any other party to be heard". An application for the permission itself
is never barred (s 11A(9)). Asserted.

### 3. Everything is private by default, and naming a witness can cost 3 years

s 10(1): all family proceedings "must be heard in private" unless the court opens
them. s 10(4), (5): breach of an order against publishing a witness's identity is
punishable by up to $5,000 or **3 years**. Asserted.

### 4. The court can decide substantive issues no one applied for

s 11B: the court may make, of its own motion, any order it could make on application,
"including an order of a substantive nature" -- provided everyone affected has been
heard and it is in the interests of justice. Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 26(3A):** where a Family Court and the Family Division both have jurisdiction,
  the application **must** start in the Family Court.
- **s 33:** a signed agreement that a decision is final, made before or after it,
  bars appeal save for fraud, illegality or a fundamental breach of natural justice.
- **s 34:** an appeal does not stay enforcement of a maintenance or other order
  unless a court orders it.
