## Why this Act, and why scoped

**13 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the liabilities
of the serviceman, his employer and his parents. Fitness examination detail, mobilised
and regular service, loss of civilian pay, the discretionary release, postponement and
exemption powers, and the regulations are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The definition has no sex limit

s 2: a "person subject to this Act" is any citizen or permanent resident aged 16½ to 40
(50 for officers, senior military experts and designated skilled occupations). The
definition contains no reference to sex. The operative sections use "he", and who is
actually called up depends on notices under s 3, which this row does not see.
Asserted (on age and status only).

### 2. Promotion lengthens full-time service

s 12(1): full-time service is up to 2 years, but up to **2½ years** for anyone promoted
above Lance-Corporal or selected for officer training. s 12(2): a later demotion does
not reduce the liability. Asserted.

### 3. A parent overseas commits an offence when the child overstays

s 32: a citizen or PR aged 13 to under 16½ (a "relevant child") needs an exit permit
to leave or stay abroad. If the child overstays, **each parent**, "within or outside
Singapore", commits an offence, as does the child. Asserted.

### 4. Employers bear the burden on dismissal

- **s 22(3):** where service "caused or contributed to" a dismissal, it is deemed the
  reason unless the employer proves otherwise.
- **s 22(4):** $2,000 or 6 months, plus up to 3 months' pay in compensation.
- **s 21:** an employee of 6 months' standing must be reinstated after service, unless
  the job was a fixed term.
- **s 23:** refusing leave for service carries up to 3 years.

Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 15:** for operationally ready national service, up to 4 hours counts as half a
  day; 4 to 24 hours as one day; 18 to 24 hours over two calendar days as two days; and
  longer periods count every calendar day touched. The cap is 40 days a year (s 14).
- **s 4(3):** after conviction for not registering, $50 a day until registered.
- **s 33:** the offences apply to "any person within or outside Singapore".
