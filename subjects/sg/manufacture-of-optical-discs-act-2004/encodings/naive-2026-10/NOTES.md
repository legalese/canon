## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is requirement **REQ-0085**: Tier 2 of the remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by
everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what the Act decides for a person or
business it applies to. No scenario has asked a sharper question yet.

The Act is short (31 sections), so this row covers nearly everything a disc
manufacturer, its officers, or anyone dealing with an enforcement officer meets:
what counts as an optical disc (s 2, The Schedule), the licence and premises
offences (ss 4, 5), the grounds for refusal, suspension and cancellation (ss 7(2),
10(1)), when a suspension, surrender or appeal takes effect or is in time (ss 10(3),
11(2), 14(1)), code marking and false codes (ss 15, 17), the penalty scales (ss 19(3),
20(3), 22, 23, 24), forfeiture (s 21), liability of officers and partners (s 25) and
composition (s 26).

Not encoded: appointments (s 3), application procedure (s 6), the form and duration
of licences (s 8), variation of conditions (s 9), the register (s 12), the content of
records (s 13), marking standards (s 16), arrest, inspection and computer-search
powers (ss 18 to 20) apart from the offences they create, and ss 27 to 31. No
regulations were retrieved, so nothing prescribed is known: which offences are
compoundable, what records must contain, the marking standards, and any further
suspension grounds under s 10(1)(d).

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Only five kinds of media are "optical discs", and Blu-ray is not one of them

s 2 defines "optical disc" as any medium or article in the Schedule, and the Schedule
lists only compact discs (including CD-ROM), DVDs (including DVD-ROM), video compact
discs, master discs and stampers. A Blu-ray disc is not listed, so on the text a
Blu-ray plant needs no licence and its discs need no code. Only a Ministerial order
in the Gazette can add to the Schedule (s 30). Asserted.

### 2. Owning or running the business is manufacturing

s 4(3): anyone who "owns, directs, manages or otherwise has control of" a business that
manufactures optical discs is deemed to engage in manufacture for a commercial
purpose. An owner who never touches a press commits the s 4 offence if the business
is unlicensed. Making discs for no sale or commercial purpose is outside s 4.
Asserted.

### 3. The code may be left off only at a verified rights holder's request

s 15(2) excuses marking "if, and only if" a person who owns or licenses the
reproduction right, "which the licensee has verified", asked for no marking. A
request the licensee has not verified does not help. Asserted.

### 4. The penalty scales differ more than their headings suggest

Offences under Parts 2 and 3 (s 22) carry up to $200,000 for a body corporate
($400,000 for a repeat) and $100,000 or 12 months for others ($200,000 or 2 years for a
repeat). Failing to provide exemplars (s 19(3)), information offences (s 23) and
obstruction (s 24) carry half of that. Refusing technical help with a computer
(s 20(3)) carries a flat $20,000 or 12 months, with no higher figure for a body
corporate or a repeat. That a body corporate faces no prison term is an inference:
the text gives it none. Asserted.

### 5. Forfeiture follows conviction unless the court finds a reason not to

s 21(1): on any conviction under the Act the court "must" order forfeiture of the discs,
articles or things involved, unless the offence is minor or there are exceptional
circumstances, and it is in addition to the penalty. Asserted.

### 6. A suspension waits 14 days, or for the Minister

s 10(3): with no appeal, a suspension or cancellation takes effect on the expiry of 14
days from service of the notice; with an appeal, only when the Minister confirms it.
An appeal must be made within 14 days of notification or any longer period the
Minister allows (s 14(1)), and the Minister's decision is final (s 14(3)). Reading
"the expiry of 14 days from" service as service day plus 14 is an inference. A
surrender takes effect on receipt or on a later date in the notice, never an earlier
one (s 11(2)). Asserted.

### 7. Smaller points

The false-code offence (s 17) applies to any person, with a defence that the accused
must prove: acting without intent to deceive or defraud. Officers, partners and
association office-holders are guilty with the organisation where it acted with their
consent or connivance or through their neglect (s 25). The Registrar may compound a
prescribed offence for up to $10,000 (s 26). Asserted.
