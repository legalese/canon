## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is **requirement REQ-0063**: Tier 2 of the remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by
everyday-life relevance. It asks what the Act decides for a person or business it
applies to. No scenario has asked a sharper question yet. This row takes what an
operator, a ride manager, a premises owner and a rider meet: what counts as an
amusement ride and who is the "person responsible" (s 2), the conditions for lawful
operation (s 13), the permit term (s 15), ride managers (s 16), stop directions
(s 18), lapse and revocation (s 22), closure and adverse incidents (ss 35, 54), the
defences in ss 19(3) and 59(2), the maximum penalties, and composition (s 62).
Installation and modification permit procedure, relocation, the fire and rescue
management system (Part 6), qualified persons (Part 7), enforcement powers, appeals,
exemptions and everything left to regulations are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A coin-operated kiddie ride is outside the Act only if it sits on a fixed base

s 2 excludes a "coin-operated ride", but defines one as a device that "(a) is fixed to
a non-moving base; and (b) upon the insertion of any coin ... operates as a ride for
children". A token-operated ride that moves on a mobile base is therefore not a
"coin-operated ride" and, if it is otherwise a power-driven device carrying the public
for amusement, it is an amusement ride. Playground equipment, including
"rider-propelled merry-go-rounds", is excluded outright, and "power-driven" excludes
anything driven "solely by human physical effort". Asserted.

### 2. Who answers for a ride depends on whether anyone holds the permit

The "person responsible" is the operating permit holder. Only when no one holds a
permit does the duty spread to the ride's owner or controller, the owner of the
premises and the Public Entertainments Act licensee for the venue. So a mall owner
answers for an unpermitted ride on its floor, but not for a permitted one.
Separately, s 59(1) presumes the premises owner to be the person operating an
unlawful ride "until the contrary is proved" (not encoded). The definition still
names "the person who is allowed by virtue of section 75" to operate without a
permit, yet the 2020 Revised Edition's Comparative Table lists 2012 Ed. ss 75 and 76
as "[Omitted as spent]": a cross-reference to a provision that is no longer printed.
Asserted (the s 75 limb is not encoded).

### 3. Six things must all hold every time a ride runs

s 13(1): a valid operating permit, operation within its conditions, good repair, a
fire and rescue certificate of compliance in force, liability insurance at the
prescribed minimum, and at least one ride manager. Missing any one is the Act's top
offence: $200,000 or 2 years, plus $1,000 a day while it continues and $2,000 a day
after conviction (s 13(2)). If the insurance lapses, the permit itself lapses
automatically (s 22(1)(b)). The insurance minimum is in regulations not retrieved.
Asserted.

### 4. Losing the only ride manager stops the ride

s 16(3): if the departing ride manager "is the only ride manager", the ride must not
run until a substitute is both appointed and notified to the Commissioner. Both the
holder and the departing manager must notify within 14 days, and the substitute
appointment within 7 days. Asserted.

### 5. The composition cap always binds for the Act's own offences

s 62 caps a composition at "the lower of" half the maximum fine and $5,000. The
smallest maximum fine in the Act is $20,000 (s 56 and others), so half of it is
$10,000 and the $5,000 cap always wins. Only an offence under the regulations with a
maximum below $10,000 could compound for less. Which offences are compoundable is left
to regulations. Asserted for two offences.

### 6. A stop direction lasts until written consent

s 18(2): after a stop direction the ride "must not be resumed without the written
consent of the Commissioner", and resuming without it is a separate offence ($50,000
or 6 months, s 18(5)). An adverse incident (which includes riders "stranded or
trapped" and anything likely to injure a bystander) must be reported by both the
person responsible and the ride manager "as soon as practicable" (s 54(1)), and the
ride must be closed or cordoned off (s 35(1)(b)). Asserted.
