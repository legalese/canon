# Revised Edition of the Laws Act 1983 — naive encoding

**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test
pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation. The cover says it
"incorporates all amendments up to and including 1 December 2021"; the body is
annotated with later amendments, by Act 32 of 2024 (in force 25 November 2024) and
Act 5 of 2025 (in force 9 March 2025).

**Checks:** one case file, 55 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**7 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. Its own cover says the
edition was prepared by the Law Revision Commission "under the authority of" this
Act; that the other 2020 Revised Edition deposits here were prepared the same way
is an inference, not checked. This
row takes the rules with decision content: who the Commissioners are (s 3), what
they may change themselves and what needs Parliament or the rule-maker (ss 4, 6,
15(2)), omitted Acts and old instruments (ss 4(1)(a) proviso, 5, 13, 16, 19), what
the revised edition must contain (s 8(1)), later editions and their covers (ss 9,
10, 11(2)), who makes an edition the sole proper law (ss 7, 11, 17), print against
electronic (s 17A) and rectification (s 23). Definitions, binding, title lists,
reprints, construction of references and expenses are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Where print and electronic versions straddle the appointed date, s 17A is silent

s 17A(1) makes an electronic revised edition "prima facie evidence" of the law.
On an inconsistency, an electronic version published on or after the appointed
date prevails over the print version published on or after that date (s 17A(2));
before that date, print prevails over electronic (s 17A(3)). Both subsections
compare versions on the **same side** of the date. If one version is from before
and the other from after, neither applies. The appointed date is the commencement
of s 3(m) of the Electronic Gazette and Legislation Act 2025, which is not in the
deposit. Asserted.

### 2. One kind of edition becomes law on the Commissioners' word, and need not go to Parliament

For the revised edition of Acts (s 7), later editions under s 10 (s 11(3)) and
revised subsidiary legislation under s 15 (s 17(2)), the **President** fixes the
date by order in the Gazette, and the edition must be presented to Parliament. For
an edition of subsidiary legislation under s 17(5), the **Commissioners** fix the
date "by notification in the Gazette" (s 17(8)), and the deposit shows no duty to
present it to Parliament. s 17(6) is marked deleted by Act 5 of 2025; what it said
is not in the deposit. Asserted.

### 3. The Commissioners can rewrite, but not change the meaning; anything more needs Parliament or the rule-maker

s 4(1) lists what the Commissioners may do "without changing the meaning of any
Act": omit repealed Acts, Supply Acts, preambles and enacting clauses, alter titles,
consolidate Acts in pari materia, renumber, change "the way of ... expressing" a
penalty, make language gender-neutral, correct typographical mistakes, update
cross-references, restyle Ordinances as Acts. Anything else goes to Parliament as an
Act (s 6(1)), or, for subsidiary legislation, to "the authority having power to
make the same" (s 15(2)(b)). Increasing a penalty or creating an offence is not
listed. A listed kind of change that alters the meaning is outside s 4 too.
Asserted.

### 4. Omission by the Commissioners is a repeal; omission by the President is not

Omissions under s 4(1)(a) are treated under s 16 of the Interpretation Act 1965 "as
if the enactments omitted had been repealed", and s 13's saving expressly excludes
"any Act omitted under section 4". Acts the President specifies for omission under
s 5 "remain in force until they have been expressly repealed or have expired or
become spent or had effect" (s 5(2)). Any other omitted Act is not without force "by
reason only of" the omission (s 13). Asserted.

### 5. Only the presidential appointees have a term limit or can be removed

s 3(2) limits a Commissioner appointed under s 3(1)(e) to "a period not exceeding 3
years", and s 3(3) lets the President revoke such an appointment at any time. The
Act states no term and no revocation power for the Attorney-General, a Deputy
Attorney-General, the Solicitor-General or the Chief Legislative Counsel; the
encoding reads that as "no limit stated", which is an inference. Asserted.

### 6. Corrections to subsidiary legislation do not go to Parliament

s 23(1) lets the Commissioners rectify clerical or publication errors, and other
errors consistently with their revision powers, in (a) Acts and (b) subsidiary
legislation. s 23(2) requires only orders "under section (1)(a)" to be presented to
Parliament. ("section (1)(a)" is as printed, read as subsection (1)(a).) Asserted.

### 7. An Act amended, but not extensively, has no s 10 route to a new edition

s 10(1)(a) allows a revised edition of an Act the Commissioners consider
"extensively amended"; s 10(2) allows one of an Act "which has not been amended". On
the words, an Act amended only modestly falls between them. This is a reading of
the text, not a stated rule. A new Act whose "sole or substantial effect" is to
amend other Acts is excluded by s 10(1)(b). Asserted.

### 8. A few housekeeping rules

The revised edition must include every Act wholly or substantially in force on 31
December 2020, may include later Acts, and must omit Acts the President specifies
(ss 5(1), 8(1)). A s 10 edition carries its year of publication on the front page
instead of "2020 Revised Edition" (s 11(2)). Instruments under repealed or omitted
Acts continue so far as not inconsistent with an Act in force (s 19(2)).
s 17A(1) still refers to "section 11A(1) as in force before the appointed date",
though s 11A is shown repealed by Act 5 of 2025. Asserted, except the last.
