## Why this Act, and why scoped

**16 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes the duties the
Act lays on individuals: notification, isolation, not spreading a dangerous disease,
the HIV provisions, confidentiality, childhood vaccination and the general penalty.
Surveillance and premises powers, public health threat and emergency orders, port
health, investigation powers and all prescribed lists and periods are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The anti-spreading offence covers five diseases only

s 21A applies only to diseases "set out in the Second Schedule": Ebola, MERS-CoV,
plague, SARS and yellow fever (item 1 was deleted by S 172/2024). Going to work knowing
you have measles, influenza or tuberculosis is not a s 21A offence. Isolation orders
under s 15 are the tool for those diseases. Asserted.

### 2. Leaving isolation has no reasonable-excuse defence

s 15(4): failing to go to the isolation place, or breaching a condition, is an offence
"without reasonable excuse". **Leaving** is an offence "without the permission of the
Director-General", and on that limb a reasonable excuse is no defence. Asserted.

### 3. Knowing your HIV status gives you fewer ways to comply than only suspecting it

s 23(1): a person who **knows** they have HIV complies only by disclosure followed by
voluntary acceptance, or by a sustained undetectable viral load (s 23(1A), added
2024). s 23(2): a person who only has **reason to believe** may also comply by testing
negative or by taking reasonable precautions. Precautions alone protect the person who
suspects but not the person who knows. Knowledge is deemed once a positive result has
been communicated (s 23(5)). Prosecution under (2) needs the Public Prosecutor's
consent. Asserted.

### 4. Confidentiality

s 25(1): identifying information about HIV or STD status, learned under the Act, may be
disclosed with consent, or to:
- treating staff
- a court that orders it
- the police under the CPC
- a blood or organ bank that received from the person
- the victim of a sexual assault by the person
- the immigration authority

It may not be disclosed to an employer or the press. Asserted.

### 5. Smaller things worth recording

- **s 46 and the Fourth Schedule:** only diphtheria and measles vaccination is compulsory
  for children.
- **s 65:** where no penalty is stated, up to $10,000 or 6 months; $20,000 or 12 months
  on a repeat offence.
- **s 6:** doctors must notify on "reason to believe or suspect", and diagnostic
  laboratories must notify too.
