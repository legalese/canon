**Method: naive.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
conventions of the example rows and nothing else. No pipeline, no coverage table,
no independent test pass, no human gate.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation. The deposit
(`IPA2017.txt`) labels itself "version in force from 1/7/2025"; the latest
amendment annotated is Act 3 of 2021 wef 1 July 2025 (s 28(3)), and Act 25 of 2021
wef 1 April 2022 is annotated at s 77.

**Checks:** one case file, 57 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Why this Act, and why scoped

**7 of the 527 Singapore Acts** deposited here cite it. This row takes what a
member of the public, a building owner or a developer meets: entry to protected
places and directions (ss 20-23), defensive measures (s 25), the specified
surrounding area (ss 26, 27, 31), photography including by drone (ss 29, 30),
specified works and major renovation (s 2), the security plan regime for special
developments (ss 35, 36(3), 39), appeals (s 62), and the penalty, composition and
bail rules (ss 74, 75). The making of protected area and place orders, special
infrastructures designated under s 41, the content of security directives and
Minister's orders, the Appeal Advisory Board, enforcement powers and Part 9 are
not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Deadly force after three warnings, and no compensation behind warning signs

s 23: where a person attempts to enter or is in a protected area or place and
"does not stop despite being directed to do so at least 3 times", the force used
to arrest "may, if necessary to effect that person's arrest, extend to the
voluntary causing of that person's death". s 25: the Minister may authorise
defensive measures endangering life; if precautions such as prominent warning
notices are taken, no one is entitled to compensation for injury or death from
unauthorised entry. Asserted.

### 2. "Photography" includes sketching, mapping and measuring, from anywhere

s 29(1) covers photographs taken "from inside, outside or above", and s 29(5)
extends "photograph" to film, live-streaming, and "a drawing, diagram, plan,
model, map, measurement, sounding or survey". For drones (s 30(6)) the
prosecution need not prove the accused knew the premises was protected; the only
defence is to prove, on a balance of probabilities, that the shot was
unintentional because of weather or other unavoidable cause. Asserted.

### 3. Entry without a permit is an offence only at a protected place

s 20 bars entry to a protected **place** without a permit or an on-duty officer's
permission. There is no equivalent bar for a protected **area**; there, the
offence is disobeying a direction under s 21. The s 22 removal and arrest power
follows the same split, but also arises wherever the officer thinks there is no
good and lawful reason or the person is acting suspiciously. Asserted.

### 4. Refusing an officer is not an offence if the officer will not show authority

s 31(2): for requests and directions under ss 27-30, it is "not an offence for
any person to refuse to comply" if the officer, on demand, does not produce
evidence of identity and authority. Asserted for s 27.

### 5. Most of the street-level offences are non-bailable; the Minister's order carries 5 years

s 75(1) makes every offence arrestable; s 75(2) makes non-bailable the offences
under ss 20(2), 21(6), 27(5), 28(2), 29(4), 30(4) and (5), 37(7), 44(7), 56(3)
and (4) and 60(3) — trespass and photography included, while works without an
approved security plan ($200,000) stays bailable. Breach of a Minister's order
(s 60(3)) is the only offence read with a 5-year maximum; the rest read carry
2 years or a fine only. Asserted.

### 6. The composition cap is always $5,000 for the offences read

s 74 caps composition at the lower of half the maximum fine or $5,000. The
smallest maximum fine read is $10,000 (ss 39(4), 77(6)), so half is never below
$5,000 and the cap is $5,000 throughout. Which offences are compoundable is left to
regulations not deposited. Asserted.

### 7. Missing the certificate before a TOP has no penalty in s 39

s 39(2) requires the certificate of works completion (a) before any temporary
occupation permit where one is required, and (b) otherwise within the prescribed
time. s 39(4) penalises only (2)(b). The encoding returns no s 39(4) offence where
a TOP is required; whether another provision catches it was not checked. Asserted.

### 8. Appeals: 14 days, final, and the decision is suspended meanwhile

s 62 allows appeal against rejection of a security plan or amended plan, or a
Commissioner's security directive, within 14 days. Unless the Minister directs
otherwise, the decision does not take effect until the appeal is determined or
withdrawn, and the Minister's decision is final. No appeal is listed against a
Minister's order under s 58 or a provisional approval with conditions. Asserted.
