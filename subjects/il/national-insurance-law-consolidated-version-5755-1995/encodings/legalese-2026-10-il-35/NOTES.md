# NOTES: row IL-35, the consumer price index readings and the rise of the index (ITO s 1 / s 120B, NII s 334(a)(1))

Status: **draft**. Version **0.1.0** (2026-10-08), run id `IL-35-20261008`, agent `enc-il-35`.
No domain expert has read this encoding against the source.
This row is data plus the rules that choose which readings a Law takes.
The data (`cpi-il35-published-figures.l4`) is the Central Bureau of Statistics' series and is not law; the module says so on its face.

## 1. What is encoded

**The readings.**
`cpi-il35-published-figures.l4` carries the 320 monthly readings of the CBS general consumer price index (code 120010) from January 2000 to August 2026, each in the base in force when published, with the Bureau's two percentages for the reading (the month, the twelve months).
It is generated by `tools/gen_published_figures.py` from the two deposited JSON files and `check.sh` regenerates it and diffs.
The thirteen bases of the series (1998, 2000, 2002, 2006, 2008, 2010, 2012, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024) are carried with the Bureau's twelve step coefficients, which the linkage file prints as `prevBase.coeff`.

**What the Laws say about the index** (from the deposited texts):

- ITO s 1 "מדד" (source line 179): the CBS consumer price index.
  NII s 1 "מדד" (NII line 171): the same, "published monthly".
- ITO s 1 "שיעור עליית המדד" (line 180): for a period, the difference between the index published last before the END of the period and the index published last before the START of the period, divided by the latter.
  No rounding is stated.
- ITO s 120B(a) (line 4338): on 1 January of each tax year, adjust by the rate of rise of the index "in the previous tax year".
  s 120B(b) (line 4339): the allowance point, from a month named, by the rise "from the start of the tax year until the end of the said month".
  s 120B(d) (line 4341): rounding by an Order of the Minister (not deposited).
  s 120B(e) (lines 4343-4345): freeze for 2025-2027; on 1 January 2028 the 1 January 2024 amounts before rounding are adjusted, "the index in the previous tax year" being "the index of tax year 2027".
- ITO s 1 "שנת מס" (line 202): twelve consecutive months beginning 1 January, or a special assessment period.
- NII s 334(a) "מדרגת גבייה מופחתת" (lines 3605-3606): 7,522 shekels (2025; "בשנת 2026, 7,703 ש״ח" is a consolidation note), updated each 1 January for 2026 to 2028 "by the rate of rise of the index published last before 1 January, against the index published last before 1 January of the previous year"; from 2029 by the average wage.

**What the rules do.**
Neither Law has an "index for a year": a year's index is a pair of readings.
So the rules give:

- `the reading the Bureau published for` a month: "the index for month M" (the reading of that month), refused by name outside January 2000 to August 2026.
- `the reading last published before the start of` / `before the end of` a month: the reading of month M-2 / M-1 (assumption A1).
- `the rate of rise of the index in` a period of whole months (a tax year, or `the period of twelve months beginning with` a month), and `the rate of rise of the index from` one reading `to` another.
- `s 120B(a) — the rate of rise of the index for the adjustment on 1 January of tax year` Y (the rise in tax year Y-1), `s 120B(b) — the rate of rise of the index in tax year` T `from its start to the end of month` X, `s 120B(e)(2) — …` and `s 334(a)(1) — the rate of rise of the index for 1 January of` Y.
- `the readings on one scale for` a period (and the s 120B(a) and s 334(a)(1) forms): the two readings as numbers on one scale, the shape IL-03's `Index readings for a period` and IL-04's two figures take.
  The field names are IL-03's, so a caller hands the two values across.

**What it does not do.**
It adjusts no amount, applies no freeze (s 120B(e)(1) is IL-03's), rounds nothing, and decides nothing about a fall: the signed rise is returned (a fall is negative) and IL-03 F4 and IL-04 F6, the earlier rows' switches, decide.
A fall happens: tax year 2020 is -1/168 (-0.6%), tax year 2006 is -1/344, the single month of January 2026 against November 2025 is -3/1036.

## 3. Assumptions (assumed, not read)

**A1. A month's reading is published during the next month.**
The Laws speak of "the index published last before" a day, and the files carry no publication date.
SOURCES.json says of the latest reading "August 2026 (published in September 2026)", which is the only deposited statement about publication.
The encoding uses: a reading is published after the first day and before the last day of the following month (in practice on the 15th; that last part is from general knowledge, not from any deposited source).
Then the reading last published before the start of month M is that of M-2, and the one before the end of month M is that of M-1; for a tax year, November of the year before and November of the same year.
The constant is `the number of months from the month a reading is of to the month it is published in` (= 1).
If a month's publication slips past the 1st or the end of its following month, the rules are wrong for that month.
This does not need a Meng switch because it is a fact about the world; it needs a source (the Bureau's release calendar) to be closed.

**A2. A tax year is the calendar year.**
ITO s 1 allows a special assessment period; none is encoded.

**A3. The Bureau's published figures are the readings the Laws mean.**
The readings are the Bureau's, in the base in force when published, not restated; where a reading is later re-based the series here still carries it in the old base, and the Bureau's coefficient is the bridge.
Evidence that this is how the Bureau itself compares them: for all 308 months that have the same month a year earlier in the series (144 of them across a change of base), the rise computed by linking (restating both readings in the older base with the file's own values) and rounded half-up to one decimal place equals the Bureau's `percentYear` for that month in every case (`tools/spotcheck_tests.py`, which `check.sh` runs).

## 4. Fork register

Both forks: **ruled by Meng 2026-10-08 (SHRUG)**: one named switch, default DECLINE (a refusal by name), the other readings kept by name and tested.

### F1. How readings of two bases are compared (the s 1 arithmetic across a change of base)

**The text.**
ITO s 1 (line 180) divides a difference of two published readings by the earlier.
It says nothing of a change of base between the two.
The Bureau re-bases every two years or so (the base changes between December and January in 2001, 2003, 2007, 2009, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025), so a tax-year rise (November to the previous November) crosses a base change in the year after each of those Januaries, and NII s 334(a)(1) for 2026 does (November 2025 is base 2024, November 2024 is base 2022).

**The readings** (nouns: `A reading of how readings of two bases are compared`):

1. `readings of two bases are compared by linking them through the Bureau's coefficients`: restate both in the older base with the Bureau's step coefficients, then s 1's arithmetic.
   For November 2025 against November 2024: 103.6 x 1.074 = 111.2664; (111.2664 - 108.7) / 108.7 = 3208 / 135875 = 2.361%.
2. `a rise over twelve months is the percent the Bureau published for the twelve months`: for a span of exactly twelve months between the same calendar months, the Bureau's rounded percentage: 2.4% = 3/125.
   Not defined for any other span, which refuses by name.
3. `a rise across a change of base is declined` (the default): answered only where readings 1 and 2 give the same rise, otherwise refused with "the text does not say how readings of two bases are compared, and the two ways open … give different answers here".

**Within one base** there is one reading and it answers (the Law's own arithmetic on the Bureau's figures as printed), under every setting of the switch.
**Where readings 1 and 2 agree** the default answers: tested with synthetic readings (not the Bureau's) that make them equal.
They did not agree at any real cross-base pair in the tests.

**Consequence for the default.**
At the default, the rise is declined for every tax year whose November-to-November span crosses a base change (the answer table below), which is 11 of the 25 adjustment years 2002 to 2026 (including the adjustments on 1 January 2026, 2024 and 2022) and NII s 334(a)(1) for 2026.
This is the lead's SHRUG policy working as stated, not an encoding defect, and it is the finding that matters for the capstone (section 8).

**Evidence that moves the odds but does not decide.**
NII s 334(a) prints 7,522 and the note "בשנת 2026, 7,703" (line 3605).
7,522 x (1 + 3/125) = 7,702.528, which rounds to 7,703; 7,522 x (1 + 3208/135875) = 7,699.59, which rounds to 7,700.
So the figure printed beside the Law is what reading 2 (the Bureau's percentage) gives, not what reading 1 gives.
The Institute's method is not in the sources, the text states no rounding, and the note is an aid, so the default stays declined; `cpi-il35-tests.l4` section J records the arithmetic as evidence only.
Against reading 2: it exists only for a span of exactly twelve months, which s 120B(b) (start of the year to the end of a month) and s 120B(e)(2) (three years) are not; and it is a rounded figure the Law does not mention.
For reading 1: it is the Bureau's own method and reproduces the Bureau's percentage to the published precision in all 308 pairs.

### F2. s 120B(e)(2): which rise "the index of tax year 2027" means

**The text** (line 4345): on 1 January 2028 the amounts as they were on 1 January 2024, before rounding, are adjusted under (a) and (b), "and for this purpose the index in the previous tax year to be taken into account for their adjustment shall be the index of tax year 2027".

**The readings** (nouns: `A reading of the rise s 120B(e)(2) takes on 1 January 2028`):

1. `the rise of the index in tax year 2027 alone`: the plain (a), the period 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2027.
2. `the rise of the index from the start of tax year 2024 to the end of tax year 2027`: the amounts are those of 1 January 2024, so the rise of 2024 to 2027 is the one to catch up.
3. `the period of the rise on 1 January 2028 is declined` (the default).

The two give different periods, so the default declines.
Neither rise can be computed from the sources: November 2026 and November 2027 are later than the series (refused by name).
The periods are tested.

### Recorded, not gating (not switches)

- **A fall of the index.** The signed rise is returned; what a fall does is IL-03 F4 and IL-04 F6.
- **Publication timing** is assumption A1, a fact, not a reading of the text.
- **The end of a period is the end of its last month.** s 120B(b) says "until the end of the said month"; the reading published last before that moment is the previous month's (A1), by the text's own definition. A reading that "until the end of the month" excludes the month's own publication is not open: the definition says "before the end of the period".
- **Tax year before the section existed.** s 120B(a)'s rule is applied to any year in the series without a vintage gate: the deposited text is s 120B as amended at retrieval and IL-03 gates the vintage (its A1). This row returns a rise, not an adjustment.
- **The Bureau's own percentage for the month** (`percent`) is carried and not used by any rule.

## 8. For the capstone (IL-55), and what the earlier rows would need

- **Inputs the capstone does not supply today.**
  IL-03 takes `Index readings for a period` and IL-04 takes two readings or a rate, as bare numbers.
  This row's `s 120B(a) — the readings for the adjustment on 1 January of tax year` Y and `s 334(a)(1) — the readings for 1 January of` Y return a record with IL-03's two field names, on one scale.
  Nothing calls them yet; wiring is IL-55's.
  This module's imports resolve beside it, so the capstone would vendor `cpi-il35-nouns.l4`, `cpi-il35-published-figures.l4`, `cpi-il35-index-rules.l4` and the two rule modules beside whatever imports them.
- **The decision that matters.**
  At the SHRUG default the supplier declines for every year whose span crosses a base change (11 of the 25 adjustment years 2002 to 2026, including NII s 334(a)(1) for 2026 and ITO s 120B(a) for 2024 and 2026).
  IL-04 already carries the published 7,703 as an input, so it is unaffected; but wiring this row in at the default would make those years decline.
  Whoever wires it must either pass reading 1 or 2 by name, or leave the earlier rows' inputs as they are.
  The evidence in F1 favours reading 2 for NII 2026 only; I did not change the default.
- **The series ends in August 2026.**
  Rises for 1 January 2027 on (NII) and tax year 2026 on (ITO) refuse by name until a series reaching November of the year is deposited; the September 2026 reading is published in October 2026.
- **Not assumed from the earlier rows:** no module of IL-03 or IL-04 was edited, vendored or imported; only their field names were copied.
